Oscillatory integrals appear throughout physics, from diffraction and gravitational lensing to real-time path integrals. Their asymptotic evaluation is complicated by the need to determine which real and complex saddle points contribute, and how they are connected by Stokes phenomena. In multiple dimensions, this traditionally requires computing steepest descent manifolds in a high-dimensional complex space. Here, we develop an algebraic alternative for integrals with polynomial and rational exponents. Using integration by parts and Brieskorn reduction, we construct a finite-dimensional Gauss-Manin system that generates the asymptotic expansion of all saddle points. Its Borel transform yields a Fuchsian system, whose mondromy determines the Stokes constants and hence the adjacency of the saddle points. Combined with the recent South-East rule, this identifies the contributing saddles and enables superasymptotic and hyperasymptotic approximations without computing gradient flows. We demonstate the method for the parabolic umbilic diffraction integral and a Lorentzian lens as examples, obtaining agreement to machine precision. The methods are implemented in an accompanying open-source Julia package Resurgence.jl

We describe spectral networks for cubic differentials on $\mathbf{P}^1$ with exactly three poles all of order two, following observations of Hollands-Neitzke. To do so, we give a precise definition and establish some basic properties of a class of finite webs (corresponding to BPS states in physics) in the cubic differential setting, including their non-vanishing central charge. Turning to the example, we prove that at rational angles the network is totally degenerate; the number of walls and Stokes regions is explicitly computed and depends on an arithmetic criterion. At all other angles, the spectral network is dense in the surface and contains no finite webs, as expected. Physically, this example corresponds to a fundamental building block for a large class of four-dimensional $\mathcal{N}=2$ QFTs with gauge group $\mathrm{SU}(3)$.

We give an explicit construction of a pair of anticommuting Weyl displacement operators for a harmonic oscillator and use bounded functional calculus to obtain exact dichotomic representatives of the Pauli algebra. Two independent oscillators provide commuting Pauli algebras for Alice and Bob. The standard singlet-sector projector, written entirely in terms of these bounded oscillator observables, is then applied to the two-oscillator vacuum. We show explicitly that the projected vacuum has nonzero norm, so its normalization is well defined, has the exact singlet correlations, and reaches the Tsirelson value $2\sqrt2$ for the usual Bell--CHSH settings. We also clarify that the construction generally realizes a singlet sector with multiplicity, rather than selecting a unique vector in the full oscillator Hilbert space, and we compare the construction with the older pseudospin approach to continuous-variable Bell tests.

We develop a systematic construction of renormalization group (RG) domain walls between two-dimensional rational conformal field theories. Using phantom currents, we consider an auxiliary chiral extension linked to the folded ultraviolet--infrared theory by a topological interface. The construction of RG walls then reduces to identifying suitable automorphism-twisted Cardy states in the auxiliary theory. We find two such types of automorphisms: outer automorphisms exchanging identical current algebra factors, as in Gaiotto's construction, and continuous inner automorphisms generated by the zero modes of spin-one phantom currents. In the latter case, the RG domain walls generically admit a continuous defect conformal manifold. We further show that, when an RG flow can be orbifolded to another one, the orbifold may lift defect conformal moduli of the RG brane constructed using inner automorphisms. The two types of RG branes can then be mapped to each other via a topological interface defined by the orbifold. We demonstrate the two types of RG brane construction via various examples of flows between non-unitary minimal models, from parafermions to minimal models, and between generalized parafermions. Many physical observables of the RG walls are computed non-perturbatively, including boundary entropies and spectra, and UV--IR operator mixings. We also find interesting nontrivial boundary monodromy: a closed cycle in the defect conformal moduli space can carry an RG domain wall into other distinct non-trivial conformal interfaces.

Our detectors for Dark Matter and Coherent Elastic Neutrino--Nucleus Scattering (CE$ν$NS) use photolithographically patterned superconducting athermal phonon sensors that are coupled to kg-scale absorbers of different materials (germanium, silicon, sapphire). Following a particle interaction, athermal phonons from the bulk absorber break Cooper pairs in a superconducting aluminum film that surrounds a tungsten Transition Edge Sensor (TES) in equilibrium through electrothermal feedback. This sensor design is called Quasiparticle-trap-assisted Electro-thermal-feedback Transition-edge-sensor (QET). The QET aluminum film that absorbs the athermal phonons is patterned into fins with lengths limited by the quasiparticle diffusion length in aluminum. Improving the quasiparticle diffusion length in the superconducting aluminum is an area of vigorous research to improve phonon collection efficiency and increase the signal strength for lower-threshold searches. We report significantly improved aluminum films that show systematically larger grain sizes and residual-resistivity ratio (RRR) compared to detectors we have fabricated in the past. The improved RRR and grain sizes were expected to improve the collected signal for a given energy. We report higher signal strength achieved in a detector in comparison to another detector in which all other fabrication parameters were kept identical except for the aluminum film deposition.

Magnetic null points define the magnetic field topology in stellar coronae, planetary magnetospheres, and astrospheres, and are commonly associated with magnetic reconnection. We investigate the local structure of stationary three-dimensional (3D) resistive, incompressible magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) flows in the vicinity of an isolated magnetic null and an isolated velocity stagnation point, assuming that the two nulls are colocated. The governing MHD equations are solved self-consistently using a first-order local expansion of the magnetic and velocity fields about the null. For any given magnetic-null configuration, the equations admit a two-parameter family of stationary solutions, comprising multiple solution branches with distinct velocity topologies. Along these families, the velocity-null polarity can change at bifurcation points where the flow becomes effectively 2.5D. A single magnetic null can therefore support positive-radial, negative-radial, and spiral stagnation flows, with qualitatively different inflow, outflow, and rotational properties. The associated pressure and resistivity distributions are likewise determined self-consistently and vary across the solution family, demonstrating that they are coupled to the flow topology rather than prescribed independently. The generic solutions do not require the velocity and magnetic separatrices to coincide, allowing plasma to cross the magnetic fan separatrix and providing a local reconnective flow. These results demonstrate that, in the presence of nonuniform resistivity, the magnetic topology alone does not uniquely determine the associated stationary flow, pressure, or resistivity structure around a 3D magnetic null, revealing the nonlinear feedback between all macroscopic variables and resistivity. The constant-resistivity field-aligned and spiral field-crossing solutions identified previously are recovered as special cases.

We present the design methodology, implementation, and data products of the Energetic Particle Detector (EPD) instrument on-board the Electron Losses and Fields Investigation (ELFIN) dual SmallSat mission. ELFIN was designed to study the time-evolution of energetic particle distributions in Earth's magnetosphere, having operated in polar Low Earth Orbit (LEO) from 2018 to 2022. Each satellite's instrument payload contained a pair of detector heads to sense differential-directional fluxes of 50 keV to $\sim$5 MeV electrons (EPDE), and 50 keV to $\sim$2 MeV atomic ions (EPDI), with an energy resolution better than 50% ($ΔE/E<0.5$). The satellites were spinning with a nominal period of 2.8 s in a plane containing Earth's background magnetic field, with particle counting intervals subdivided into 16 consecutive spin sectors. Each EPD was paired with a three-axis Fluxgate Magnetometer (FGM) sampled at 80 Hz for local pitch-angle determination. Each detector head operated on the principle of pulse-height analysis (PHA) in solid state media, collimated by a rectangular 22.5$^\circ$ field of view and entrance aperture. The EPDE contained a stack of 6 silicon detectors (0.7 cm total thickness) with 0.15 cm$^2$ str geometric factor, with ion contamination mitigated by an aperture foil and digital coincidence logic. The EPDI contained a stack of 2 silicon detectors (0.1 cm total thickness) with 0.03 cm$^2$ str geometric factor, with electron contamination mitigated by a magnetic broom stage and digital anti-coincidence logic.
