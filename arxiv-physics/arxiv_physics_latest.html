Deep learning (DL) models are increasingly being deployed into radiation-challenging environments in spacecraft, particle accelerators, and, soon, space-based data centers. Instantiated as a learned orchestration of numbers stored in persistent memory, models are susceptible to continual degradation as high-energy particles corrupt data. At inference time, new data passed through the network fundamentally break the off-line assumptions and guarantees of a static model determined during training. To preserve the quality and efficacy of the models, fault tolerance against bit flips is a clear objective. In this work we developed a runtime method for repairing the quantized weights of neural networks (NNs) after bit flips on commodity, commercial off-the-shelf (COTS) hardware, without storing extra replicas. Our work extends the well-established Fault-Aware Training (FAT) paradigm by adding an alignment penalty to concentrate undamaged activations and weights into a low-rank subspace, and we then reconstruct flagged corrupted coordinates from the uncorrupted survivors. We call this method {\it Projection Healing} and apply it to six regression benchmarks on quantized NNs. Holding a FAT checkpoint fixed and toggling only the runtime heal gives a further ${\sim}23\%$ reduction in the native-unit excess integral. Across those datasets the method extends the operational scrub interval by a median of $2.04\times$ at a $+5\%$ RMSE degradation and $1.96\times$ at $+50\%$, compared with $1.62\times$ and $1.07\times$ for classical Single Error Correction, Double Error Detection (SECDED). This introduces a new path towards designing resilient data-driven models, complementing established hardware redundancy, ECC, and FAT-based techniques.

The tree-level open superstring has an integer mass-squared spectrum on linear Regge trajectories. To what extent is this fixed by consistency conditions: locality, analyticity, crossing, unitarity together with the requirement that $2\to2$ scattering minimize the entanglement it generates? Using the local two-channel crossing symmetric dispersion relation with one-parameter ambiguity ($λ$-CSDR) we set up a bootstrap treating both the mass-squared spectrum and the partial-wave coefficients as unknowns which lets the machine learning algorithm to learn the mass-squared spectrum and partial-wave coefficients simultaneously, going beyond earlier work in which the spectrum was fixed. Using the local three-channel crossing symmetric dispersion relation with one-parameter ambiguity we obtain, within the class of amplitudes with soft Regge behavior and infinite higher-spin tower, a bound on the leading Wilson coefficients that we compare with existing bounds and trace to spin-0 dominance. We locate the Virasoro-Shapiro amplitude in the allowed region of the Wilson coefficients space. For amplitudes with two-channel crossing symmetry we find that a Non-dual amplitude exhibits a Polchinski-Strassler type transition from Regge to power-law behavior.

Magnetic null points define the magnetic field topology in stellar coronae, planetary magnetospheres, and astrospheres, and are commonly associated with magnetic reconnection. We investigate the local structure of stationary three-dimensional (3D) resistive, incompressible magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) flows in the vicinity of an isolated magnetic null and an isolated velocity stagnation point, assuming that the two nulls are colocated. The governing MHD equations are solved self-consistently using a first-order local expansion of the magnetic and velocity fields about the null. For any given magnetic-null configuration, the equations admit a two-parameter family of stationary solutions, comprising multiple solution branches with distinct velocity topologies. Along these families, the velocity-null polarity can change at bifurcation points where the flow becomes effectively 2.5D. A single magnetic null can therefore support positive-radial, negative-radial, and spiral stagnation flows, with qualitatively different inflow, outflow, and rotational properties. The associated pressure and resistivity distributions are likewise determined self-consistently and vary across the solution family, demonstrating that they are coupled to the flow topology rather than prescribed independently. The generic solutions do not require the velocity and magnetic separatrices to coincide, allowing plasma to cross the magnetic fan separatrix and providing a local reconnective flow. These results demonstrate that, in the presence of nonuniform resistivity, the magnetic topology alone does not uniquely determine the associated stationary flow, pressure, or resistivity structure around a 3D magnetic null, revealing the nonlinear feedback between all macroscopic variables and resistivity. The constant-resistivity field-aligned and spiral field-crossing solutions identified previously are recovered as special cases.

We present the design methodology, implementation, and data products of the Energetic Particle Detector (EPD) instrument on-board the Electron Losses and Fields Investigation (ELFIN) dual SmallSat mission. ELFIN was designed to study the time-evolution of energetic particle distributions in Earth's magnetosphere, having operated in polar Low Earth Orbit (LEO) from 2018 to 2022. Each satellite's instrument payload contained a pair of detector heads to sense differential-directional fluxes of 50 keV to $\sim$5 MeV electrons (EPDE), and 50 keV to $\sim$2 MeV atomic ions (EPDI), with an energy resolution better than 50% ($ΔE/E<0.5$). The satellites were spinning with a nominal period of 2.8 s in a plane containing Earth's background magnetic field, with particle counting intervals subdivided into 16 consecutive spin sectors. Each EPD was paired with a three-axis Fluxgate Magnetometer (FGM) sampled at 80 Hz for local pitch-angle determination. Each detector head operated on the principle of pulse-height analysis (PHA) in solid state media, collimated by a rectangular 22.5$^\circ$ field of view and entrance aperture. The EPDE contained a stack of 6 silicon detectors (0.7 cm total thickness) with 0.15 cm$^2$ str geometric factor, with ion contamination mitigated by an aperture foil and digital coincidence logic. The EPDI contained a stack of 2 silicon detectors (0.1 cm total thickness) with 0.03 cm$^2$ str geometric factor, with electron contamination mitigated by a magnetic broom stage and digital anti-coincidence logic. The total assembled mass, volume, and power required for the EPD detector heads and circuit boards were 939 g, 10$\times$9.8$\times$8.4 cm$^3$, and 2.3 W, respectively. We describe each EPD design element alongside instrument data products and processing.

Collisionless shocks confine the particles they accelerate with the upstream wave field they themselves drive. Streaming particles resonantly amplify Alfvén waves, and the waves then confine the same particles by pitch-angle scattering. The rigidity a shock precursor can hold is therefore capped by how fast those waves grow, and that coupling has not previously been constrained in-situ at any interplanetary shock. Here we report Parker Solar Probe measurements of a fast, near-parallel coronal mass ejection-driven shock at 0.24~AU on 2023 March 13 that constrain this wave-particle coupling through the energetic-particle population it accelerates. We measure the spectral folding energy, the boundary between the confined and the upstream-escaping protons, and find that it falls from $\sim$6 to $\sim$1.5~MeV as $E_\mathrm{fold}\proptoΔt^{\,α}$ with $α\simeq0.34$, where $Δt$ is the time remaining before shock arrival. One precursor convective time before arrival this boundary is at $E_\mathrm{c}\simeq 2.46$~MeV, a characteristic confinement energy of a precursor of scale $L_\mathrm{EP}$. Read through the quasi-linear marginal-stability condition, the same evolution implies a resonant wave-growth timescale $τ_\mathrm{growth}(E)\propto E^{1/α}\simeq E^{2.92}$ that exceeds the precursor convective time above $E_\mathrm{c}$. The post-shock pitch-angle anisotropy reverses sign across the same confinement--escape transition at 2--5~MeV. The rigidity to which a shock precursor can confine particles is therefore set, in-situ, by how quickly the streaming population builds its own confining waves.

Black holes are usually described either as classical geometries on which quantum fields propagate or as objects whose complete quantum description lies beyond semiclassical gravity. Hawking radiation already shows, however, that a black hole is not an exactly isolated system: the horizon carries a thermal scale and, after coarse graining, may be associated with fluctuations, dissipation and environmental correlations. Whether this horizon-scale openness can be encoded directly in the gravitational background, before any stochastic treatment of the metric, remains an open question. Here we answer it by introducing, for the first time, a conserved Hawking-scale anisotropic source representing the coarse-grained near-horizon atmosphere and solving the Einstein equations for a static black hole at fixed ADM mass. The dressed geometry has a renormalized mass function, a self-consistently shifted horizon and a modified surface gravity. Because the same lapse governs the horizon and timelike geodesics, the thermal response is accompanied by a correlated orbital restructuring: the effective potential is renormalized, the ISCO moves inward and the near-horizon residence time increases. The construction adds no Langevin force to a fixed Schwarzschild background; rather, it defines a deterministic dressed mean geometry, providing a controlled background on which stochastic metric fluctuations may subsequently be treated in the Einstein-Langevin framework. This establishes the deterministic mean-field sector as the necessary first step for future stochastic gravity programs addressing horizon-scale openness.

Collisionless shocks accelerate particles at a rate set by the diffusion coefficient of those particles in waves they themselves generate. Theory fixes that coefficient only in steady state, and no measurement has tested whether an evolving shock ever reaches that state. We report Parker Solar Probe observations of a fast, near-parallel interplanetary shock at 0.24 au, encountered four hours after eruption, with the upstream waves and the protons they scatter recorded on the same magnetic field line. From the wave spectra alone we derive the parallel mean free path of 0.05-30 MeV protons, resolved in energy and in distance from the shock. The mirror force of the measured compressive fluctuations supplies the scattering through the pitch angles that gyroresonance cannot reach. The mean free path grows with distance nearly as the steady self-generated solution requires, yet the steady balance holds at one energy only, near 1.3 MeV. The intensity profiles are a factor of two shallower than the measured coefficient predicts, because the waves are still growing. Confinement fails between 2.3 and 6 MeV, beginning a factor of two below what the age of the shock allows. The growth of the waves its own particles drive, rather than its age or its size, limits the energy an evolving shock confines.

We analyse the AstroSat Soft X-ray Telescope (SXT) observation of the high-synchrotron-peaked blazar 1ES 1959+650 taken on 2023 September 2, which covers 362 ks with about 95 ks of good exposure. We build 0.6-7 keV light curves in six sub-bands, binned at 5830 s (the orbital period) and 1000 s. All sub-bands show excess variance, with fractional variability of 3.7 to 6.5 per cent that increases with energy ($\propto E^{0.32\pm0.07}$). The power spectrum of the total band, fitted with a model that includes the window function of the orbital gaps, is a red-noise power law with slope $-1.80$ (95 per cent range $-2.8$ to $-1.4$). A Lomb-Scargle search finds no significant periodicity (global $p>0.57$) and would have detected, with 90 per cent probability, a sinusoid with an rms amplitude above 1.9 to 3.7 per cent at periods of 3 to 50 ks. The soft (0.6-1.4 keV) and hard (1.9-7 keV) light curves are correlated near zero lag, but only marginally (global $p=0.04$ and 0.07 for power-spectrum slopes of $-1.7$ and $-2.0$), and the lag is $-1^{+6}_{-10}$ ks, consistent with zero. No pair of sub-bands that includes 0.6-0.8 keV shows a significant correlation or lag, so an energy-dependent lag of a few ks can be neither confirmed nor excluded. The 0.6-7 keV light curve contains one hump with rise and decay times of about 0.4 to 0.6 d, which is only marginally distinguishable from red noise ($p\approx0.05$ to $0.07$). The count rate distribution is consistent with a lognormal distribution, and the hardness ratio does not depend significantly on flux.

We present an analysis of a long NuSTAR observation of the intermediate supergiant fast X-ray transient (SFXT) IGR J00370+6122, obtained around the peak of its orbital modulation. The observation has a net exposure ~93 ks. During the observation, the source displayed a bright flaring event that was resolved into numerous short flares with durations of 10-1000 s. The 3-70 keV luminosity spanned a dynamic range of ~200, from ~1E34 to ~1.8E36 erg/s; the maximum was reached for only ~40 s in total. The spectrum is well described by an absorbed double-Comptonisation model, with parameters consistent with those found in other X-ray pulsars accreting at similarly low luminosities. This is the first detection of a double-hump continuum in IGR J00370+6122, which extends this spectral state to the class of intermediate SFXTs. We searched for the previously reported ~346 s and ~674 s periodicities and find no significant signal at either period, which makes their interpretation as the neutron star spin period less likely. Using ~21 yr of Swift/BAT monitoring, we refine the orbital period of the system to P_orb=15.6654 +/- 0.0008 d. Finally, we discuss the observed flaring activity in the framework of quasi-spherical settling accretion, which is expected at the observed luminosities.

Context: Superbursts are energetic explosions in the crusts of neutron stars in Low-Mass X-ray Binaries (LMXBs). At temperatures above 4 GK, photodisintegration of heavy rp-process ashes may play a major role alongside $^{12}$C fusion. Aims: We investigate how photodisintegration of rp-process ashes affects superburst properties and the initial $^{12}$C abundance required for ignition. Methods: We simulate superbursts with MESA v.24.08.1. Besides the canonical fuel of $20\%$$^{12}$C and $80\%$$^{56}$Fe, we test different $^{12}$C abundances mixed with scaled rp-process ashes. Results: Superbursts can ignite with only $10\%$$^{12}$C when the remaining $90\%$ consists of rp-process ashes. Their luminosity is comparable to that of the canonical fuel, whereas $10\%$$^{12}$C mixed with $90\%$$^{56}$Fe yields lower luminosity. $^{56}$Fe remains the most abundant ash nuclide. Although photodisintegration drives nucleosynthesis, neutron, proton and alpha captures release energy at late times. We also find that $A=64$ nuclides or $^{20}$Ne help reduce the $^{12}$C abundance needed for ignition. Conclusions: The composition of accreted material - not just its $^{12}$C content - substantially affects superburst ignition, luminosity and nucleosynthesis.

Ultraviolet (UV) observations of Type Ia supernovae (SNe Ia) exhibit significant diversity, but their physical origin remains poorly understood. We investigate UV spectra of SNe Ia observed with the Swift UltraViolet and Optical Telescope. Our expanded pre-maximum sample probes dependencies on decline rate, ejecta properties, and host environment. We recover the correlation between UV flux and decline rate near maximum in the near-UV (NUV), and find correlations at pre-maximum phases in both the NUV and mid-UV (MUV). This trend is strongly wavelength dependent, with slow decliners showing higher NUV but lower MUV flux than fast decliners. Synthetic TARDIS spectra varying the 56Ni abundance show that enhanced 56Ni suppresses the MUV while increasing the NUV, broadly reproducing the trend. We find no significant correlation between UV flux and ejecta velocity, while Si II 6355 pEW shows trends similar to those with decline rate. We also find no significant correlations with host-galaxy properties, although host environment may contribute to the remaining UV diversity among SNe with similar decline rates. Early-excess SNe show enhanced flux mainly in the NUV rather than a broad UV excess, suggesting that strong circumstellar interaction is not necessarily required to explain the observed excess emission. Peculiar subclasses show distinct UV properties, with brighter and hotter subclasses showing higher UV flux, although the current sample is small and does not cover all SN Ia subtypes. Our results suggest that the UV diversity of SNe Ia is shaped by multiple mechanisms that contribute differently across the UV wavelength range.

Tidal disruption events (TDEs) provide a unique laboratory for studying transient accretion, outflows, particle acceleration, and the possible connection between stellar disruptions and high-energy neutrinos. Motivated by recent candidate TDE-neutrino associations and by the forthcoming sensitivity of multimessenger facilities, we investigate under which physical conditions TDEs can be detected in MeV, GeV, and TeV gamma rays, and in high-energy neutrinos. We develop a lepto-hadronic emission framework and apply it to three representative sources: AT2019dsg, AT2019ehz, and ASASSN-14li. For each source we explore a set of physically motivated models that vary the proton and electron spectral indices, the minimum Lorentz factor of non-thermal protons, the particle energy partition, the acceleration efficiency, the magnetic compactness, and the presence of a compact corona. We compute the broadband spectral energy distributions, the associated muon-neutrino spectra, and compare them with reference sensitivities for COSI, Fermi/LAT, CTAO, and KM3NeT/ARCA. We find that hard proton spectra and/or large minimum proton Lorentz factors are the most favourable conditions for TeV gamma-ray and neutrino detectability, while GeV-bright models can be predominantly leptonic and neutrino quiet. AT2019dsg and AT2019ehz can reach the CTAO and KM3NeT benchmarks in hadronically favourable configurations, whereas ASASSN-14li is more naturally bright in the MeV-GeV band and constrained by its radio synchrotron emission. Our results highlight the importance of combining MeV, GeV, TeV, radio, and neutrino observations to distinguish between transparent, hidden, and lepton-dominated TDE emission regimes.
