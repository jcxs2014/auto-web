We revisit the classical spectral problem for the Laplace-Beltrami and Laplace-Bochner operators on complex Grassmannians. After reviewing the standard approach based on non-commutative harmonic analysis, we develop two complementary methods originating from the quantization of the corresponding one-dimensional sigma model, both with and without a magnetic monopole field. The first is based on $\mathbf{SU}(n)$-equivariant first-order GLSM formulation and gives explicit eigenfunctions in terms of projectors and Plücker coordinates. The second uses a spin-chain realization of free particles on manifolds and describes the space of square-integrable functions, or sections in the magnetic case, through limiting tensor products of long rectangular $\mathbf{SU}(n)$ representations. Together, these approaches provide a more explicit picture of the spectral geometry of Grassmannians and clarify the relation between harmonics, Young diagrams, magnetic bundles, and Laplacian spectra.

The memory-burden effect was recently proposed to take into account the information back-reaction in the evaporation of primordial black holes (PBHs). In this work, we systematically explore the axion-like particle (ALP) production via the evaporation of memory-burdened PBHs and the subsequent ALP-photon conversion in cosmic magnetic fields. The PBH evaporation progress is divided in the semiclassical phase of Hawking radiation and the quantum memory-burden phase. The time evolution of PBH mass and the gamma-ray flux after the conversion of PBH-emitted ALP are then evaluated by considering the memory-burden induced PBH entropy suppression. Furthermore, we investigate the detection prospects of this evaporation-conversion signal in next-generation satellite telescopes, including AMEGO, e-ASTROGAM and MAST. The detectable PBH mass range is extended down to masses much smaller than $10^{15}$ g. Applying the Fisher forecasting method, we derive the projected sensitivity for PBH fraction $f_{\rm PBH}$ and ALP-photon coupling $g_{aγγ}$.

We address this by placing \(J/ψ\) and \(ϕ\) in one soft-wall model on the planar limit of a five-dimensional equal-spin Myers--Perry (MP) AdS black hole, computing spectral functions and spin alignment within the same current-correlator framework; the nonrotating results of Sheng et al. For \(ϕ\), rotation enhances the negative deviation, more along \(z\) than along \(x\). Fragmentation of relativistic neon nuclei was studied with the Abrasion-Ablation Monte Carlo for Colliders model (AAMCC-MST), which incorporates pre-equilibrium clustering of spectator matter via a Minimum Spanning Tree (MST) algorithm, subsequent decays of the excited clusters, and Coulomb repulsion between final fragments. After validating the model against data on $^{20}$Ne and $^{22}$Ne fragmentation on various targets, the production of spectator nuclear fragments, neutrons, and protons in $^{20}$Ne-$^{20}$Ne collisions at the LHC was modeled at $\sqrt{s_{\rm NN}}=5.36$ TeV. The production cross sections, transverse and longitudinal momentum distributions for $^2$H, $^4$He, $^6$Li, $^{10}$B, $^{12}$C, $^{14}$N, and $^{16}$O fragments were calculated as these fragments may contaminate $^{20}$Ne beams circulating in the LHC. A simple parametrization describing relative width of the longitudinal momentum distributions calculated for these fragment species was constructed. To study the sensitivity of results to $α$-clustering and nuclear density distributions in neon nuclei, two density profiles for $^{20}$Ne and $^{22}$Ne were used: a deformed Woods-Saxon, and a five-$α$ bi-pyramidal configuration. In addition, a spherical profile was considered for $^{20}$Ne. Comparing results obtained with these profiles and with or without MST clustering shows that the calculated yields of spectator nucleons and fragments, their momentum distributions are sensitive to the details of nuclear structure of $^{20,22}$Ne and fragmentation modeling. The results provide the input for simulations of the transport of secondary forward nuclei in the LHC to estimate their interaction rates with circulating $^{20}$Ne beam nuclei, and also for calculations of responses of neutron and proton Zero Degree Calorimeters to $^{20}$Ne-$^{20}$Ne events.

The standard definition of entanglement entropy in quantum field theory requires regularizing the entangling surface and factorizing the Hilbert space, which in turn involves a choice of boundary conditions at the regularized surface. This raises two natural questions which are open at present. The first is under what conditions there is a choice of boundary conditions that leads to a unitary map between the original Hilbert space and the factorized one, which is a necessary condition for a well-defined entanglement entropy. The second concerns the situation where there are multiple such choices, in which case the question is whether they all yield the same entropy, or at least the same terms usually referred to as universal. Focusing on topological theories and using three-dimensional Chern-Simons theory as a concrete illustration, we show under specified assumptions that different such choices affect only the non-universal terms in the entropy, such that the remaining terms are universal in this sense as well, while we explicitly demonstrate that boundary conditions corresponding to non-unitary maps can change these terms. We then explain how a similar argument applies to two-dimensional conformal field theories and discuss a condition for universality in general theories.

A proposed resolution of the black-hole information paradox must explain which information an outside observer can recover from escaping radiation\cite{Hawking,Page,Hayden,Almheiri}. Environmental memory can return correlations to the source without delivering them to that observer\cite{ChenQi,ChenMemory}. Here we establish a sharp recovery frontier at fixed mean radiation yield for single-excitation channels and realize its qubit limit with an autonomous quantum-memory emitter. Unequal transmissions balance retained coherence against information in failed collection events. A reference--radiation correlation test distinguishes this gain even when the mean collected energy is unchanged. Identical transmission channels instead give the same recovery at matched collection, regardless of their emission timescales. For a broader class of interacting sources, an ordering of collected and inaccessible coupling spectra forbids unassisted quantum transmission irrespective of internal memory. A separate coding example supports a positive rate where uniform transmission at matched actual yield has zero capacity. Together, these results identify what openness must change to assist recovery: the accessible transmission structure. They supply a physically constrained comparison of evaporation mechanisms, separating information delivered to radiation from coherence retained elsewhere or an increase in the collected output.

TianQin, a space-borne gravitational wave observatory, is designed to detect gravitational-wave signals via laser interferometry. As the inhomogeneous space plasma surrounds the inter-satellite laser links, the propagation of the laser in space plasma can introduce pointing error noise. In this work, we quantitatively assess the effect of plasma on the pointing accuracy of TQ's inter-satellite laser links. We employ a magnetohydrodynamic model to simulate the plasma distribution around the TQ orbit for different orbital orientations. Using the fourth-order Runge-Kutta method to conduct the ray-tracing calculations over a TQ orbital cycle under a solar wind dynamic pressure close to the 23-year average, we find that the plasma-induced laser deflection angle and pointing eccentricity are on the order of $10^{-20} \ \mathrm{rad}$ and picometers, respectively. These values are significantly lower than TQ's requirements. Our results demonstrate that the impact of space plasma on TQ's pointing error is negligible in moderate space weather conditions, indicating a positive outlook for the TQ mission.

We use the core-radius, $r_γ,$ of a galaxy's central core along with a volume-limited sample of brightest cluster galaxies (BCG) to determine the $z=0$ mass distribution function of the most massive super-massive black holes (SMBH). We recover a $M_{BH}-r_γ$ relationship between the SMBH mass, $M_{BH},$ and $r_γ$ of form $M_{BH} \approx1.3\times10^{10}~(r_γ/{\mathrm{kpc}})~M_\odot,$ with $σ= 0.25\pm0.03$ dex scatter in $M_{BH}.$ We derive a core distribution function (CDF), based on HST and DLS images of the BCGs.
