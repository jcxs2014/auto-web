We compute the entropic order parameter introduced in (1905.10487) using the replica trick for two disjoint intervals. For a global symmetry, this quantity measures the mutual information carried by charge-anticharge intertwiners. We formulate the problem in terms of averages over twisted partition functions on replica Riemann surfaces, and obtain exact solutions for free fermions and compact bosons, for $U(1)$ symmetry and its discrete $\mathbb Z_N$ subgroups. We show that further averaging over both families of non-contractible cycles gives access to an additional quantity, which is the loss of Holevo information associated with complementary-charge measurements that occurs upon removing the intertwiners. For the boson, this quantity vanishes, whereas for Dirac fermions it equals the difference between the mutual information of the fermion-parity-gauged and ungauged theories, independently of the choice of even $\mathbb Z_N$ subgroup. Finally, we extend the analysis to non-Abelian symmetries using saddle expansions, deriving universal behavior in the nearly touching intervals limit and for a large number of fermions $N_F\gg1$.

We show that certain geometric constructions, including the Fefferman-Graham ambient metric, Brinkmann spaces, and ultrastatic vacuum spacetimes, admit natural conformal compactifications and can be unified within a single framework arising naturally from conformal geometry. The framework is based on a two-scale system comprising two weight-one conformal scalars satisfying almost-Einstein equations and certain regularity conditions. As a particular instance of this unifying framework, we obtain a new ambient construction for Spi and Ti, the extended spatial and temporal infinities of Ricci-flat spacetimes. A remarkable feature of this construction is that Spi/Ti and the null infinity of the corresponding spacetimes are naturally realised as a boundary face and the corner of the two-scale ambient space, respectively. We also demonstrate how the two-scale system can serve as a background ambient geometry for studying the asymptotic behaviour of (gauge) fields at spatial, temporal, and null infinity.

We use the projected spin-1 Duffin--Kemmer--Petiau (DKP) equation in $2+1$ dimensions to clarify the physical content of the irreducible $3\times3$ representations. The projected antisymmetric tensor is algebraically dual to the projected vector, and the two twin representations select conjugate self-dual covariant-curl sectors, which reduce to opposite massive helicities in the flat-space limit. We then apply this structure to the static wormhole problem previously studied with a single irreducible representation. The first-order radial DKP system contains an exceptional normalizable bound-state branch at $ω^2=M^2$ that is absent from the master-variable reduction, whereas the generic master and circular sectors are related by a Darboux transformation. The latter preserves the transmission and reflection probabilities, while the quasinormal modes are organized into two pole families in the complex radial-momentum plane, leading to the physical pairing $(ω,-ω^*)$ rather than a global $\pmω$ prescription.

In this paper, we have generalized Poisson's(1993) calculation for the case of gravitational radiation from a particle in a circular orbit around a black hole to the case of the wormhole. Considering the motion of a test particle around a Schwarzschild like wormhole and using Teukolsky formalism, we have obtained an analytical result of flux at infinity under the assumption of small velocities. We have further corroborated our results with the numerical investigations, which show that the two waveforms match well when the particle lies far from ISCO. Thus, our work provides one of the first attempts toward the study of the post-Newtonian waveforms involving wormholes (ECOs).

We investigate quantum gate teleportation as a primitive for satellite-enabled distributed quantum computing. In the considered architecture, a satellite distributes entangled photon pairs to two optical ground stations, which transfer the shared quantum resource to remote quantum processing units. This entanglement is then used to implement a nonlocal controlled-NOT gate between two circuit qubits located in different processors. We formulate the minimal four-qubit protocol, consisting of two network qubits that provide the entangled resource and two circuit qubits on which the distributed gate is performed. Atmospheric propagation effects in the gravitational field are modeled through an effective two-qubit depolarizing channel whose parameters depend on atmospheric and optical quantities, including the Fried parameter and receiver aperture. The performance of the protocol is quantified by the fidelity between the ideal CNOT output state and the state produced by noisy quantum gate teleportation. Our results show that the fidelity is governed by the quality of the distributed entanglement and depends jointly on atmospheric coherence, aperture size, and effective depolarization strength. These findings connect physical-layer modeling of satellite optical channels with gate-level performance in distributed quantum computing.

We develop a gauge-independent theory of magnetic reconnection beyond the standard special-relativistic framework of inertial systems in flat spacetime. Our definitions of reconnection and its rate are independent of reference frame and coordinate system, allowing physical effects of gravity and net linear acceleration to be distinguished from coordinate effects. We extend the mesoscale theory of Liu et al. [PRL 118, 085101 (2017)], in which the reconnection rate depends on physics both within the diffusion region and in an upstream ideal, force-free region. The theory identifies three non-inertial and curved-spacetime (NICS) effects, including gravitationally induced magnetic tension: gravitational distortion of the reconnection plane changes the curvature of magnetic field lines. Applying the theory to a simplified accretion plasma near )a compact object, we find that (1) whether gravity increases or decreases the reconnection rate depends on the system's orientation relative to the gravitational source, with the greatest increase occurring when the outflow jet points toward or away from the source; and (2) the magnitude of the gravitational effect scales with the dimensionless parameter $\barκ \equiv r_sΔy^2/r^3$, where $r$ is the Schwarzschild radial coordinate, $r_s$ is the source's Schwarzschild radius, and $Δy$ is the mesoscale length.

Energetic-particle irradiation can produce persistent charge separation within lunar regolith, but the internal fields predicted by subsurface-charging models depend on how particle interactions are translated into retained charge. This critical review examines the evidence and assumptions connecting microscopic charge formation to macroscopic charging. We distinguish energy deposition, net charge transfer across a specified boundary, and the spatial distribution of signed excess charge. We organize these distinctions using an average retained-charge response defined for specified material, irradiation, electrical-state, and evaluation conditions. Its use as a volumetric source requires consistent charge accounting, compatible incident-flux definitions, and an adequate separation between response formation and subsequent transport. Applying these criteria to existing models, emission studies, and silicate irradiation experiments yields three assessments. First, emission measurements constrain electron loss across the measured boundary but do not locate internally redistributed charge. Second, evidence of internal reabsorption does not by itself establish that the charge separation sustaining subsurface fields is reduced; an illustrative calculation shows that the same net charge supply can produce different peak fields depending on transfer depths. Third, laboratory electrical damage in silicate crystals does not determine breakdown rates in cryogenic, porous regolith, which additionally require constraints on local fields, failure properties, and discharge recurrence. Joint constraints on boundary charge transfer, spatial charge separation, and post-irradiation relaxation are central to evaluating lunar subsurface-charging predictions.

A proposed resolution of the black-hole information paradox must explain which information an outside observer can recover from escaping radiation\cite{Hawking,Page,Hayden,Almheiri}. Environmental memory can return correlations to the source without delivering them to that observer\cite{ChenQi,ChenMemory}. Here we establish a sharp recovery frontier at fixed mean radiation yield for single-excitation channels and realize its qubit limit with an autonomous quantum-memory emitter. Unequal transmissions balance retained coherence against information in failed collection events. A reference--radiation correlation test distinguishes this gain even when the mean collected energy is unchanged. Identical transmission channels instead give the same recovery at matched collection, regardless of their emission timescales. For a broader class of interacting sources, an ordering of collected and inaccessible coupling spectra forbids unassisted quantum transmission irrespective of internal memory. A separate coding example supports a positive rate where uniform transmission at matched actual yield has zero capacity. Together, these results identify what openness must change to assist recovery: the accessible transmission structure. They supply a physically constrained comparison of evaporation mechanisms, separating information delivered to radiation from coherence retained elsewhere or an increase in the collected output.

TianQin, a space-borne gravitational wave observatory, is designed to detect gravitational-wave signals via laser interferometry. As the inhomogeneous space plasma surrounds the inter-satellite laser links, the propagation of the laser in space plasma can introduce pointing error noise. In this work, we quantitatively assess the effect of plasma on the pointing accuracy of TQ's inter-satellite laser links. We employ a magnetohydrodynamic model to simulate the plasma distribution around the TQ orbit for different orbital orientations. Using the fourth-order Runge-Kutta method to conduct the ray-tracing calculations over a TQ orbital cycle under a solar wind dynamic pressure close to the 23-year average, we find that the plasma-induced laser deflection angle and pointing eccentricity are on the order of $10^{-20} \ \mathrm{rad}$ and picometers, respectively. These values are significantly lower than TQ's requirements. Our results demonstrate that the impact of space plasma on TQ's pointing error is negligible in moderate space weather conditions, indicating a positive outlook for the TQ mission.

We present the high-cadence light curve and rapid spectroscopy of the Type II SN 2024phv, discovered just 16.8 hours after the estimated explosion time. The early light curve exhibits a rapid 2 mag rise over the first day of observation, and spectra at 1.7 and 2.6 days reveal weak, narrow emission lines, suggesting moderate interaction with circumstellar material during the first few days after explosion. Models of shock-cooling emission provide a reasonable fit to the light curve after day 2, implying minimal interaction at later epochs, and yield a best-fit progenitor radius of $R=390\pm90\,R_{\odot}$. We also compare our earliest spectra with radiation hydrodynamical models of exploding red supergiants to measure circumstellar densities of ${\sim}10^{-12}$ g cm$^{-3}$ within a few stellar radii of the progenitor, followed by a sharp dropoff in the density profile. This rapidly decreasing profile leads us to conclude that the progenitor of SN 2024phv had an extended atmosphere and/or closely confined circumstellar material, likely from rapidly increasing or eruptive mass loss prior to explosion. In the context of other supernovae discovered at similarly early phases, SN 2024phv points to a diversity in the mass-loss rates of red supergiant stars in their final years.

Large-scale core-collapse supernova simulations rely on tabulated nuclear equations of state (EOSs), whose memory requirements can become a practical constraint in massively parallel calculations. We explore a compact alternative by replacing the tabulated thermodynamic mapping with a neural-network surrogate, using the Lattimer--Swesty EOS as a representative reference model. The surrogate directly predicts pressure, sound speed, and effective adiabatic index from $(\log ρ, \log e, Y_e)$ and accurately reconstructs the nonlinear thermodynamic structure of the EOS, including its behavior across phase-transition regions. It reproduces one-dimensional shock-tube solutions with hydrodynamic errors at the $\sim 10^{-3}$ level under different thermodynamic conditions and yields hydrostatic equilibrium configurations closely matching those obtained with the reference EOS. We find that smaller regression errors do not necessarily yield better hydrodynamic performance, demonstrating the importance of hydrodynamic validation in surrogate model selection. A compact network with only $\sim 10^4$ parameters retains this accuracy while reducing the memory required to store the EOS from $\sim 160$ MB to $\sim 100$ KB, corresponding to a reduction by a factor of $\sim 1500$. The surrogate also remains stable through collapse, bounce, shock formation, and post-bounce evolution in a one-dimensional adiabatic core-collapse calculation. These results demonstrate the potential of compact neural EOS surrogates for reducing memory requirements in large-scale core-collapse supernova simulations.

Sparse plasma immersed in a strong magnetic field is common in astrophysical scenarios, for which a collisionless description is more appropriate. The guiding-center approximation serves as an effective tool for describing the motion of charged particles in a strong magnetic field, revealing the drift, a sub-leading order motion that causes particles to deviate from the magnetic field lines. It is widely accepted that the charged particles drift under the induction of gravity in Newtonian framework. However, a covariant formulation of the guiding-center approximation, as well as the drift of a charged particle induced by strong gravitational field, has yet to be fully explored. As a preliminary study, we aim to analyze the drift of a single charged particle in a black hole magnetosphere via a covariant approach. Particularly, we focus on the effects of black hole spin. We first examine the drift caused by the rotation of a massive object in the Newtonian regime using the language of gravitoelectromagnetism. A covariant formula of drift is derived, which is confirmed to reduce to the Newtonian results in the weak gravitational field and low speed limit. The impact of BH spin on the drift is analyzed in detail in Kerr spacetime specifically, for which we define the drift rate whose value quantifies the significance of the drift. Our results indicate that the impact of BH spin on the drift of a charged particle in a BH magnetosphere can be comparable to that of other factors in some extreme cases.

We present an X-ray analysis of XMM-Newton and NuSTAR spectra of the Narrow-Line Seyfert 1 (NLS1) galaxy Mrk 110. Our analysis reveals the intriguing presence of a multi-component ultra fast outflow (UFO) both in the grating spectrum and the NuSTAR data. The moderate UFO significance (3-4$σ$), in the grating and CCD data indicates a shallow absorber, consistently with the absence of previous such claims in this source. The two components (UFO1 and UFO2) in the grating spectrum show a fairly wide range of velocities (0.05-0.06c), as their respective ionization state (Log U = -1.43 and -1.82), however they share the same column density (Log N$_H$$\sim$ 19.7). In the NuSTAR spectrum, we found a much faster wind component with a velocity of $\sim$ 0.21c (UFO3). We show that, in an energy-driven wind scenario, UFO3 might carry enough energy to produce significant feedback on its host galaxy. Considering its known radio properties, Mrk 110 is therefore another Narrow-Line Seyfert 1 with stratified ultra-fast outflows co-existent with a pc-scale jet/outflow radio structure.
