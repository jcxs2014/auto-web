We derive new transformation formulas for elliptic hypergeometric sum/integrals associated with the orthogonal groups $\mathrm{SO}(N)$. The sum/integrals are written in terms of the lens elliptic gamma function, which depends on an integer $r\geq 1$ in addition to the usual complex variables, and they reduce to the elliptic hypergeometric integrals of Spiridonov and Vartanov for $r=1$. The derivation uses the $BC_n\leftrightarrow BC_{\tilde n}$ transformation formula proven in our previous paper, together with a duplication formula for the lens elliptic gamma function proven here. The duplication formula takes a different form depending on whether $r$ is even or odd. Physically, the new transformations show that the supersymmetric lens indices on $S^1\times S^3/\mathbb{Z}_r$ of the 4d $\mathcal{N}=1$$\mathrm{SO}(N)$ and $\mathrm{SO}(N_f-N+4)$ gauge theories related by Seiberg duality are equal for odd $r$. For even $r$, where the lens index depends on the global form of the gauge group, they show the equality of the contributions from flat connections whose holonomies lie in a common maximal torus. The $BC_n$ formula itself gives the corresponding statement for the symplectic groups.

We give a geometric formulation of the $q$DE/ODE/IM correspondence for families of differential operators associated with simple Lie algebras of ADE type. Under analytic hypotheses, the $q$-connection associated with shifted monodromy defines a twisted $(G,q)$-oper and has Steinberg form in the frame of solutions normalized by prescribed asymptotics in a sector at infinity. We identify the central connection between the normalized solution frames at zero and infinity with a universal meromorphic $(G,q)$-Wronskian. Its generalized minors give $Q$-functions satisfying the ADE $QQ$-relations. When $q$ is not a root of unity, we parametrize shifted monodromy by quantum-affine $q$-characters evaluated on shifted $Q$-ratios, using the same character formulas that describe normalized transfer-matrix eigenvalues. We verify the analytic hypotheses for generic untwisted Feigin--Frenkel--Hernandez affine opers, thereby relating these affine opers to twisted $(G,q)$-opers.

We consider a QCD-inspired chiral $SU(4)$ model, with a new fundamental sector ($q_{\rm HC}$) charged under the confining hypercolor gauge group $SU(3)_{\rm HC}$ as a template for electroweak dynamics and compositeness. The unification of the four fundamental interactions remains one of the central open challenges in fundamental physics. Most existing unification scenarios rely on extra spatial dimensions, novel symmetry structures, or unobserved dynamical degrees of freedom. In this work, we construct a minimal phenomenological dual-moment framework rooted in primitive charges and motion-induced kinetic moments. Gravitational, electromagnetic, and strong interactions are coherently described within a unified inverse-square-law formalism. We postulate a universal coupling relation $T = Z/c^2$ that connects source-charge coupling coefficients and moment-mediated coupling coefficients across different interaction sectors. While the framework can formally accommodate weak-charge degrees of freedom, full quantitative computations for weak-interaction dynamics are deferred to future follow-up studies. The model reproduces established low-energy dynamical limits for planetary orbits, atomic systems, and heavy-quark hadrons, and predicts distinguishable corrections to light deflection by rapidly spinning compact objects. We further design three independent test schemes: improved torsion-balance measurements, a mass-current laboratory deflection experiment, and astronomical light-bending observations. Although laboratory measurements impose formidable technical challenges, astronomical observations of compact spinning objects offer promising avenues to constrain or falsify the proposed framework.

Energetic-particle irradiation can produce persistent charge separation within lunar regolith, but the internal fields predicted by subsurface-charging models depend on how particle interactions are translated into retained charge. This critical review examines the evidence and assumptions connecting microscopic charge formation to macroscopic charging. We distinguish energy deposition, net charge transfer across a specified boundary, and the spatial distribution of signed excess charge. We organize these distinctions using an average retained-charge response defined for specified material, irradiation, electrical-state, and evaluation conditions. Its use as a volumetric source requires consistent charge accounting, compatible incident-flux definitions, and an adequate separation between response formation and subsequent transport. Applying these criteria to existing models, emission studies, and silicate irradiation experiments yields three assessments. First, emission measurements constrain electron loss across the measured boundary but do not locate internally redistributed charge. Second, evidence of internal reabsorption does not by itself establish that the charge separation sustaining subsurface fields is reduced; an illustrative calculation shows that the same net charge supply can produce different peak fields depending on transfer depths. Third, laboratory electrical damage in silicate crystals does not determine breakdown rates in cryogenic, porous regolith, which additionally require constraints on local fields, failure properties, and discharge recurrence. Joint constraints on boundary charge transfer, spatial charge separation, and post-irradiation relaxation are central to evaluating lunar subsurface-charging predictions.

A proposed resolution of the black-hole information paradox must explain which information an outside observer can recover from escaping radiation\cite{Hawking,Page,Hayden,Almheiri}. Environmental memory can return correlations to the source without delivering them to that observer\cite{ChenQi,ChenMemory}. Here we establish a sharp recovery frontier at fixed mean radiation yield for single-excitation channels and realize its qubit limit with an autonomous quantum-memory emitter. Unequal transmissions balance retained coherence against information in failed collection events. A reference--radiation correlation test distinguishes this gain even when the mean collected energy is unchanged. Identical transmission channels instead give the same recovery at matched collection, regardless of their emission timescales. For a broader class of interacting sources, an ordering of collected and inaccessible coupling spectra forbids unassisted quantum transmission irrespective of internal memory. A separate coding example supports a positive rate where uniform transmission at matched actual yield has zero capacity. Together, these results identify what openness must change to assist recovery: the accessible transmission structure. They supply a physically constrained comparison of evaporation mechanisms, separating information delivered to radiation from coherence retained elsewhere or an increase in the collected output.

TianQin, a space-borne gravitational wave observatory, is designed to detect gravitational-wave signals via laser interferometry. As the inhomogeneous space plasma surrounds the inter-satellite laser links, the propagation of the laser in space plasma can introduce pointing error noise. In this work, we quantitatively assess the effect of plasma on the pointing accuracy of TQ's inter-satellite laser links. We employ a magnetohydrodynamic model to simulate the plasma distribution around the TQ orbit for different orbital orientations. Using the fourth-order Runge-Kutta method to conduct the ray-tracing calculations over a TQ orbital cycle under a solar wind dynamic pressure close to the 23-year average, we find that the plasma-induced laser deflection angle and pointing eccentricity are on the order of $10^{-20} \ \mathrm{rad}$ and picometers, respectively. These values are significantly lower than TQ's requirements. Our results demonstrate that the impact of space plasma on TQ's pointing error is negligible in moderate space weather conditions, indicating a positive outlook for the TQ mission.

We use the core-radius, $r_γ,$ of a galaxy's central core along with a volume-limited sample of brightest cluster galaxies (BCG) to determine the $z=0$ mass distribution function of the most massive super-massive black holes (SMBH). We recover a $M_{BH}-r_γ$ relationship between the SMBH mass, $M_{BH},$ and $r_γ$ of form $M_{BH} \approx1.3\times10^{10}~(r_γ/{\mathrm{kpc}})~M_\odot,$ with $σ= 0.25\pm0.03$ dex scatter in $M_{BH}.$ We derive a core distribution function (CDF), based on HST and DLS images of the BCGs. The CDF falls steeply as $N(r_γ)\sim r_γ^{-3}$ for $r_γ>0.50$ kpc. Only $\sim5\%$ of BCGs have $r_γ>1$ kpc. Combining the CDF and $M_{BH} - r_γ$ relationship yields the BH mass distribution function (MDF). For $M_{BH} \geq 7\times10^{9}~\mathrm{M_\odot}$ (the mean $M_{BH}$ at $r_γ=0.50$ kpc) we derive: $\log\left(\frac{dN(M_{BH})}{d{M_{BH}/10^{10} M_\odot}}~[\rm Mpc^{-3}]\right)=(-3.05\pm0.42)~\log\left(\frac{M_{BH}}{10^{10}M_\odot}\right)~-~5.11\ (+0.41, -0.36)$. Cores with $r_γ\geq 0.50$ kpc are found almost exclusively in BCGs, implying that SMBHs with $M_{BH} \geq7\times10^{9}~\mathrm{M_\odot}$ may also be exclusive to BCGs. Core-radius correlates with $α,$ a parameter that characterizes the growth of BCG envelopes by mergers, suggesting that the most massive SMBHs grew in tandem with their BCG hosts. The BH MDF embodies a significant population of SMBH with $M_{BH} >7\times10^{9} M_\odot.$ When combined with the Liepold & Ma (2024) BH MDF to account for SMBH with $M_{BH} < 7\times10^{9} M_\odot,$ it can comfortably explain the nHz gravitational wave background.

Magnetic null points define the topology of magnetic fields in stellar coronae, planetary magnetospheres, and astrospheres and are commonly associated with magnetic reconnection. We investigate the local structure of stationary 3D resistive, incompressible magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) flows in the vicinity of an isolated magnetic null and an isolated velocity stagnation point, assuming that the two nulls are colocated. The governing MHD equations are solved self-consistently using a first-order local expansion of the magnetic and velocity fields about the null. For any given magnetic-null configuration, the equations admit a two-parameter family of stationary solutions, comprising multiple solution branches with distinct velocity topologies. Along these families, the velocity-null polarity can change at bifurcation points where the flow becomes effectively 2.5D. A single magnetic null can therefore support positive-radial, negative-radial, and spiral stagnation flows, with qualitatively different inflow, outflow, and rotational properties. The associated pressure and resistivity distributions are likewise determined self-consistently and vary across the solution family, demonstrating that they are coupled to the flow topology rather than prescribed independently. The generic solutions do not require the velocity and magnetic separatrices to coincide, allowing plasma to cross the magnetic fan separatrix and providing a local reconnective flow. These results demonstrate that, in the presence of nonuniform resistivity, the magnetic topology alone does not uniquely determine the associated stationary flow, pressure, or resistivity structure around a 3D magnetic null, revealing the nonlinear feedback between all macroscopic variables and resistivity. The constant-resistivity field-aligned and spiral field-crossing solutions identified previously are recovered as special cases.

We present results from deep XMM-Newton observations ($\sim$180 ksec) of the galaxy group IC 2476 in the X-GAP sample, capturing a rare AGN feedback regime, where the central radio AGN has ceased activity but the radio lobes are still detectable. The hot intragroup medium of IC 2476 features a compact cool core, with a size of ~10 kpc, and a moderate X-ray luminosity of $\sim3.4\times10^{40}$ erg/s. We measured an unusually low temperature of $\sim$0.45 keV within the cool core. We measured an exceptionally low abundance of below ~0.2 solar, even in the central region. The X-ray surface brightness of the group is not symmetric and shows a clear depression in the region overlaid with the eastern radio lobe, suggesting the presence of an X-ray cavity. The scaled entropy of the hot gas within 0.1 r500 of IC 2476 is among the highest in X-ray galaxy groups with a similar system temperature. Our study suggests that the past powerful radio AGN outburst in IC 2476 has disrupted the cool core and the X-ray cavity has dissipated or mixed with the surrounding hot gas since the shutdown of the central AGN. A large cool core may be gradually re-established in the absence of AGN activity. This system therefore provides a great example of AGN feedback destroying the group cool core, even with a moderate radio luminosity at present.

High-energy cosmic rays produce atmospheric muons through cascades of hadronic interactions, yet simulations often underestimate the muon content of extensive air showers. Whether the same problem affects energetic atmospheric muons, which probe a different part of the cascade, is less clear. Here we compare published atmospheric-muon measurements from four experiments with reference predictions based on a shared choice of hadronic interaction model and primary cosmic-ray flux. We find that the measurements predominantly exceed the central predictions across different experimental techniques, from surface measurements to deep detectors. This recurring excess is more consistent across the measurements than any inferred dependence on energy or zenith angle. These atmospheric muons preferentially originate from energetic mesons produced early in the cascade, whereas the total muon content of extensive air showers also reflects interactions at later stages. A common explanation of both excesses must therefore increase the effective supply of energetic muon parents, rather than merely increasing the multiplicity of soft particles. This provides a way to distinguish between competing explanations of the cosmic-ray muon puzzle.

Active learning is becoming standard practice in undergraduate STEM instruction in North America and is slowly being adopted in graduate courses. Graduate summer schools, one- to two-week programs in which students from many institutions gather for intensive, specialized instruction in their field, are common in physics; they are taught by researchers chosen for their expertise, and their pedagogy remains almost entirely traditional lecturing. We report a study at a one-week graduate school in neutrino and astroparticle physics attended by 38 students from 8 countries. Six lecturers with limited or no prior experience of teaching with active learning introduced Peer Instruction-style active learning into their lectures after approximately four hours of preparation led by a physics-education specialist. Each of the three main lecture streams was taught traditionally on one day and in the active format on the other. Students were surveyed after each of the first two days and at the end of the week; all six instructors were surveyed as well. In every active lecture, spanning three instructors and three topics, 82-100% of respondents reported that the interactive segments helped them stay engaged and check their understanding, and in every stream the difficulty was rated as better matched on the active day. At the end of the week, 33 of 36 wanted such teaching in future graduate courses, and not one respondent disagreed. All six instructors judged the approach productive and intend to use it again; only two found implementation straightforward. With brief expert-led preparation, lecturers new to active learning implemented it successfully for a heterogeneous audience, and both students and instructors want more. The school was also the first time any of the six instructors implemented structured active learning: a summer school can develop its lecturers as well as its students.

Optical synchrotron polarization is one of the most direct probes of the magnetic-field structure and particle-acceleration processes in the relativistic jets of blazars. Although long-term optical flux variability is often attributed to multiplicative stochastic processes because of its log-normal behavior, but the physical origin of optical polarization variability remains less understood. In this study, we investigate the long-term optical flux and polarization variability of a sample of blazars using nearly a decade of photo-polarimetric observations from the Steward Observatory. We find that, like the optical flux, the optical degree of polarization is generally well described by a log-normal distribution and shows an approximately linear relationship between its variability amplitude and mean value. We interpret this behavior within a magneto-hydrodynamic (MHD) framework in which the magnetic-field undergoes repeated stochastic changes driven by shocks, turbulence, velocity shear, and large-scale jet dynamics. The cumulative effect of these processes can naturally generate multiplicative variability, leaving similar statistical signatures in both the synchrotron flux and the degree of polarization. Our results provide new observational evidence that the long-term statistical behavior of optical flux and polarization may arise from a common set of MHD processes associated with magnetic-field evolution.
