We construct charge-two Schrödinger conformal blocks in one spatial dimension, independently of any dynamical realization. We consider ordered vacuum four-point functions of fundamental fields of dimension $1/2$, with homogeneous Schrödinger equations at the earliest and latest insertions and interactions allowed in the intermediate two-particle interval. For reduced blocks analytic in signed spatial coordinates and even under simultaneous spatial inversion, two functions of the time cross-ratio determine the full spatial dependence: the collinear value and a rescaled mixed spatial derivative. An exact Kummer lift, built from confluent hypergeometric functions, gives this reconstruction. An operator identity reduces the quartic Casimir equation, modulo the endpoint equations, to an equivalent coupled system for this pair. At generic exchanged dimension, OPE conditions select a unique block up to normalization for each primary parity. We obtain collinear ${}_4F_3$ functions and sums of Kummer products, including the shadow branches. The blocks depend on the relative sign of the signed coordinates, distinguishing piecewise linear worldlines with an inflection from those with monotone segment velocities. In the Calogero model, we recover the previously derived bosonic four-point function and obtain its fermionic counterpart, with an independent integral check. At the diffusion-limited fixed point of $A+B\to\emptyset$, two blocks of opposite primary parity give the same collinear unlike-species response. Nonnegativity for all positions and ordered times selects the even family, thereby establishing that the response summed over species contains the even shadow pair with dimensions $Δ=1,2$. The nonnegativity argument demonstrates a concrete constraint for a Schrödinger bootstrap beyond collinear data.

We study the low energy behaviour of B-type D-branes in non-Abelian gauged linear sigma models. The main problem is to determine the grade restriction rule for D-brane transport from one phase to another along paths. We analyze the convergence condition of the integral for the hemisphere partition function. We perform a thorough analysis in a particular model with two phases, one weakly coupled and one strongly coupled, both corresponding to Calabi-Yau threefolds. General methods are developed in this study and are applied to other models successfully. The integrals for a set of special Chan-Paton representations exhibit critical behaviour around each singular point on the phase boundary. From each of these points emanate ``no entry zones'' where a convergent contour is lost or is forced to traverse Gamma function poles. Knowledge of the special representations and no entry zones is strong enough to uniquely determine the grade restriction rule. In the course of this study, we encounter a problem in Weyl invariance of convergent contours, which forces us to modify the grade restriction criterion. The special representations play an important rôle in the monodromy around the singular point. We also obtain exact results on boundary conditions in supersymmetric QCDs and apply them to understand the low energy behaviour of D-branes in the strong coupling phase.

We study the electromagnetic and gravitational form factors of the charged kaon in a light-front Hamiltonian model with coupled $|u\bar s\rangle$ and $|u\bar s g\rangle$ Fock sectors. We solve the coupled Hamiltonian in a longitudinal Jacobi and transverse harmonic-oscillator basis, with the interaction strength fixed by the kaon mass. The momentum form factors are extracted from $T^{++}$, while the stress form factors are obtained from the transverse shear projection of the QCD Belinfante energy-momentum tensor, including diagonal, sector-changing, and constrained-field contributions. The strange antiquark carries the largest fraction of the kaon's longitudinal momentum, whereas the gluon carries only $2.05\%$. Despite this small momentum fraction, the gluon contributes $42.4\%$ of $|D_K(0)|$, for which we obtain $D_K(0)=-2.03610$. The charge and transverse momentum radii are $0.5178\pm0.0278\,\mathrm{fm}$ and $0.3394\pm0.0113\,\mathrm{fm}$, respectively, with uncertainties estimated from mass and basis variations. We obtain the transverse mechanical distributions using two ultraviolet continuations of the directly calculated $D$ form factors beyond $Q^2=2\,\mathrm{GeV}^2$. Both continuations give positive pressure at small transverse distances and negative pressure at larger distances. The corresponding transverse mechanical radii are $0.4616\pm0.0130\,\mathrm{fm}$ and $0.4768\pm0.0132\,\mathrm{fm}$, both larger than the transverse momentum radius. The central pressure changes substantially between the two continuations, whereas the pressure zero and mechanical radii vary much less.

我们研究了耦合 $|u\bar s\rangle$ 和 $|u\bar s g\rangle$ Fock 扇区的光锋哈密顿模型中带电 kaon 的电磁和引力形状因子。我们在纵向雅可比和横向谐振子基础上求解耦合哈密顿量，相互作用强度由 kaon 质量固定。动量形状因子是从 $T^{++}$ 中提取的，而应力形状因子是从 QCD Belinfante 能量动量张量的横向剪切投影中获得的，包括对角线、扇区变化和约束场贡献。奇怪的反夸克携带了 kaon 纵向动量的最大部分，而胶子只携带了 $2.05\%$。尽管动量分数很小，但胶子贡献了 $|D_K(0)|$ 的 $42.4\%$，为此我们得到 $D_K(0)=-2.03610$。电荷半径和横向动量半径分别为 $0.5178\pm0.0278\,\mathrm{fm}$ 和 $0.3394\pm0.0113\,\mathrm{fm}$，不确定性根据质量和基础变化估计。我们使用直接计算的 $D$ 形状因子的两个紫外线延续来获得超出 $Q^2=2\,\mathrm{GeV}^2$ 的横向力学分布。两种延续都在较小的横向距离处产生正压，在较大的距离处产生负压。相应的横向机械半径为 $0.4616\pm0.0130\,\mathrm{fm}$ 和 $0.4768\pm0.0132\,\mathrm{fm}$，均大于横向动量半径。中心压力在两个延续之间变化很大，而压力零和机械半径变化要小得多。

We study the unpolarized generalized parton distributions (GPDs) of the nucleon in a covariant quark-diquark approach, in which both scalar and axial-vector diquarks are treated as composite subsystems. Motivated by previous form factor studies, we introduce a vertex correction based on the pion cloud picture as a first exploratory treatment of pion cloud effects at the GPD level, in order to improve the description of $E^q(ξ, x, t)$ and related magnetic observables. At the model input scale, only valence quarks are included. With the vertex correction, the electromagnetic form factors $G_{E, M}$ and the mass form factor $A$ obtained from the Mellin moments of the GPDs agree well with those obtained from direct calculations. The vertex correction brings the evolved charge weighted distribution $\sum{e^q E^q(ξ, x, t)}$ closer to the phenomenological parametrization. The correction shifts transverse strength from the central region toward the peripheral region, consistent with the long range character of the pion cloud contribution. The transverse structure results further suggest a harder longitudinal momentum distribution in the central region of the nucleon than in its periphery. Furthermore, the evolved real part of the proton Compton form factor, $\mathrm{Re} \mathcal{H}(ξ, t)$, has a reasonable magnitude and follows the main trend of the Quantum Deep Neural Networks (QDNN) extraction. In addition, $\mathrm{Re} \mathcal{H}$ may be more sensitive to the ERBL region than the other physical quantities calculated in this work.

In this paper, we have generalized Poisson's(1993) calculation for the case of gravitational radiation from a particle in a circular orbit around a black hole to the case of the wormhole. Considering the motion of a test particle around a Schwarzschild like wormhole and using Teukolsky formalism, we have obtained an analytical result of flux at infinity under the assumption of small velocities. Focusing on the 22-mode, we have found a novel 0.5PN order term beyond the quadrupole order, which vanishes in the black hole limit. On the other hand, all higher order terms are getting modified in such a way that they give correct correspondence in the black hole limit. We have further corroborated our results with the numerical investigations, which show that the two waveforms match well when the particle lies far from ISCO. Thus, our work provides one of the first attempts toward the study of the post-Newtonian waveforms involving wormholes (ECOs).

We investigate quantum gate teleportation as a primitive for satellite-enabled distributed quantum computing. In the considered architecture, a satellite distributes entangled photon pairs to two optical ground stations, which transfer the shared quantum resource to remote quantum processing units. This entanglement is then used to implement a nonlocal controlled-NOT gate between two circuit qubits located in different processors. We formulate the minimal four-qubit protocol, consisting of two network qubits that provide the entangled resource and two circuit qubits on which the distributed gate is performed. Atmospheric propagation effects in the gravitational field are modeled through an effective two-qubit depolarizing channel whose parameters depend on atmospheric and optical quantities, including the Fried parameter and receiver aperture. The performance of the protocol is quantified by the fidelity between the ideal CNOT output state and the state produced by noisy quantum gate teleportation. Our results show that the fidelity is governed by the quality of the distributed entanglement and depends jointly on atmospheric coherence, aperture size, and effective depolarization strength. These findings connect physical-layer modeling of satellite optical channels with gate-level performance in distributed quantum computing.

JWST has uncovered compact high-redshift sources with red, $\sim 5000$ K optical continua and broad Balmer lines, known as "little red dots" (LRDs). Whether these lines trace gas orbiting a massive black hole or electron scattering in a dense, Eddington-limited envelope around a lighter black hole leaves inferred masses spanning $10^{4}$--$10^{8}\,M_\odot$. We test both pictures by uniformly refitting public JWST/NIRSpec spectra of 29 LRDs, 15 other JWST broad-line sources, and luminosity-matched SDSS quasars. At fixed $\mathrm{H}α$ luminosity, LRD broad $\mathrm{H}α$ widths are remarkably uniform: their intrinsic scatter is 0.054 dex ($<0.075$ dex at 95%), less than half that of quasars (0.12 dex), clustering near $1760\ \mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$ outside the three brightest sources. Because single-epoch virial masses scale as width squared, they scatter intrinsically by $<0.16$ dex at fixed $\mathrm{H}α$ luminosity, making virial masses and mass functions nearly deterministic restatements of luminosity regardless of the underlying physics. Compared with the Keplerian speed $v_{\rm K}$ at the photosphere of an Eddington-limited envelope, computed from each source's measured continuum luminosity and temperature, observed widths are $2.5$--$3.0\,v_{\rm K}$ ($2\text{--}3\times$ standard expectations for virial factors $f_{\rm vir}\approx0.5\text{--}1$), with a quarter of the flux above escape speed and no detected continuum-luminosity dependence at fixed $\mathrm{H}α$ luminosity. Bound orbits outside a spherical photosphere thus require sub-Eddington luminosities ($\leq 0.10\text{--}0.30\,L_{\rm Edd}$), whereas an Eddington or super-Eddington envelope requires uniform $2\text{--}3\times$ non-gravitational broadening within the $\mathrm{H}α$-emitting gas.
