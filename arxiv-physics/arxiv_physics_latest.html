We derive an exact dynamical consistency relation for a smooth, periodically compactified five-dimensional Einstein--Gauss--Bonnet cosmology sourced by a real, minimally coupled canonical scalar field. For a spatially flat Friedmann--Lemaitre--Robertson--Walker slicing and a generally time-dependent warp factor, a contraction of the bulk equations eliminates both the scalar potential and the bulk cosmological constant. A direct orthonormal-curvature reconstruction gives the independent diagonal and mixed field equations and verifies the contracted Gauss--Bonnet identity. The integrated relation separates an exact total derivative from the dynamically active Lovelock sector. Importantly, satisfying the integrated sum rule alone does not imply a solution of the complete system. We prove that, for positive Gauss-Bonnet coupling, a smooth periodic time-independent warp factor and a canonical scalar exclude all exact expanding power-law scale factors $a(t)\propto t^p$ with $p>0$. This strengthens the previously established de Sitter sign obstruction and removes an earlier interpretation of $p=1/2$ as an admissible sign-compatible physical branch. For time-dependent warping, we reformulate bouncing conditions using proper time and the physical scale factor on each constant-$y$ hypersurface. We compare the common $w=-1$ geometric member with the perturbative weighted construction of Dias, Hoff da Silva, and da Rocha. At the level of the unexpanded Ricci-trace rearrangement, the curvature-only tensor contribution differs from the full Lanczos contraction by a definite scalar-curvature term. This distinction, and the additional independent field equations, delimit the physical conclusions that may be drawn from a necessary integrated condition.

On a four-dimensional, globally hyperbolic and asymptotically flat spacetime, we consider a real, linear, massive scalar field. Within the framework of perturbative algebraic quantum field theory and inspired by the principle of perturbative agreement, we regard the mass term in the underlying Lagrangian as an interaction potential. This allows us to construct the algebra of observables of the massive theory and, using a Møller map, to represent its elements as formal power series in the squared mass, with coefficients in the algebra of observables of a massless, conformally coupled scalar field. Following [DMP06], we use the bulk-to-boundary correspondence for the massless theory to project each coefficient to future null infinity $\mathscr{I}^+$.In four-dimensional Minkowski spacetime, we prove convergence of the projected series in the presence of an infrared cut-off and show that the global adiabatic limit exists but is trivial. We circumvent this obstruction by exploiting the time-slice axiom, which allows us to restrict our attention to observables localized in a causally convex neighbourhood containing a Cauchy hypersurface. Removing the cut-off locally yields a non-trivial limit and hence a bulk-to-boundary correspondence for the massive theory. We then extend our procedure to a self-interacting real scalar field. We represent interacting observables as formal power series in the coupling constant, with coefficients in the algebra of observables of a massless, conformally coupled scalar field. Each coefficient in the domain of the bulk-to-boundary map can thus be projected to future null infinity. As a concrete example, we apply this method to the stress-energy tensor of a massless scalar field with a quartic self-interaction, computing its projection up to first order in perturbation theory.

Supersymmetric indices provide stringent tests of dualities between superconformal field theories (SCFTs). We establish infinite families of Schur-index identities among four-dimensional $\mathcal N=2$ Argyres-Douglas (AD) theories, with the logarithms of their fugacities related linearly. These identities connect theories with markedly different physical properties without requiring infrared duality, while still implying simple relations among their central charges and flavor levels. Through the SCFT/VOA correspondence, they become character identities for the associated affine and $W$-algebras. One broad family relates $D_p(G)$ and $(A_{(h+1)(p-1)},G)$ theories, corresponding to affine and principal $W$-algebras at boundary admissible levels. At the level of the underlying state spaces, these character identities suggest that the corresponding VOAs are related by graded vector space isomorphisms, which are weaker than full VOA isomorphisms. We explicitly construct such a graded isomorphism between the VOAs associated with $(A_1,A_{4p-3})$ and $(A_1,D_{2p})$. We also show the correspondence for universal affine and principal $W$-algebras and test its compatibility with their simple quotients by matching the lowest-degree null states. We establish analogous character identities for certain non-vacuum modules in concrete examples and conjecture a broader extension. Finally, beyond the SCFT/VOA correspondence, we discover similar identities between level-one affine and Virasoro algebras, whose further generalizations point toward a new spectral level-rank duality in WZW models.

How precisely can an observer estimate a parameter related to a quantum field? This question is answered for quantum-mechanical systems using the SLD--Bures--Fisher metric and the quantum Cramér--Rao bound. However, local algebras in relativistic quantum field theory are of type III: they carry no trace and normal states have no density matrices, so the SLD quantum Fisher information and the quantum Cramér--Rao bound cannot be defined in the usual way. We define the SLD (Bures) quantum Fisher information on arbitrary von Neumann algebras through Uhlmann's transition probability and prove a quantum Cramér--Rao bound for collective measurements. We also show that the bound is sharp, and equals the largest classical Fisher information obtainable by measurements in the region.

We study the effect of the Chern--Simons (CS) coupling on the radial spectral stability of axially symmetric vortices in the $(2+1)$-dimensional Abelian--Higgs model. From the linear stability approach, we obtain the dispersion relations of gauge field perturbations, which signal the existence of a critical CS coupling $k_{\rm c}$ that depends on Maxwell's coupling constant. For $k<k_{\rm c}$, the continuous spectrum is purely imaginary and the modes remain gapped. At $k=k_{\rm c}$, one of the modes becomes gapless, while for $k>k_{\rm c}$ real eigenvalues appear, corresponding to exponentially growing radial modes. Direct time-evolution simulations are consistent with the spectral analysis, which shows instability for $k>k_{\rm c}$. Below the threshold, the perturbed vortex relaxes toward its stationary profile, whereas above the threshold, growing fluctuations deform the core and propagate outward. These results show that the CS coupling controls radial spectral stability of Abelian-Higgs vortices, and destabilization occurs for sufficiently large CS coupling, relative to the Maxwell coupling.

We consider 3-dimensional topological gauge theories with nilpotent gauge groups, both finite, that is Dijkgraaf-Witten theories, and continuous, that is Chern-Simons theories. Abelian topological quantum field theories are exactly those which do not detect the Borromean rings, and nilpotent gauge groups provide the natural next steps beyond the abelian case. We show that the untwisted Dijkgraaf-Witten theory with gauge group $G$ detects Brunnian links with up to $n+1$ components, but no Brunnian links with more components, if and only if $G$ is $n$-step nilpotent. Moreover, the expectation values of such links are determined by their Milnor invariants. We also study the properties of Chern-Simons theories with nilpotent Lie groups and draw parallels with those of the Dijkgraaf-Witten theories. Finally, we conjecture, using the Lazard correspondence, a relation between expectation values of line operators in the two types of theories, and test it in examples.

In this paper, we have generalized Poisson's(1993) calculation for the case of gravitational radiation from a particle in a circular orbit around a black hole to the case of the wormhole. Considering the motion of a test particle around a Schwarzschild like wormhole and using Teukolsky formalism, we have obtained an analytical result of flux at infinity under the assumption of small velocities. Focusing on the 22-mode, we have found a novel 0.5PN order term beyond the quadrupole order, which vanishes in the black hole limit. On the other hand, all higher order terms are getting modified in such a way that they give correct correspondence in the black hole limit. We have further corroborated our results with the numerical investigations, which show that the two waveforms match well when the particle lies far from ISCO. Thus, our work provides one of the first attempts toward the study of the post-Newtonian waveforms involving wormholes (ECOs).

We investigate quantum gate teleportation as a primitive for satellite-enabled distributed quantum computing. In the considered architecture, a satellite distributes entangled photon pairs to two optical ground stations, which transfer the shared quantum resource to remote quantum processing units. This entanglement is then used to implement a nonlocal controlled-NOT gate between two circuit qubits located in different processors. We formulate the minimal four-qubit protocol, consisting of two network qubits that provide the entangled resource and two circuit qubits on which the distributed gate is performed. Atmospheric propagation effects in the gravitational field are modeled through an effective two-qubit depolarizing channel whose parameters depend on atmospheric and optical quantities, including the Fried parameter and receiver aperture. The performance of the protocol is quantified by the fidelity between the ideal CNOT output state and the state produced by noisy quantum gate teleportation. Our results show that the fidelity is governed by the quality of the distributed entanglement and depends jointly on atmospheric coherence, aperture size, and effective depolarization strength. These findings connect physical-layer modeling of satellite optical channels with gate-level performance in distributed quantum computing.

We develop a gauge-independent theory of magnetic reconnection beyond the standard special-relativistic framework of inertial systems in flat spacetime. Our definitions of reconnection and its rate are independent of reference frame and coordinate system, allowing physical effects of gravity and net linear acceleration to be distinguished from coordinate effects. We extend the mesoscale theory of Liu et al. [PRL 118, 085101 (2017)], in which the reconnection rate depends on physics both within the diffusion region and in an upstream ideal, force-free region. The theory identifies three non-inertial and curved-spacetime (NICS) effects, including gravitationally induced magnetic tension: gravitational distortion of the reconnection plane changes the curvature of magnetic field lines. Applying the theory to a simplified accretion plasma near )a compact object, we find that (1) whether gravity increases or decreases the reconnection rate depends on the system's orientation relative to the gravitational source, with the greatest increase occurring when the outflow jet points toward or away from the source; and (2) the magnitude of the gravitational effect scales with the dimensionless parameter $\barκ \equiv r_sΔy^2/r^3$, where $r$ is the Schwarzschild radial coordinate, $r_s$ is the source's Schwarzschild radius, and $Δy$ is the mesoscale length.

Energetic-particle irradiation can produce persistent charge separation within lunar regolith, but the internal fields predicted by subsurface-charging models depend on how particle interactions are translated into retained charge. This critical review examines the evidence and assumptions connecting microscopic charge formation to macroscopic charging. We distinguish energy deposition, net charge transfer across a specified boundary, and the spatial distribution of signed excess charge. We organize these distinctions using an average retained-charge response defined for specified material, irradiation, electrical-state, and evaluation conditions. Its use as a volumetric source requires consistent charge accounting, compatible incident-flux definitions, and an adequate separation between response formation and subsequent transport. Applying these criteria to existing models, emission studies, and silicate irradiation experiments yields three assessments. First, emission measurements constrain electron loss across the measured boundary but do not locate internally redistributed charge. Second, evidence of internal reabsorption does not by itself establish that the charge separation sustaining subsurface fields is reduced; an illustrative calculation shows that the same net charge supply can produce different peak fields depending on transfer depths. Third, laboratory electrical damage in silicate crystals does not determine breakdown rates in cryogenic, porous regolith, which additionally require constraints on local fields, failure properties, and discharge recurrence. Joint constraints on boundary charge transfer, spatial charge separation, and post-irradiation relaxation are central to evaluating lunar subsurface-charging predictions.

A proposed resolution of the black-hole information paradox must explain which information an outside observer can recover from escaping radiation\cite{Hawking,Page,Hayden,Almheiri}. Environmental memory can return correlations to the source without delivering them to that observer\cite{ChenQi,ChenMemory}. Here we establish a sharp recovery frontier at fixed mean radiation yield for single-excitation channels and realize its qubit limit with an autonomous quantum-memory emitter. Unequal transmissions balance retained coherence against information in failed collection events. A reference--radiation correlation test distinguishes this gain even when the mean collected energy is unchanged. Identical transmission channels instead give the same recovery at matched collection, regardless of their emission timescales. For a broader class of interacting sources, an ordering of collected and inaccessible coupling spectra forbids unassisted quantum transmission irrespective of internal memory. A separate coding example supports a positive rate where uniform transmission at matched actual yield has zero capacity. Together, these results identify what openness must change to assist recovery: the accessible transmission structure. They supply a physically constrained comparison of evaporation mechanisms, separating information delivered to radiation from coherence retained elsewhere or an increase in the collected output.

TianQin, a space-borne gravitational wave observatory, is designed to detect gravitational-wave signals via laser interferometry. As the inhomogeneous space plasma surrounds the inter-satellite laser links, the propagation of the laser in space plasma can introduce pointing error noise. In this work, we quantitatively assess the effect of plasma on the pointing accuracy of TQ's inter-satellite laser links. We employ a magnetohydrodynamic model to simulate the plasma distribution around the TQ orbit for different orbital orientations. Using the fourth-order Runge-Kutta method to conduct the ray-tracing calculations over a TQ orbital cycle under a solar wind dynamic pressure close to the 23-year average, we find that the plasma-induced laser deflection angle and pointing eccentricity are on the order of $10^{-20} \ \mathrm{rad}$ and picometers, respectively. These values are significantly lower than TQ's requirements. Our results demonstrate that the impact of space plasma on TQ's pointing error is negligible in moderate space weather conditions, indicating a positive outlook for the TQ mission.

Supernova cooling provides some of the strongest constraints on light particles beyond the Standard Model, including sub-GeV dark matter (DM). While sufficiently large couplings to the Standard Model sector can trap these particles inside the supernova or cause them to decay before escaping, there typically exists an intermediate range of couplings in which their efficient production and subsequent escape lead to excessive energy loss, resulting in stringent constraints on new light particles. In this work, we demonstrate for the first time that, for certain types of DM-neutrino interactions, resonantly enhanced $s$-channel scattering with neutrinos can efficiently trap DM inside the supernova and can fundamentally alter the conventional supernova cooling constraints. In particular, we show that for mediator masses comparable to the supernova core temperature, this resonant scattering can prevent a substantial fraction of the produced DM from freely escaping, thereby significantly weakening, or even evading, the usual supernova cooling constraints on such models.

The detection of solar neutrino provides direct evidence for on-going fusion in the core of the Sun and serve as a probe for that solar core. Production rate of $^8$B is sensitive to the solar core temperature. Adiabatic flavor conversion of $^8$B solar neutrinos depends on the local solar electron density. Fast radio bursts (FRBs) are millisecond duration coherent radio transients, some of whose sources repeat, but the nature and active lifetimes of their engines remain uncertain. Strong lensing by galaxy clusters provides a physical lever on this lifetime problem: a short campaign can sample emission epochs of a repeating FRB separated by years, and distinct bursts in different lensed images, given a known relative lens delay, set a lower bound on its active lifetime, whereas delayed copies of a single burst only encode the lens delay. Based on the cluster-lens model of MACS J0717.5+3745, we quantify the accessible range of emission epochs for finite activity episodes, observing windows, and discovery selection. For a source-frame episode of duration \(100~{\rm yr}\) at \(z_s=2\) and a source-frame rate of \(0.1\,{\rm h}_{\rm src}^{-1}\), a five year campaign conditional on discovery yields a \(95\%\) gain threshold of \(m_{95}\simeq25.6\), corresponding to a source-frame span of \(L_{95}\simeq42.6~{\rm yr}\). At the population level, combining distinct burst associations with same burst echoes provides a way to test specified activity durations with a finite lens catalogue, e.g., activity durations differing by an order of magnitude can be distinguished with expected Bayes factor above $20$. More broadly, these lensed source frame epochs enable applications beyond activity-duration tests, e.g., linking burst activity to the evolution of the engine and its environment. Galaxy-cluster lensing therefore provides a promising future probe for accessing otherwise unobserved source history and for distinguishing between different activity duration models.

Binary systems in which a neutron star accretes material from a low-mass stellar companion arise from evolutionary processes that depend sensitively on the progenitor properties. It is usually assumed that the accreted material has approximately solar composition, unless the orbital period is $\lesssim80$ min, indicating an "ultracompact" system with a hydrogen deficient donor. However, few constraints on the donor composition are available. Here we apply a Bayesian model-comparison framework, BEANSP, to a sample of 21 thermonuclear bursts from the accretion-powered millisecond pulsar, IGR J17511-3057, to constrain the fuel composition as well as the system parameters. Our analysis strongly indicates an extremely hydrogen- and CNO-deficient fuel composition, with $X \approx 0.03$ and $Z_\rm{CNO}\approx 4 \times 10^{-4}$, unexpected for this system with its relatively long 3.5 hr orbital period. We explore the possible circumstances under which such an object can evolve, and discuss the implications for its history also in light of the three transient outbursts that have been observed to date. Such analyses are finding an unexpectedly wide range in donor compositions, that may help to explain the remarkable diversity in the burst properties of the known burst sources

Numerical relativity simulations of binary neutron star mergers and core-collapse supernovae indicate that the neutron star matter can be driven far from equilibrium due to large amplitude density oscillations, with frequency in the kHz range. Urca processes in neutron star matter try to restore the system towards equilibrium providing strong bulk viscous damping of these density oscillations. Standard bulk viscosity calculations typically assume small amplitude density oscillations, such that the deviation from chemical equilibrium $μ_Δ$ is less than the temperature $T$. In this work we compute the complete amplitude dependent bulk viscosity, taking into account, for the first time, thermal blurring effects and collisional broadening of in-medium nucleons. Our results show that the non-linear effects in bulk viscosity start to become important when the oscillation amplitude is about $10$ percent of the background density. In this regime the bulk viscosity can increase by three to five orders of magnitude. These results have important consequences for simulations of binary neutron star mergers as well as their multi-messenger observations.
