We study certain simple affine vertex algebras $L_{-1-h/6}(\mathfrak{g})$ from rank-one Argyres-Douglas theories, where $\mathfrak{g}$ is a non-simply-laced simple Lie algebra and $h$ is its Coxeter number. For $L_{-3}(F_4)$, we determine the maximal ideal of $V^{-3}(F_4)$, Zhu's algebra, and the associated variety. We also classify the irreducible highest-weight $L_{-3}(F_4)$-modules and determine the characters of its irreducible ordinary modules. The associated variety is $\overline{\mathbb{O}}_{\widetilde A_1}$, and the level $-3$ is totally collapsing for the orbit $\mathbb{O}_{\widetilde A_1}$. The Poisson coinvariants of the $C_2$-algebra of $L_{-3}(F_4)$ are isomorphic to $\mathbb{C}[\ell]/(\ell^5)$, where $\ell$ is induced by the conformal vector. Consequently, the characters of ordinary $L_{-3}(F_4)$-modules satisfy a fifth-order modular linear differential equation. We also give modular linear differential equations of orders five and eleven satisfied by the characters of ordinary modules for $L_{-2}(B_3)$ and $L_{-2}(G_2)$ respectively.

Replacing the circle by a manifold leads to current Lie algebras and their central and derivation extensions. We separate the coefficient algebra, harmonic analysis and geometric data. Differentials modulo exact forms describe central extensions; divergence-free vector fields give explicit cocycles, whose compatible derivations satisfy a stabilizer equation. Compact examples include the three-sphere and its current--Virasoro extension. We also explain the analytic qualifications for SL(2,R) and its symmetric space and the role of transported multiplication in soft geometries. The recent harmonic constructions of Campoamor-Stursberg, Marrani and Rausch de Traubenberg are compared with the cohomological work of Coquereaux, Frappat, Ragoucy and Sorba and Castellani's loop geometry and infinite-dimensional free differential algebras. A coefficient-algebra construction connects currents to higher brackets and clarifies the additional requirements of a dynamical theory. For Lie algebras of order F, central extensions obey coupled binary and symmetric F-ary conditions, with explicit examples and obstructions.

We study the contributions of half-BPS surface defects to supersymmetric Casimir energy and the universal logarithmic term of supersymmetric Rényi entropy in four- and six-dimensional SCFTs. Using equivariant integration of defect anomaly polynomials and its extension to supersymmetric Rényi backgrounds, we obtain closed expressions for these observables in terms of defect 't Hooft anomalies. For Gukov--Witten defects preserving transverse rotations in four-dimensional $\mathcal{N} = 4$ super Yang--Mills theories, both observables depend on a single R-symmetry anomaly. For $\mathcal{N} = (0, 4)$ defects in six-dimensional $\mathcal{N} = (1, 0)$ theories, additional terms arise from the gravitational anomaly. We provide complementary checks using chiral algebras, five-dimensional localization, and holographic probe branes at large $N$, while localized chiral fermions reproduce the gravitational-anomaly contributions. The framework also recovers Wilson-surface results in six-dimensional $\mathcal{N} = (2, 0)$ theories.

We study thermodynamic consistency and stability in a covariant two-flavor nonlocal Nambu--Jona-Lasinio model at zero chemical potential in the homogeneous quark mean-field approximation. A controlled large-source expansion gives a sufficient entropy-sign criterion directly from Euclidean kernel moments, without locating propagator poles. At fixed couplings and kernel, every admissible positive Gaussian mixture develops negative total entropy at sufficiently large source mass for each fixed positive temperature. In a separate light-source fit to pion observables and a subtracted condensate, one quartic-exponent kernel has positive entropy and heat capacity for all $T>0$, while $dP/dε=s/C_V$ exceeds unity on a finite interval. This constructive example separates thermal positivity from the static EOS slope test. Numerical refits show that the enhancement weakens with the condensate input, and a certified counterexample excludes uniform thermal positivity over the adopted matching domain. The results provide diagnostics of the mean-field functional, rather than a preferred phenomenological kernel. Omitted thermal mesons can change the low-temperature slope test, and the static derivative does not determine a real-time front velocity.

We consider the matrix model of a seven-dimensional quantum space built from concentric fuzzy $CP^3$'s of increasing matrix size, and show that its continuum limit is the metric cone $C(CP^3)$ over $CP^3$, not flat $R^7$. Every ingredient is fixed before any comparison, so this geometry is a prediction rather than an input. The two geometries agree in the $s$ wave ($p=0$) but separate in the higher angular sectors (harmonics on $CP^3$ of degree $p\geq1$): in each sector tested, the spectrum of the free Laplacian converges at second order to that of the cone, and at 20480 layers the cone is matched to $1\times10^{-9}$ for $p\leq8$, whereas flat space misses by 8-18%. The free propagator converges to that of the cone at the same order, and heat diffusion reproduces the $r^6$ measure and the Bessel spectrum of the cone. The maps between neighbouring layers are obtained in closed form from the polar decomposition of the second-quantized Schwinger-boson lift, using a known operator identity, and coincide with the Kováčik-Tekel coefficient-copying prescription. For $CP^1$ the construction reduces, away from the outer boundary, to the Kováčik-Tekel fuzzy onion. Modes along the twistor fibre are exactly degenerate with the harmonics on $S^4$.

In this paper, we present the first results for the one-loop decay $A \rightarrow ZZγ$ in the Two-Higgs-Doublet Model and its extension with Vector-Like Quarks. We calculate the one-loop partial decay widths and branching ratios for both unpolarized and polarized final-state $Z$ bosons. The effects of Vector-Like Quarks propagating in the loop are also examined in our phenomenological analysis. We find that the branching ratios can reach $10^{-5}-10^{-4}$ for several benchmark points within the viable parameter space of the considered models. With the high luminosities expected at future multi-TeV muon colliders, these effects may be experimentally accessible.

Collisionless shocks confine the particles they accelerate with the upstream wave field they themselves drive. Streaming particles resonantly amplify Alfvén waves, and the waves then confine the same particles by pitch-angle scattering. The rigidity a shock precursor can hold is therefore capped by how fast those waves grow, and that coupling has not previously been constrained in-situ at any interplanetary shock. Here we report Parker Solar Probe measurements of a fast, near-parallel coronal mass ejection-driven shock at 0.24~AU on 2023 March 13 that constrain this wave-particle coupling through the energetic-particle population it accelerates. We measure the spectral folding energy, the boundary between the confined and the upstream-escaping protons, and find that it falls from $\sim$6 to $\sim$1.5~MeV as $E_\mathrm{fold}\proptoΔt^{\,α}$ with $α\simeq0.34$, where $Δt$ is the time remaining before shock arrival. One precursor convective time before arrival this boundary is at $E_\mathrm{c}\simeq 2.46$~MeV, a characteristic confinement energy of a precursor of scale $L_\mathrm{EP}$. Read through the quasi-linear marginal-stability condition, the same evolution implies a resonant wave-growth timescale $τ_\mathrm{growth}(E)\propto E^{1/α}\simeq E^{2.92}$ that exceeds the precursor convective time above $E_\mathrm{c}$. The post-shock pitch-angle anisotropy reverses sign across the same confinement--escape transition at 2--5~MeV. The rigidity to which a shock precursor can confine particles is therefore set, in-situ, by how quickly the streaming population builds its own confining waves.

Black holes are usually described either as classical geometries on which quantum fields propagate or as objects whose complete quantum description lies beyond semiclassical gravity. Hawking radiation already shows, however, that a black hole is not an exactly isolated system: the horizon carries a thermal scale and, after coarse graining, may be associated with fluctuations, dissipation and environmental correlations. Whether this horizon-scale openness can be encoded directly in the gravitational background, before any stochastic treatment of the metric, remains an open question. Here we answer it by introducing, for the first time, a conserved Hawking-scale anisotropic source representing the coarse-grained near-horizon atmosphere and solving the Einstein equations for a static black hole at fixed ADM mass. The dressed geometry has a renormalized mass function, a self-consistently shifted horizon and a modified surface gravity. Because the same lapse governs the horizon and timelike geodesics, the thermal response is accompanied by a correlated orbital restructuring: the effective potential is renormalized, the ISCO moves inward and the near-horizon residence time increases. The construction adds no Langevin force to a fixed Schwarzschild background; rather, it defines a deterministic dressed mean geometry, providing a controlled background on which stochastic metric fluctuations may subsequently be treated in the Einstein-Langevin framework. This establishes the deterministic mean-field sector as the necessary first step for future stochastic gravity programs addressing horizon-scale openness.

Collisionless shocks accelerate particles at a rate set by the diffusion coefficient of those particles in waves they themselves generate. Theory fixes that coefficient only in steady state, and no measurement has tested whether an evolving shock ever reaches that state. We report Parker Solar Probe observations of a fast, near-parallel interplanetary shock at 0.24 au, encountered four hours after eruption, with the upstream waves and the protons they scatter recorded on the same magnetic field line. From the wave spectra alone we derive the parallel mean free path of 0.05-30 MeV protons, resolved in energy and in distance from the shock. The mirror force of the measured compressive fluctuations supplies the scattering through the pitch angles that gyroresonance cannot reach. The mean free path grows with distance nearly as the steady self-generated solution requires, yet the steady balance holds at one energy only, near 1.3 MeV. The intensity profiles are a factor of two shallower than the measured coefficient predicts, because the waves are still growing. Confinement fails between 2.3 and 6 MeV, beginning a factor of two below what the age of the shock allows. The growth of the waves its own particles drive, rather than its age or its size, limits the energy an evolving shock confines.

Earth's ionosphere is primarily generated by the Sun's ionization of atoms in the upper atmosphere. This plasma environment is highly dynamic, exhibits strong spatial and temporal, variations and significantly impacts power systems, navigation, and satellite and ground communications. The University of Delaware Ionospheric Probe (UDIP) was developed as a hands-on learning experience for University of Delaware students on the ionosphere itself and on the development of space technology. Students (mostly undergraduates) have led all aspects of the experiment including mission planning, electronics development, mechanical development, software development, and scientific data analysis. The present study is intended as a guide to readers on how the development of space and near-space flight missions can be successfully incorporated into an undergraduate physics curriculum. The fourth version of the experiment (UDIP-4) flew along with student experiments from across the USA on the 2024 RockSat-C sounding rocket, which was launched from NASA Wallops Flight Facility. During the flight, UDIP-4's Langmuir probe successfully measured the ionosphere's density profile -- electron density as a function of altitude -- up to an apogee of 114 km. Though the measured density profile agrees well overall with the predictions of theoretical models, UDIP-4 identified a thin layer of enhanced electron density at an altitude of approximately 110 km that is consistent with a sporadic E layer.

SRAM-based FPGA system-on-chip devices are vulnerable to single-event upsets (SEUs) in configuration memory, which may perturb programmable routing resources and degrade communication fabrics. In modern Zynq UltraScale+ MPSoCs, such routing disturbances can introduce small propagation delay shifts that remain logically transparent yet compromise AXI-based data transfers and lead to silent data corruption. Although routing delay degradation and AXI interconnect failures have been studied independently, their experimental correlation under neutron irradiation has not been established. This work presents a cross-layer investigation on a ZCU104 platform integrating routing-dominated delay sensors with an AXI interconnect benchmark comprising replicated accelerators. Neutron irradiation experiments were conducted on the fully operational system, while a frame-level configuration fault injector implemented via the internal configuration access port enables controlled upset emulation. Measured routing delay events are statistically correlated with communication failures, and cross-sections for both timing shifts and AXI malfunctions are derived. The results experimentally demonstrate how neutron-induced routing perturbations propagate into system-level silent data corruption in UltraScale+ MPSoCs, providing insight for resilience-oriented AXI-based design in neutron-rich environments.

In this paper we propose a geometrically motivated formulation of gravity in an invariant Weyl--Integrable space-time (IWIST), in which the background geometry is not imposed a priori but is dynamically determined through the Palatini variational principle. For a scalar field non-minimally coupled to gravity action, the resulting compatibility condition defines a Weyl--Integrable geometry which is preserved by a local Weyl transformation of the metric and the geo­me­tri­cal scalar field. We construct an action invariant under both diffeomorphisms and Weyl transformations and introduce a Weyl-covariant variational procedure based on an invariant extension of the divergence theorem. The corresponding field equations are obtained for the metric, the Weyl scalar, and the Weyl gauge field. We further formulate the theory in the Einstein--Riemann frame, where the effective metric is Riemannian and the scalar field is re­inter­pre­ted as a phy­si­cal degree of freedom of geometric origin. Finally, we propose a geometrical mechanism for the explicit breaking of Weyl symmetry by introducing a coupling between the Weyl gauge field and a current constructed from the scalar sector. The resulting theory remains diffeomorphism covariant while departing from local Weyl invariance. The Einstein--Riemann representation of the broken theory contains an additional generally covariant interaction, making the Weyl-invariant model a particular limit of a more general gravitational theory.

We present the first targeted Einstein Probe (EP) X-ray observations of any planetary nebula (PN). Using the onboard Follow-up X-ray Telescope (FXT), we detect X-ray emission within the central region of IC 1297. The spectral analysis yields a plasma temperature of $kT = 0.086^{+0.054}_{-0.058}$ keV ($T\sim1.0 \times 10^{6}$ K), characteristic of shocked wind bubbles in PNe, whereas there is no evidence that its Wolf-Rayet central star emits X-rays. The absorption-corrected X-ray luminosity in the 0.3--2.0 keV energy band is $L_{\mathrm{X}} = 2.8 \times 10^{32}$ erg s$^{-1}$, also consistent with a hot bubble. At an adopted distance of $\sim3.8$ kpc, IC 1297 is the most distant X-ray emitting PN found to date, opening up a new observational window on hot-bubble formation and evolution. It is also among the most luminous known X-ray-emitting PNe. Our results demonstrate the capability of the EP FXT for detecting and characterizing faint, extended X-ray sources in the soft X-ray band for distant PNe, extending the known spectral properties of detectable X-ray emitting PNe. We also compiled the X-ray properties of all 41 known Galactic XPNe, finding a clear correlation between distance and X-ray luminosity that reflects an observational bias toward detecting the brightest sources at large distances. The compilation also shows a separation in X-ray luminosity between hot bubbles powered by H-rich and [WR]-type central stars, with the latter reaching luminosities up to an order of magnitude higher. The ten most luminous XPNe all host [WR]/PG 1159 central stars.

The event horizon is the most crucial feature that distinguishes black hole (BH) from all other objects in the universe. However, since no radiation can escape from the event horizon, its direct observational evidence remains lacking. Although both are compact objects, the event horizon leads to differences in the radiative behavior between black hole and neutron star (NS) X-ray binaries. Historically efforts to probe the existence of event horizons revealed that the BH X-ray binaries are fainter than NSs with similar orbital periods in their quiescent states. Here, we use a special class of X-ray binaries namely the ultra-compact X-ray binaries (UCXBs), to reveal the existence of the event horizon near stellar-mass black holes. In short-period UCXBs, gravitational radiation dominates the orbital angular momentum loss, allowing us to accurately determine their accretion rates. Based on five UCXBs with orbital periods shorter than 40 min, located in globular clusters, and identified accretors (NS or BH), we obtained their accretion rates. Combining their accretion rates and X-ray luminosities, we calculated the X-ray radiative efficiencies of NS and BH. The radiative efficiency of BH is about two to three orders of magnitude lower than that of NS systems, indicating the accretion energy is swallowed by the BH, thereby indirectly probe the presence of the event horizon.

We review the promise of upcoming gravitational-wave (GW) observations in addressing some of the important open questions in astrophysics and cosmology. A decade after the first direct detection of GWs, the field is entering a transformative phase driven by progressive detector upgrades and the anticipated advent of space-based and next-generation ground-based observatories. This review surveys our current understanding across this landscape and offers some perspective on the discoveries that upcoming GW observations are poised to deliver.

A0538-66 is a peculiar Be/X-ray binary in the Large Magellanic Cloud, hosting the fastest spinning neutron star of its class (~69ms) and showing the brightest optical bursts of any system of this type. We report four XMM-Newton observations obtained near periastron in 2025, together with MAXI and REM monitoring, and a reanalysis of archival RXTE data. On 30 September 2025 the source reached L_X~1.5E39 erg/s (0.2-10 keV), the highest level since 1980, and in February, May, and August 2026, MAXI detected similarly bright outbursts. During the XMM-Newton observation taken on 30 September 2025, we detected the pulsations, measuring P_spin=69.25878(2) ms and dP_spin/dt=6.58(9)E-10 s/s; the pulsed fraction was low (~3% in 1-10 keV), increased with energy, and the soft and hard pulse profiles were shifted by half a cycle. A new detection in RXTE data of 3 December 1998 gives a faster spin period of P_spin=68.88813(28) ms. The XMM-Newton/RGS spectra show emission lines from photoionised plasma at rest and blueshifted absorption lines that imply an outflow at ~0.12c. This indicates a highly super-Eddington accretion rate and an intrinsic L_X~1.5E39 erg/s, and it makes A0538-66 a transient ultra-luminous X-ray pulsar, a class of which only a few objects are known so far. Since 2023 the optical bursts observed by REM have become longer and brighter, resembling those of the 1980s, but most of them lack a luminous X-ray counterpart (L_X>1E37 erg/s). We interpret these results in terms of a radiation pressure dominated accretion disc whose geometrically thick inner regions intermittently screen the innermost emitting regions.

Finite-Temperature Equations of State for Neutron-Star Mergers within the Quark Mean-Field Model

Han, Shuochong 等

We extend the quark mean-field (QMF) model to finite temperature and systematically investigate the thermodynamic properties of uniform nuclear matter, using the relativistic mean-field (RMF) model as a reference. For each model, we consider two parameter sets calibrated to the same selected nuclear-matter saturation properties but with different nucleon Dirac effective-mass ratios at saturation. We examine the effective nucleon mass and the composition of $β$-equilibrium matter, together with the free energy, energy density, pressure, entropy, and heat capacities of symmetric nuclear matter, pure neutron matter, and $β$-equilibrium matter. The QMF and RMF models exhibit similar qualitative trends, but their predictions increasingly differ at high density, where the effective masses have distinct density dependences. These differences are particularly evident in the thermal pressure and thermal index. For the two parameter sets considered in this work, we further provide compact analytical representations of the thermal index, $Γ_{\rm th}(ρ_{\rm b},T,β)$ over baryon densities of 0.08-1.0 $\rm fm^{-3}$, temperatures of 5-100 MeV, and isospin asymmetries of 0-1. The fits reproduce the numerical results with an accuracy better than $2\%$ and can be directly implemented in binary neutron-star merger simulations. This study establishes a finite-temperature extension of the QMF framework and provides a basis for future assessments of how a quark-structure-based description of nuclear matter affects thermal support and post-merger dynamics in binary neutron-star mergers.
