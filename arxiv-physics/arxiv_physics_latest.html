We demonstrate that weight-space programming and edits guided by sparse autoencoders can remove unwanted aspects of a particle-physics foundation model through direct edits to its weights. In a foundation model trained on jets, we observe that adding a single soft neutral particle causes large changes in jet classification, violating infrared safety. We locate the responsible attention heads with mechanistic analysis and trace the flaw's origin to pretraining on datasets with different reconstruction thresholds. We then propose two targeted approaches to repair the flaw: by removing three latent directions identified with sparse autoencoders, and by letting language-model agents edit the weights directly, without gradients and with only 50 queries to the model. Both their edits individually remove almost the entire flaw and leave the rest of the model's response nearly unchanged. Compared to fine-tuning, both methods change far fewer parameters. Although built only on $W$ and QCD jets, the edits carry over to all signal classes, the pretrained generator, and a newly trained regression head.

We study the relationship between the pair creation mechanism in physics and the pairing ($\boxtimes_{\mathbf{A}_{\text{ub}}}$) in mathematics, focusing on the modular and algebraic properties. We investigate the massless Dirac field in locally rotationally symmetric (LRS) spacetimes using a covariant spinorial-fluid representation, with the spinor current and spin direction aligned with the LRS frame. When the current has both vorticity and sheet twist, characterized by a constant ratio $λ$, the LRS integrability condition relates evolution and propagation derivatives, reducing the field equations to an ordinary differential system that can be integrated exactly. The integrability of the chiral angle forces the heat flux to vanish and the source to saturate the null energy condition along the two radial null directions. A constant boost in the radial Lorentzian two-plane maps the spinor current to a canonical congruence, which is stationary (LRS~I) for $|λ|>1$ and irrotational and geodesic (LRS~III) for $|λ|<1$. In this frame, the local metric is reconstructed in the Goode--Wainwright--Van den Bergh canonical form. For $|λ|>1$ the energy density is positive, and the metric is locally isometric to the horizonless charged Taub--NUT (Brill) metric: in the polar representation, the massless Dirac field supplies a stress--energy tensor algebraically identical to that of the Brill electromagnetic field, with no Maxwell field present. For $|λ|<1$, the energy density is negative and the metric is the hyperbolic ($k=-1$) charged NUT metric with a formal negative squared-charge parameter, whose homogeneous slices are of LRS Bianchi type~VIII.

我们使用协变旋量流体表示研究局部旋转对称 (LRS) 时空中的无质量狄拉克场，其中旋量电流和自旋方向与 LRS 框架对齐。当电流同时具有涡度和片扭曲（以恒定比率 $λ$ 为特征）时，LRS 可积条件将演化导数和传播导数联系起来，将场方程简化为可精确积分的常微分系统。手性角的可积性迫使热通量消失，并且源沿着两个径向零方向使零能量条件饱和。径向洛伦兹两平面中的恒定增强将旋量电流映射到正则同余，对于 $|λ|>1$ 是静止的 (LRS~I)，对于 $|λ|<1$ 是无旋和测地线 (LRS~III)。在此框架中，局部度量以 Goode-Wainwright-Van den Bergh 规范形式重建。对于$|λ|>1$，能量密度为正，并且度量与无水平带电Taub--NUT（布里尔）度量局部等距：在极坐标表示中，无质量狄拉克场提供了与布里尔电磁场代数上相同的应力-能量张量，但不存在麦克斯韦场。对于 $|λ|<1$，能量密度为负，度量为双曲 ($k=-1$) 带电 NUT 度量，具有正式的负平方电荷参数，其齐次切片为 LRS Bianchi 型~VIII。

In this paper, we have generalized Poisson's(1993) calculation for the case of gravitational radiation from a particle in a circular orbit around a black hole to the case of the wormhole. Considering the motion of a test particle around a Schwarzschild like wormhole and using Teukolsky formalism, we have obtained an analytical result of flux at infinity under the assumption of small velocities. We investigate quantum gate teleportation as a primitive for satellite-enabled distributed quantum computing. In the considered architecture, a satellite distributes entangled photon pairs to two optical ground stations, which transfer the shared quantum resource to remote quantum processing units. This entanglement is then used to implement a nonlocal controlled-NOT gate between two circuit qubits located in different processors. We formulate the minimal four-qubit protocol, consisting of two network qubits that provide the entangled resource and two circuit qubits on which the distributed gate is performed. Atmospheric propagation effects in the gravitational field are modeled through an effective two-qubit depolarizing channel whose parameters depend on atmospheric and optical quantities, including the Fried parameter and receiver aperture. The performance of the protocol is quantified by the fidelity between the ideal CNOT output state and the state produced by noisy quantum gate teleportation. Our results show that the fidelity is governed by the quality of the distributed entanglement and depends jointly on atmospheric coherence, aperture size, and effective depolarization strength. These findings connect physical-layer modeling of satellite optical channels with gate-level performance in distributed quantum computing.

We develop a gauge-independent theory of magnetic reconnection beyond the standard special-relativistic framework of inertial systems in flat spacetime. Our definitions of reconnection and its rate are independent of reference frame and coordinate system, allowing physical effects of gravity and net linear acceleration to be distinguished from coordinate effects. We extend the mesoscale theory of Liu et al. [PRL 118, 085101 (2017)], in which the reconnection rate depends on physics both within the diffusion region and in an upstream ideal, force-free region. The theory identifies three non-inertial and curved-spacetime (NICS) effects, including gravitationally induced magnetic tension: gravitational distortion of the reconnection plane changes the curvature of magnetic field lines. Applying the theory to a simplified accretion plasma near )a compact object, we find that (1) whether gravity increases or decreases the reconnection rate depends on the system's orientation relative to the gravitational source, with the greatest increase occurring when the outflow jet points toward or away from the source; and (2) the magnitude of the gravitational effect scales with the dimensionless parameter $\barκ \equiv r_sΔy^2/r^3$, where $r$ is the Schwarzschild radial coordinate, $r_s$ is the source's Schwarzschild radius, and $Δy$ is the mesoscale length.

Energetic-particle irradiation can produce persistent charge separation within lunar regolith, but the internal fields predicted by subsurface-charging models depend on how particle interactions are translated into retained charge. This critical review examines the evidence and assumptions connecting microscopic charge formation to macroscopic charging. We distinguish energy deposition, net charge transfer across a specified boundary, and the spatial distribution of signed excess charge. We organize these distinctions using an average retained-charge response defined for specified material, irradiation, electrical-state, and evaluation conditions. Its use as a volumetric source requires consistent charge accounting, compatible incident-flux definitions, and an adequate separation between response formation and subsequent transport. Applying these criteria to existing models, emission studies, and silicate irradiation experiments yields three assessments. First, emission measurements constrain electron loss across the measured boundary but do not locate internally redistributed charge. Second, evidence of internal reabsorption does not by itself establish that the charge separation sustaining subsurface fields is reduced; an illustrative calculation shows that the same net charge supply can produce different peak fields depending on transfer depths.
