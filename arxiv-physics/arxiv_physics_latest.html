We derive new transformation formulas for elliptic hypergeometric sum/integrals associated with the orthogonal groups $\mathrm{SO}(N)$. The sum/integrals are written in terms of the lens elliptic gamma function, which depends on an integer $r\geq 1$ in addition to the usual complex variables, and they reduce to the elliptic hypergeometric integrals of Spiridonov and Vartanov for $r=1$. The derivation uses the $BC_n\leftrightarrow BC_{\tilde n}$ transformation formula proven in our previous paper, together with a duplication formula for the lens elliptic gamma function proven here. The duplication formula takes a different form depending on whether $r$ is even or odd. Physically, the new transformations show that the supersymmetric lens indices on $S^1\times S^3/\mathbb{Z}_r$ of the 4d $\mathcal{N}=1$$\mathrm{SO}(N)$ and $\mathrm{SO}(N_f-N+4)$ gauge theories related by Seiberg duality are equal for odd $r$. For even $r$, where the lens index depends on the global form of the gauge group, they show the equality of the contributions from flat connections whose holonomies lie in a common maximal torus. The $BC_n$ formula itself gives the corresponding statement for the symplectic groups.

We give a geometric formulation of the $q$DE/ODE/IM correspondence for families of differential operators associated with simple Lie algebras of ADE type. Under analytic hypotheses, the $q$-connection associated with shifted monodromy defines a twisted $(G,q)$-oper and has Steinberg form in the frame of solutions normalized by prescribed asymptotics in a sector at infinity. We identify the central connection between the normalized solution frames at zero and infinity with a universal meromorphic $(G,q)$-Wronskian. Its generalized minors give $Q$-functions satisfying the ADE $QQ$-relations. When $q$ is not a root of unity, we parametrize shifted monodromy by quantum-affine $q$-characters evaluated on shifted $Q$-ratios, using the same character formulas that describe normalized transfer-matrix eigenvalues. We verify the analytic hypotheses for generic untwisted Feigin--Frenkel--Hernandez affine opers, thereby relating these affine opers to twisted $(G,q)$-opers.

We consider a QCD-inspired chiral $SU(4)$ model, with a new fundamental sector ($q_{\rm HC}$) charged under the confining hypercolor gauge group $SU(3)_{\rm HC}$ as a template for electroweak dynamics and compositeness. The $(\mathrm{SU}(4)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(4)_R)/\mathrm{SU}(4)_V$ breaking leads to a rich structure of spin-0 and spin-1 resonances which can be produced by the hadronization of $q_{\rm HC}$ in Drell-Yan processes at FCC-hh. Working in the true vacuum at a general misalignment angle $θ$, the fermion currents are derived for the fifteen techni-rho ($ρ_{\rm HC}$) resonances, the techni-omega ($ω_{\rm HC}$), and the techni-eta-prime ($η^{\prime}_{\rm HC}$) using the Hidden Local Symmetry formalism. The estimated branching fractions pave the way for studying the model spectroscopy. The analysis of the production by hadronization of $ρ_{\rm HC}$, $ω_{\rm HC}$ and $η^{\prime}_{\rm HC}$ states is presented for a 100 TeV future hadronic collider, FCC-hh. The projected discovery of $ρ_{\rm HC}$ and $η^{\prime}_{\rm HC}$ is complementary to the direct production by Drell-Yan or Vector Boson Fusion, providing a smoking gun signature for Higgs compositeness.

The unification of the four fundamental interactions remains one of the central open challenges in fundamental physics. Most existing unification scenarios rely on extra spatial dimensions, novel symmetry structures, or unobserved dynamical degrees of freedom. In this work, we construct a minimal phenomenological dual-moment framework rooted in primitive charges and motion-induced kinetic moments. Gravitational, electromagnetic, and strong interactions are coherently described within a unified inverse-square-law formalism. We postulate a universal coupling relation $T = Z/c^2$ that connects source-charge coupling coefficients and moment-mediated coupling coefficients across different interaction sectors. While the framework can formally accommodate weak-charge degrees of freedom, full quantitative computations for weak-interaction dynamics are deferred to future follow-up studies. The model reproduces established low-energy dynamical limits for planetary orbits, atomic systems, and heavy-quark hadrons, and predicts distinguishable corrections to light deflection by rapidly spinning compact objects. We further design three independent test schemes: improved torsion-balance measurements, a mass-current laboratory deflection experiment, and astronomical light-bending observations. Although laboratory measurements impose formidable technical challenges, astronomical observations of compact spinning objects offer promising avenues to constrain or falsify the proposed framework.

四种基本相互作用的统一仍然是基础物理学中的核心开放挑战之一。大多数现有的统一场景依赖于额外的空间维度、新颖的对称结构或未观察到的动态自由度。在这项工作中，我们构建了一个植根于原始电荷和运动引起的动矩的最小唯象双矩框架。引力、电磁和强相互作用在统一的平方反比定律形式中得到了连贯的描述。我们假设一个通用耦合关系 $T = Z/c^2$ 连接不同相互作用部分的源电荷耦合系数和矩介导耦合系数。虽然该框架可以正式适应弱电荷自由度，但弱相互作用动力学的完整定量计算将推迟到未来的后续研究。该模型再现了行星轨道、原子系统和重夸克强子的低能动力学极限，并预测快速旋转致密物体对光偏转的可区分修正。我们进一步设计了三个独立的测试方案：改进的扭转平衡测量、质量流实验室偏转实验和天文光弯曲观测。尽管实验室测量带来了巨大的技术挑战，但对紧密旋转物体的天文观测为限制或证伪所提出的框架提供了有希望的途径。

In this paper, we have generalized Poisson's(1993) calculation for the case of gravitational radiation from a particle in a circular orbit around a black hole to the case of the wormhole. Considering the motion of a test particle around a Schwarzschild like wormhole and using Teukolsky formalism, we have obtained an analytical result of flux at infinity under the assumption of small velocities. Focusing on the 22-mode, we have found a novel 0.5PN order term beyond the quadrupole order, which vanishes in the black hole limit. On the other hand, all higher order terms are getting modified in such a way that they give correct correspondence in the black hole limit. We have further corroborated our results with the numerical investigations, which show that the two waveforms match well when the particle lies far from ISCO. Thus, our work provides one of the first attempts toward the study of the post-Newtonian waveforms involving wormholes (ECOs).

Energetic-particle irradiation can produce persistent charge separation within lunar regolith, but the internal fields predicted by subsurface-charging models depend on how particle interactions are translated into retained charge. This critical review examines the evidence and assumptions connecting microscopic charge formation to macroscopic charging. We distinguish energy deposition, net charge transfer across a specified boundary, and the spatial distribution of signed excess charge. We organize these distinctions using an average retained-charge response defined for specified material, irradiation, electrical-state, and evaluation conditions. Its use as a volumetric source requires consistent charge accounting, compatible incident-flux definitions, and an adequate separation between response formation and subsequent transport. Applying these criteria to existing models, emission studies, and silicate irradiation experiments yields three assessments. First, emission measurements constrain electron loss across the measured boundary but do not locate internally redistributed charge. Second, evidence of internal reabsorption does not by itself establish that the charge separation sustaining subsurface fields is reduced; an illustrative calculation shows that the same net charge supply can produce different peak fields depending on transfer depths. Third, laboratory electrical damage in silicate crystals does not determine breakdown rates in cryogenic, porous regolith, which additionally require constraints on local fields, failure properties, and discharge recurrence. Joint constraints on boundary charge transfer, spatial charge separation, and post-irradiation relaxation are central to evaluating lunar subsurface-charging predictions.

A proposed resolution of the black-hole information paradox must explain which information an outside observer can recover from escaping radiation\cite{Hawking,Page,Hayden,Almheiri}. Environmental memory can return correlations to the source without delivering them to that observer\cite{ChenQi,ChenMemory}. Here we establish a sharp recovery frontier at fixed mean radiation yield for single-excitation channels and realize its qubit limit with an autonomous quantum-memory emitter. Unequal transmissions balance retained coherence against information in failed collection events. A reference--radiation correlation test distinguishes this gain even when the mean collected energy is unchanged. Identical transmission channels instead give the same recovery at matched collection, regardless of their emission timescales. For a broader class of interacting sources, an ordering of collected and inaccessible coupling spectra forbids unassisted quantum transmission irrespective of internal memory. A separate coding example supports a positive rate where uniform transmission at matched actual yield has zero capacity. Together, these results identify what openness must change to assist recovery: the accessible transmission structure. They supply a physically constrained comparison of evaporation mechanisms, separating information delivered to radiation from coherence retained elsewhere or an increase in the collected output.

TianQin, a space-borne gravitational wave observatory, is designed to detect gravitational-wave signals via laser interferometry. As the inhomogeneous space plasma surrounds the inter-satellite laser links, the propagation of the laser in space plasma can introduce pointing error noise. In this work, we quantitatively assess the effect of plasma on the pointing accuracy of TQ's inter-satellite laser links. We employ a magnetohydrodynamic model to simulate the plasma distribution around the TQ orbit for different orbital orientations. Using the fourth-order Runge-Kutta method to conduct the ray-tracing calculations over a TQ orbital cycle under a solar wind dynamic pressure close to the 23-year average, we find that the plasma-induced laser deflection angle and pointing eccentricity are on the order of $10^{-20} \ \mathrm{rad}$ and picometers, respectively. Magnetic null points define the topology of magnetic fields in stellar coronae, planetary magnetospheres, and astrospheres and are commonly associated with magnetic reconnection. We investigate the local structure of stationary 3D resistive, incompressible magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) flows in the vicinity of an isolated magnetic null and an isolated velocity stagnation point, assuming that the two nulls are colocated. The governing MHD equations are solved self-consistently using a first-order local expansion of the magnetic and velocity fields about the null. For any given magnetic-null configuration, the equations admit a two-parameter family of stationary solutions, comprising multiple solution branches with distinct velocity topologies. Along these families, the velocity-null polarity can change at bifurcation points where the flow becomes effectively 2.5D. A single magnetic null can therefore support positive-radial, negative-radial, and spiral stagnation flows, with qualitatively different inflow, outflow, and rotational properties. The associated pressure and resistivity distributions are likewise determined self-consistently and vary across the solution family, demonstrating that they are coupled to the flow topology rather than prescribed independently. The generic solutions do not require the velocity and magnetic separatrices to coincide, allowing plasma to cross the magnetic fan separatrix and providing a local reconnective flow. These results demonstrate that, in the presence of nonuniform resistivity, the magnetic topology alone does not uniquely determine the associated stationary flow, pressure, or resistivity structure around a 3D magnetic null, revealing the nonlinear feedback between all macroscopic variables and resistivity. The constant-resistivity field-aligned and spiral field-crossing solutions identified previously are recovered as special cases.

High-energy cosmic rays produce atmospheric muons through cascades of hadronic interactions, yet simulations often underestimate the muon content of extensive air showers. Whether the same problem affects energetic atmospheric muons, which probe a different part of the cascade, is less clear. Here we compare published atmospheric-muon measurements from four experiments with reference predictions based on a shared choice of hadronic interaction model and primary cosmic-ray flux. We find that the measurements predominantly exceed the central predictions across different experimental techniques, from surface measurements to deep detectors. This recurring excess is more consistent across the measurements than any inferred dependence on energy or zenith angle. These atmospheric muons preferentially originate from energetic mesons produced early in the cascade, whereas the total muon content of extensive air showers also reflects interactions at later stages. Optical synchrotron polarization is one of the most direct probes of the magnetic-field structure and particle-acceleration processes in the relativistic jets of blazars.
