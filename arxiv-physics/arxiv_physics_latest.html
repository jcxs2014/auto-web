A string couples to a two-form gauge potential whose local descriptions need not fit together as a single global form. Principal 2-bundles provide a geometric description of this gluing. However, we recover surface amplitudes and quantum partition functions, as well as local classical field dynamics, without the 2-bundle formalism. For global phases, topological and index invariants, and twisted-sheaf moduli, established equivalences give alternative descriptions through cocycles, bundles, operators, or modules, with the required global compatibility data. For three-dimensional finite gauge theories with cohomological actions, Fourier transformation expresses the partition function as a sum of partition functions of ordinary finite-group gauge theories. An invertible local substitution reduces invariant classical actions for a class of 2-bundle connections represented by finite-dimensional crossed modules to ordinary gauge and tensor dynamics. These finite and local reductions explicitly eliminate field variables. A four-dimensional quadratic example instead admits a direct cochain evaluation even when an ordinary gauge-theory reduction fails. We investigate the signatures of phase-space noncommutativity in an anisotropic oscillator and propose an optomechanical protocol to experimentally characterize it through Stokes-like measurements of an optical field. Starting from a deformed phase-space algebra characterized by the position-position and momentum-momentum noncommutativity parameters $θ$ and $η$, we employ the Darboux transformation to construct an equivalent Hamiltonian in canonical variables. The resulting effective dynamics exhibits correlations between the oscillator degrees of freedom. We derive the corresponding covariance matrix and examine how the noncommutative parameters enter the mechanical dynamics and the optical response. By coupling the oscillator to a coherent optical probe, we obtain analytical expressions for the time-dependent optical coherence attenuation and the nonlinear Kerr phase. We then formulate an interferometric detection scheme through Stokes-like measurements, in which the outgoing optical field is combined with a phase-controlled reference beam at a balanced beam splitter, and the resulting photocurrents are measured using photodetectors. The optical response provides access to the modified normal-mode frequencies, which enables us to estimate experimental values of $θ$ and $η$. Our results establish a theoretical connection between NC-space dynamics, Gaussian-state correlations, and experimentally accessible optical observables, suggesting an optomechanical route toward testing signatures of phase-space noncommutativity.

We establish global control of perturbative D-term data governing semiclassical vacuum sectors in chiral supersymmetric gauge theories on $\mathbb{R}^3\times S^1$. A closed-form, one-loop-exact D-term coefficient, including 4d Fayet--Iliopoulos (FI) parameters, gives a uniformly controlled leading potential away from massless thresholds and fixes local FI and Chern--Simons couplings near them. This allows lifting the 3d Intriligator--Seiberg classification of semiclassical vacua to $\mathbb{R}^3\times S^1$ across the compact holonomy space. The framework is showcased by finding a remarkably rich set of vacuum sectors in a simple abelian model and reproducing on flat space the R-twisted reduction of an $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ Maruyoshi--Song theory to the Gang--Yamazaki theory, first found on $S^3\times S^1$. The examples demonstrate the stakes of global matching: true vacua may lie at nontrivial holonomy even with nonzero threshold values of the Coulomb-slice D-coefficient, and despite vanishing D-coefficient at trivial holonomy.

Magnetic null points define the magnetic field topology in stellar coronae, planetary magnetospheres, and astrospheres, and are commonly associated with magnetic reconnection. We investigate the local structure of stationary three-dimensional (3D) resistive, incompressible magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) flows in the vicinity of an isolated magnetic null and an isolated velocity stagnation point, assuming that the two nulls are colocated. The governing MHD equations are solved self-consistently using a first-order local expansion of the magnetic and velocity fields about the null. For any given magnetic-null configuration, the equations admit a two-parameter family of stationary solutions, comprising multiple solution branches with distinct velocity topologies. Along these families, the velocity-null polarity can change at bifurcation points where the flow becomes effectively 2.5D. A single magnetic null can therefore support positive-radial, negative-radial, and spiral stagnation flows, with qualitatively different inflow, outflow, and rotational properties. The associated pressure and resistivity distributions are likewise determined self-consistently and vary across the solution family, demonstrating that they are coupled to the flow topology rather than prescribed independently. The generic solutions do not require the velocity and magnetic separatrices to coincide, allowing plasma to cross the magnetic fan separatrix and providing a local reconnective flow. These results demonstrate that, in the presence of nonuniform resistivity, the magnetic topology alone does not uniquely determine the associated stationary flow, pressure, or resistivity structure around a 3D magnetic null, revealing the nonlinear feedback between all macroscopic variables and resistivity. The constant-resistivity field-aligned and spiral field-crossing solutions identified previously are recovered as special cases.

We present the design methodology, implementation, and data products of the Energetic Particle Detector (EPD) instrument on-board the Electron Losses and Fields Investigation (ELFIN) dual SmallSat mission. ELFIN was designed to study the time-evolution of energetic particle distributions in Earth's magnetosphere, having operated in polar Low Earth Orbit (LEO) from 2018 to 2022. Each satellite's instrument payload contained a pair of detector heads to sense differential-directional fluxes of 50 keV to $\sim$5 MeV electrons (EPDE), and 50 keV to $\sim$2 MeV atomic ions (EPDI), with an energy resolution better than 50% ($ΔE/E<0.5$). The eROSITA telescope array aboard the joint German--Russian Spektrum-Roentgen-Gamma (SRG) mission delivered the deepest soft all-sky X-ray survey to date, scanning the entire sky more than four times between December 2019 and February 2022. The potential of the survey remains, however, hampered by the fact that only data for the half of the sky owned by the German consortium have been released publicly, whereas the other half remains accessible only to the Russian consortium, effectively making eROSITA a "half-sky" survey for most applications. The only publicly accessible all-sky "data product" as of 2026 is a single press-release image published in June 2020 jointly by both consortia to celebrate the completion of the first all-sky survey (eRASS1). Here I present the first all-sky eROSITA catalogue based on this image, containing around 63 thousand sources with an estimated completeness of about 50% above $3\times10^{-13}$ erg cm$^{-2}$ s$^{-1}$ in the 0.2-2.3 keV band and a positional accuracy of $\sim$1 arcmin, validated against the full eRASS1 catalogue for half the sky and against several external catalogues. The catalogue is thus comparable with 2RXS, the deepest truly all-sky survey generally available, and gives a first glimpse of the eastern Galactic hemisphere with eROSITA. In this hemisphere, about 11000 detected sources have no counterpart in ROSAT or other public X-ray catalogues and are expected to be genuine new X-ray sources. Besides the catalogue, I release calibrated HEALPix maps to facilitate studies of large-scale extended emission, such as the eROSITA bubbles, or of Galactic absorption that require full sky coverage (https://zenodo.org/records/23640281), as well as the complete analysis pipeline to make the results fully reproducible (https://github.com/doroshv/eropngcat).
