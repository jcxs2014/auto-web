It is known due to a result of Miyamoto that zero mode correlators of weight $1$ vectors of vertex operator algebras are quasi-modular forms, and that their generating series are Jacobi forms. In this article we generalize this to higher weight vectors. We consider weight 2 vectors that generate what we call heavy Heisenberg algebras. As an application of our results, we sketch an argument that gives the asymptotic behavior of the dimensions of states of our VOAs.

However, we recover surface amplitudes and quantum partition functions, as well as local classical field dynamics, without the 2-bundle formalism. For global phases, topological and index invariants, and twisted-sheaf moduli, established equivalences give alternative descriptions through cocycles, bundles, operators, or modules, with the required global compatibility data. For three-dimensional finite gauge theories with cohomological actions, Fourier transformation expresses the partition function as a sum of partition functions of ordinary finite-group gauge theories. An invertible local substitution reduces invariant classical actions for a class of 2-bundle connections represented by finite-dimensional crossed modules to ordinary gauge and tensor dynamics. These finite and local reductions explicitly eliminate field variables. A four-dimensional quadratic example instead admits a direct cochain evaluation even when an ordinary gauge-theory reduction fails. The large hierarchy of fermion masses in the Standard Model has long posed a challenge to be explained. One approach is to try to invoke a symmetry principle to dictate the structure ("texture") of the matrices of fermion Yukawa couplings to the Higgs field. Here we invoke noninvertible symmetries, specifically, fusion algebras, to provide such textures. In particular, the structure of the fusion-algebra can be used to assign labels to each field, which determines how suppressed any given composite operator is as a term in the Lagrangian. In UV completions, this can be interpreted as specifying a hierarchy of operators that in the IR effective theory become the Yukawa couplings to the Higgs field. Remaining agnostic of a specific UV model, we perform a Monte Carlo numerical scan over unknown Wilson coefficients, and we identify the rank-8 fusion algebra $\rm{FR}^{8,6}_8$ as the one providing the best fit for the textures of the up-quark, down-quark and charged-lepton Yukawa matrices. We also study the predictions for the CKM and PMNS mixing matrices, with the conclusion that none of the fusion algebras we study provide an accurate fit for the masses and mixing angles simultaneously. The fusion algebra structure ensures that the textures that we find are stable under RG running and persist down to low energies.

Magnetic null points define the magnetic field topology in stellar coronae, planetary magnetospheres, and astrospheres, and are commonly associated with magnetic reconnection. We investigate the local structure of stationary three-dimensional (3D) resistive, incompressible magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) flows in the vicinity of an isolated magnetic null and an isolated velocity stagnation point, assuming that the two nulls are colocated. The governing MHD equations are solved self-consistently using a first-order local expansion of the magnetic and velocity fields about the null. For any given magnetic-null configuration, the equations admit a two-parameter family of stationary solutions, comprising multiple solution branches with distinct velocity topologies. Along these families, the velocity-null polarity can change at bifurcation points where the flow becomes effectively 2.5D. A single magnetic null can therefore support positive-radial, negative-radial, and spiral stagnation flows, with qualitatively different inflow, outflow, and rotational properties. The associated pressure and resistivity distributions are likewise determined self-consistently and vary across the solution family, demonstrating that they are coupled to the flow topology rather than prescribed independently. The generic solutions do not require the velocity and magnetic separatrices to coincide, allowing plasma to cross the magnetic fan separatrix and providing a local reconnective flow. These results demonstrate that, in the presence of nonuniform resistivity, the magnetic topology alone does not uniquely determine the associated stationary flow, pressure, or resistivity structure around a 3D magnetic null, revealing the nonlinear feedback between all macroscopic variables and resistivity. The constant-resistivity field-aligned and spiral field-crossing solutions identified previously are recovered as special cases.

Although validated on laboratory data (Cibrario et al. 2025), the method's astrophysical performance had not been systematically characterized. We analyze Her X-1, a bright, polarized accreting pulsar as a benchmark, and soft-state XTE J1701-461, a bright neutron-star low-mass X-ray binary with no detected polarization, to assess residual systematics. From both pipelines' Level-2 event files, we compare normalized Stokes parameters, polarization degree (PD) and angle (PA), and detection significance. For Her X-1, hybrid reconstruction increases significance at all energies, significantly above ~2.83 keV. R = σ_hyb/σ_std differs from unity by 9.3σ in the 2.83-4.00 keV bin and reaches 1.18±0.03 at 5.66-8.00 keV (6.2σ). For XTE J1701-461, PD is consistent with zero and R with unity. Fourier analysis reveals excess power in hybrid angle distributions at low energies for modes m = 3, 6, 12 and 15, significant at >7σ relative to the standard method; at 5-8 keV, all modes are consistent with noise in both methods.
