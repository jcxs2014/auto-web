We study the mirror family $\mathcal X_λ$ of the bicubic Calabi-Yau threefold, obtained from a family of complete intersections of two cubics in $\mathbb P^5$. We show that the four-dimensional weight-three Hodge structure of the fibers over $λ^6 = -8$ splits into two rational rank-two pieces of Hodge types $(1,0,0,1)$ and $(0,1,1,0)$; in particular, these points are rank-two attractor points. Using a Hesse pencil fiber-product realization, equivariant point counting, and the Serre-Faltings-Livne method, we identify their semisimplified $\ell$-adic Galois representations with those attached to the weight-four newform $f_{\mathsf{54.4.a.c}}$ and the Tate twist of the weight-two newform $f_{\mathsf{54.2.a.a}}$, where these labels refer to the L-functions and modular forms database.

Superradiant instabilities around rotating black holes can produce clouds of ultralight bosons bound by the black hole's gravitational field. A binary companion can ionize these clouds by driving transitions into continuum states in the black hole's 1/r gravitational potential, extracting energy and angular momentum from the orbit. We investigate how a companion-sourced Yukawa interaction modifies this process. At weak coupling, the interference term between the gravitational and Yukawa transition amplitudes provides the leading correction to the ionization power. For the orbital frequencies here, an attractive interaction enhances continuum ionization and accelerates the inspiral, whereas a repulsive interaction has the opposite effect. At larger coupling, the pure Yukawa contribution to the ionization power becomes increasingly important and can compete with the gravitational contribution, particularly for lighter mediators. Comparing continuum ionization with off-resonant bound-state mixing, we find that continuum ionization can halt superradiant growth at a substantially smaller Yukawa coupling. At their respective critical couplings, the Yukawa interaction provides the dominant contribution to cloud depletion in both channels. Finally, we calculate the phase correction to gravitational waves due to continuum ionization and characterize how the chirp-rate response varies with frequency near the thresholds at which successive orbital harmonics open continuum-ionization channels.

We analyze more than a decade of public Alpha Magnetic Spectrometer (AMS-02) data (2011-2021), matching daily electron and positron spectra to the orbital record on 3268 common dates. For each sky window we weight the spectra by the time the orbital zenith spends there and divide each rigidity bin by its all-date mean. At 16.6-22.8 GV, the normalized positron flux dips below its neighboring bins in several projections. At the Galactic center and the Crab, the positron dip exceeds the electron dip by $3.04\pm1.19$ and $3.57\pm1.22$ percentage points, respectively (statistical errors). A scan centered on 1177 Fermi-LAT catalog sources also finds dips at PKS~0301$-$243 and PKS~1424+240. At fixed rigidity, 13.8% of trials that shift the spectra relative to the orbit produce a dip somewhere in the catalog at least as deep as the observed maximum. An isotropic intensity with the measured daily spectra reproduces every projection exactly. In a zenith pencil-beam model, direct positron annihilation on a common cold-electron column at the Galactic center and the Crab produces less than 0.10 percentage points of dip depth in either projection.

We investigate the chemical-potential dependence of hadron masses in two-color QCD (QC$_2$D) at low temperature using lattice Monte Carlo simulations. We calculate all possible meson and diquark correlation functions, including disconnected diagrams, on a $32^4$ lattice at $T\approx40\,\mathrm{MeV}$. In the hadronic phase, we obtain a meson mass hierarchy similar to that of three-color QCD. After the superfluid transition, the ordering changes: the $σ$ and $a_1$ channels become lighter, while the pion mass increases with density. We also examine the diquark source dependence of the Nambu--Goldstone mode of $U(1)_B$ symmetry breaking and find an almost vanishing mass in the superfluid phase after extrapolation to zero source. Finally, we compare correlation functions for chiral partners and discuss indications of partial chiral-symmetry restoration. This contribution is based on our recent study of hadron spectra in dense QC$_2$D~\cite{Iida:2026dmw}.

In this paper, we have generalized Poisson's(1993) calculation for the case of gravitational radiation from a particle in a circular orbit around a black hole to the case of the wormhole. Considering the motion of a test particle around a Schwarzschild like wormhole and using Teukolsky formalism, we have obtained an analytical result of flux at infinity under the assumption of small velocities. Focusing on the 22-mode, we have found a novel 0.5PN order term beyond the quadrupole order, which vanishes in the black hole limit. On the other hand, all higher order terms are getting modified in such a way that they give correct correspondence in the black hole limit. We have further corroborated our results with the numerical investigations, which show that the two waveforms match well when the particle lies far from ISCO. Thus, our work provides one of the first attempts toward the study of the post-Newtonian waveforms involving wormholes (ECOs).

We investigate quantum gate teleportation as a primitive for satellite-enabled distributed quantum computing. In the considered architecture, a satellite distributes entangled photon pairs to two optical ground stations, which transfer the shared quantum resource to remote quantum processing units. This entanglement is then used to implement a nonlocal controlled-NOT gate between two circuit qubits located in different processors. We formulate the minimal four-qubit protocol, consisting of two network qubits that provide the entangled resource and two circuit qubits on which the distributed gate is performed. Atmospheric propagation effects in the gravitational field are modeled through an effective two-qubit depolarizing channel whose parameters depend on atmospheric and optical quantities, including the Fried parameter and receiver aperture. The performance of the protocol is quantified by the fidelity between the ideal CNOT output state and the state produced by noisy quantum gate teleportation. Our results show that the fidelity is governed by the quality of the distributed entanglement and depends jointly on atmospheric coherence, aperture size, and effective depolarization strength. These findings connect physical-layer modeling of satellite optical channels with gate-level performance in distributed quantum computing.

We develop a gauge-independent theory of magnetic reconnection beyond the standard special-relativistic framework of inertial systems in flat spacetime. Our definitions of reconnection and its rate are independent of reference frame and coordinate system, allowing physical effects of gravity and net linear acceleration to be distinguished from coordinate effects. We extend the mesoscale theory of Liu et al. [PRL 118, 085101 (2017)], in which the reconnection rate depends on physics both within the diffusion region and in an upstream ideal, force-free region. The theory identifies three non-inertial and curved-spacetime (NICS) effects, including gravitationally induced magnetic tension: gravitational distortion of the reconnection plane changes the curvature of magnetic field lines. Applying the theory to a simplified accretion plasma near )a compact object, we find that (1) whether gravity increases or decreases the reconnection rate depends on the system's orientation relative to the gravitational source, with the greatest increase occurring when the outflow jet points toward or away from the source; and (2) the magnitude of the gravitational effect scales with the dimensionless parameter $\barκ \equiv r_sΔy^2/r^3$, where $r$ is the Schwarzschild radial coordinate, $r_s$ is the source's Schwarzschild radius, and $Δy$ is the mesoscale length.

Energetic-particle irradiation can produce persistent charge separation within lunar regolith, but the internal fields predicted by subsurface-charging models depend on how particle interactions are translated into retained charge. This critical review examines the evidence and assumptions connecting microscopic charge formation to macroscopic charging. We distinguish energy deposition, net charge transfer across a specified boundary, and the spatial distribution of signed excess charge. We organize these distinctions using an average retained-charge response defined for specified material, irradiation, electrical-state, and evaluation conditions. Its use as a volumetric source requires consistent charge accounting, compatible incident-flux definitions, and an adequate separation between response formation and subsequent transport. Applying these criteria to existing models, emission studies, and silicate irradiation experiments yields three assessments. First, emission measurements constrain electron loss across the measured boundary but do not locate internally redistributed charge. Second, evidence of internal reabsorption does not by itself establish that the charge separation sustaining subsurface fields is reduced; an illustrative calculation shows that the same net charge supply can produce different peak fields depending on transfer depths.
