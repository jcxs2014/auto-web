We describe FOREST, a real, first order digital resonant filter and orthogonal state generator. We show that in spite of the imaginary nature of the roots of the transfer function of a resonator, appropriate interpretation of this transfer function nonetheless leads to a practical first order filter with real coefficients. We use a discrete state space model of FOREST to predict the resonator properties, and implement the filter in digital signal processing slices on a digital signal processing platform. Applications are in real time digital signal processing, engineering fields such as mains electricity monitoring and motor control, any application requiring a phase locked loop, and physics applications utilising high-quality-factor resonators.

In this pedagogical article we discuss the Dirac equation in arbitrary coordinate systems. We explain the need for connections when dealing with spinor fields and present the practical steps needed to compute the Dirac operator in a general setting, allowing for arbitrary coordinate systems and position-dependent orthonormal gamma matrices, both of which can simplify computations in certain scenarios. The extension to include minimally coupled gravity is straightforward. Examples are provided, including constructing the Dirac equation in spherical and cylindrical symmetry (with or without gravity) and the Kerr spacetime. We also briefly comment on the advantages and disadvantages of various tetrads and orthonormal gamma matrices. This manuscript should be accessible to readers who have studied the Dirac equation at an introductory level (e.g., in Cartesian coordinates in flat spacetime with constant gamma matrices). The emphasis is on the use of orthonormal frames, since these correspond to the coordinate systems most familiar to readers when dealing with most field theories, and represent the physical frames where measurements are performed. The aim is to get the reader quickly to the point of being able to do calculations, so the presentation favors a concise, pragmatic approach over extended derivations.

We construct boundary conditions for AdS$_3$ gravity that realize Sine-Gordon dynamics. For a periodic soliton lattice at fixed winding, we relate the angular holonomy to the exact acoustic dispersion by continuing the Bloch momentum through the zero-frequency band edge associated with rigid translation of the lattice. For suitable embedding angles, the stable vacuum has a regular BTZ geometry. When only one chiral sector carries winding, we construct a family whose asymptotic region extends to a BTZ black hole. As the spectral parameter of the winding sector tends to the corresponding band edge, the black hole approaches extremality. The leading correction to the extremal entropy is linear in the Hawking temperature defined with respect to laboratory time, with a coefficient inversely proportional to the sound velocity of the lattice. The same inverse-velocity dependence appears in the low-temperature entropy of one-dimensional quantum fluids.

Schwinger-Dyson equations are a cornerstone of quantum field theory (QFT). Derived using integration by parts, they capture relations between correlators of different orders, forming an open, infinite-dimensional system. In zero-dimensional QFTs with polynomial actions, which is just a physical interpretation for multivariate Gaussian integrals with polynomial insertions, conventional Gröbner elimination can be used to express all higher-order correlators (moments) in terms of a fixed basis set. One consequence is a closed finite-dimensional system of differential equations for the coupling dependence of these `master' correlators. An important application of zero-dimensional QFTs is in counting discrete geometric structures (as in simplicial approaches to quantum gravity). For the problem of counting labelled regular graphs, applying this Schwinger-Dyson closure results in a computational breakthrough relative to the existing state-of-the-art: for 9- and 10-regular graphs on $n$ vertices, a finite-depth, finite-width recursive formulation is obtained, making it possible to compute the total graph counts in $O(n)$ arithmetic operations.

We study the kink and particle spectra for the massive $ϕ_{2,1}$-deformations of minimal models $\mathcal M_{k+1,k+2}$ via truncated conformal space approach (TCSA) on a defect circle. The massive $ϕ_{2,1}$-deformation preserves a set of non-invertible symmetries, furnishing a fusion category $\mathcal C_k\simeq {\rm SO}(3)_k$. These non-invertible symmetries are spontaneously broken, and thus label the vacua and organize the kinks and particles into their multiplets in terms of quiver diagrams. We employ a finite-volume truncated Hamiltonian approach on defect Hilbert spaces, $\mathcal H_{X_i}$ for $X_i\in\mathcal C_k$, to study these quivers in the IR theories. The symmetry defect can then close an individual kink on a circle. We first establish the relation between states of definite fractional momentum on the defect circle and physical kink and particle states labelled by their vacuum pairs. Using form factors of local fields, we independently measure the squared amplitudes between the states of the two bases in defect TCSA. Combining these measurements with defect TCSA spectra, we reconstruct the quiver diagrams for $k=3,4,5$ without assuming them in advance. In particular, for $k=4$ we reconstruct the distinct quivers of the ordered and disordered phases. For $k=5$, we identify the quivers of both the light and heavy multiplets.

In this paper, we have generalized Poisson's(1993) calculation for the case of gravitational radiation from a particle in a circular orbit around a black hole to the case of the wormhole. Considering the motion of a test particle around a Schwarzschild like wormhole and using Teukolsky formalism, we have obtained an analytical result of flux at infinity under the assumption of small velocities. On the other hand, all higher order terms are getting modified in such a way that they give correct correspondence in the black hole limit. We have further corroborated our results with the numerical investigations, which show that the two waveforms match well when the particle lies far from ISCO. Thus, our work provides one of the first attempts toward the study of the post-Newtonian waveforms involving wormholes (ECOs).

We investigate quantum gate teleportation as a primitive for satellite-enabled distributed quantum computing. In the considered architecture, a satellite distributes entangled photon pairs to two optical ground stations, which transfer the shared quantum resource to remote quantum processing units. This entanglement is then used to implement a nonlocal controlled-NOT gate between two circuit qubits located in different processors. We formulate the minimal four-qubit protocol, consisting of two network qubits that provide the entangled resource and two circuit qubits on which the distributed gate is performed. Atmospheric propagation effects in the gravitational field are modeled through an effective two-qubit depolarizing channel whose parameters depend on atmospheric and optical quantities, including the Fried parameter and receiver aperture. The performance of the protocol is quantified by the fidelity between the ideal CNOT output state and the state produced by noisy quantum gate teleportation. Our results show that the fidelity is governed by the quality of the distributed entanglement and depends jointly on atmospheric coherence, aperture size, and effective depolarization strength. These findings connect physical-layer modeling of satellite optical channels with gate-level performance in distributed quantum computing.

We develop a gauge-independent theory of magnetic reconnection beyond the standard special-relativistic framework of inertial systems in flat spacetime. Our definitions of reconnection and its rate are independent of reference frame and coordinate system, allowing physical effects of gravity and net linear acceleration to be distinguished from coordinate effects. We extend the mesoscale theory of Liu et al. [PRL 118, 085101 (2017)], in which the reconnection rate depends on physics both within the diffusion region and in an upstream ideal, force-free region. The theory identifies three non-inertial and curved-spacetime (NICS) effects, including gravitationally induced magnetic tension: gravitational distortion of the reconnection plane changes the curvature of magnetic field lines. Applying the theory to a simplified accretion plasma near )a compact object, we find that (1) whether gravity increases or decreases the reconnection rate depends on the system's orientation relative to the gravitational source, with the greatest increase occurring when the outflow jet points toward or away from the source; and (2) the magnitude of the gravitational effect scales with the dimensionless parameter $\barκ \equiv r_sΔy^2/r^3$, where $r$ is the Schwarzschild radial coordinate, $r_s$ is the source's Schwarzschild radius, and $Δy$ is the mesoscale length.

Energetic-particle irradiation can produce persistent charge separation within lunar regolith, but the internal fields predicted by subsurface-charging models depend on how particle interactions are translated into retained charge. This critical review examines the evidence and assumptions connecting microscopic charge formation to macroscopic charging. We distinguish energy deposition, net charge transfer across a specified boundary, and the spatial distribution of signed excess charge. We organize these distinctions using an average retained-charge response defined for specified material, irradiation, electrical-state, and evaluation conditions. Its use as a volumetric source requires consistent charge accounting, compatible incident-flux definitions, and an adequate separation between response formation and subsequent transport. Applying these criteria to existing models, emission studies, and silicate irradiation experiments yields three assessments. First, emission measurements constrain electron loss across the measured boundary but do not locate internally redistributed charge. Second, evidence of internal reabsorption does not by itself establish that the charge separation sustaining subsurface fields is reduced; an illustrative calculation shows that the same net charge supply can produce different peak fields depending on transfer depths. Third, laboratory electrical damage in silicate crystals does not determine breakdown rates in cryogenic, porous regolith, which additionally require constraints on local fields, failure properties, and discharge recurrence. Joint constraints on boundary charge transfer, spatial charge separation, and post-irradiation relaxation are central to evaluating lunar subsurface-charging predictions.

Collisionless shocks are of fundamental interest in astrophysics and space physics as important sources of energetic particles. Downstream of quasi-parallel shocks, high-speed jets (HSJs) and magnetic reconnection have been frequently observed, while it remains unclear how their coupling shapes structured regions for particle energization. We use two-dimensional full particle-in-cell simulations to investigate HSJ generation, thin current-sheet formation, and associated electron energization downstream of a parallel shock. We find that portions of foreshock ultralow-frequency (ULF) waves with enhanced dynamic pressure interact with a self-reforming shock front and are transmitted downstream, forming HSJs, while neighboring low-pressure portions evolve into cavities. At the leading edge of the HSJs, the unique bent magnetic topology carried by the compressed ULF waves produces bow waves and locally squeezes out thin current sheets. These current sheets show reconnection signatures, including X-point topology, magnetic islands, enhanced J · E ,′ , , and Alfvénic outflows along the local reconnecting direction. In the reconnection region associated with HSJs, the parallel electric field, betatron, and Fermi terms all contribute to adiabatic electron energization. The betatron term appears as a visible signature near the X line, where the local magnetic structure is compressed by the HSJ. For the first time, we report a systematic mass-ratio dependence of HSJ morphology in full particle-in-cell (PIC) simulations: lower mass ratios produce fragmented HSJs with shorter lifetimes, whereas larger mass ratios yield longer and wider HSJs with clearer bow waves as ion inertia increases.

A proposed resolution of the black-hole information paradox must explain which information an outside observer can recover from escaping radiation\cite{Hawking,Page,Hayden,Almheiri}. Environmental memory can return correlations to the source without delivering them to that observer\cite{ChenQi,ChenMemory}. Here we establish a sharp recovery frontier at fixed mean radiation yield for single-excitation channels and realize its qubit limit with an autonomous quantum-memory emitter. Unequal transmissions balance retained coherence against information in failed collection events. A reference--radiation correlation test distinguishes this gain even when the mean collected energy is unchanged. Identical transmission channels instead give the same recovery at matched collection, regardless of their emission timescales. For a broader class of interacting sources, an ordering of collected and inaccessible coupling spectra forbids unassisted quantum transmission irrespective of internal memory. A separate coding example supports a positive rate where uniform transmission at matched actual yield has zero capacity. Together, these results identify what openness must change to assist recovery: the accessible transmission structure. They supply a physically constrained comparison of evaporation mechanisms, separating information delivered to radiation from coherence retained elsewhere or an increase in the collected output.

The properties of its host galaxy also bear remarkable similarities to the parent galaxies of known off-nuclear TDEs. Type Ibn supernovae (SNe) are rare interacting transients. The dense, helium-rich circumstellar material (CSM) surrounding these events provides insight into the final stages of massive-star evolution. The geometries of the ejecta and CSM provide complementary constraints on the explosion and pre-SN mass loss, yet their temporal evolution has remained largely unexplored by spectropolarimetry. Here we present the first spectropolarimetric time series spanning the interaction-to-ejecta transition of the Type Ibn SN 2025kzr. Three epochs of Very Large Telescope observations, combined with two epochs of published $V$-band imaging polarimetry, span 5--34 days after explosion. Continuum polarization is low at the earliest epoch, then rises to $\sim$0.6% by day 11 as the SN ejecta sweep up the dense CSM. This rise, together with a rotating polarization position angle, indicates a significant change in the geometry of the emitting region. The polarization subsequently falls below our detection limit as the photosphere recedes into the ejecta. Radiation-hydrodynamic and Monte Carlo polarization models illustrate that this evolution can be reproduced by an oblate CSM and prolate ejecta, with the symmetry axis of the cold dense shell tilted relative to that of the CSM. Binary mass transfer can produce an equatorially enhanced CSM, although the polarization data alone cannot identify the progenitor's formation channel. Similar polarization signatures in other supernovae suggest that misaligned ejecta and CSM may be common, making time-resolved spectropolarimetry a powerful probe of the link between pre-explosion mass loss and explosion geometry.

Fast radio bursts (FRBs) are luminous millisecond-duration radio transients of extragalactic origin, yet their progenitor systems remain unidentified. The redshift evolution of the FRB event rate provides a powerful, model-independent discriminant between young progenitors and old progenitors, but recent studies have reached conflicting conclusions. In this work, we perform a forward-modeling analysis of the apparent non-repeating FRB sample from the CHIME/FRB catalogs to constrain the intrinsic FRB population. We find that the FRB rate follows the star formation rate (SFR) with little time delay when both dispersion measure (DM) and fluence are considered. In contrast, when only DM is taken into account, a significant time delay ($3 \sim 4$ Gyr) exists between the FRB rate and the SFR. The main reason for this discrepancy is that systematic biases exist in the mapping process from burst fluence to signal-to-noise ratio. These results support that at least a fraction of FRBs arise from a progenitor population whose event rate is delayed relative to star formation.

Self-interacting neutrino gases exhibit collective behavior, in which additional incoherent collisions can induce collisional neutrino-flavor instability. We investigate the effects of energy dependence, anisotropy, and spatial inhomogeneity on the resulting flavor conversion through linear stability analysis and nonlinear quantum kinetic simulations. We find contrasting wavenumber dependences for the near/gapless and far/gapped branches, with maximum growth rates at collision-scale shifted wavenumbers and at collective-scale wavenumbers, respectively. Their eigenvectors reveal distinct energy and angular structures, which are reflected in the subsequent establishment of flavor swap and equipartition. The nonlinear evolution also features secondary flavor growth associated with changes in the stability of collective modes as the neutrino background evolves. Despite the spreading of flavor coherence across spatial scales due to nonlinear mode coupling, collisional decoherence tends to limit the development of spatial inhomogeneities, leaving the neutrino populations nearly homogeneous. These findings represent a step towards understanding and predicting the nonlinear flavor evolution of dense neutrino gases interacting with their surroundings, particularly in explosive astrophysical environments.

We present a broadband spectral and timing study of the bright atoll neutron star low-mass X-ray binary GX~9+9, using simultaneous NICER, NuSTAR, and MAXI observations from 2019 and 2022. The broadband spectra are well described by a combination of multicolor disk emission, thermal Comptonization of blackbody seed photons from the neutron star surface or boundary layer, and associated disk reflection. We report a clear detection of reflection features in this source, including a moderately broad Fe K$α$ complex and a reflection hump, confirming an earlier detection from a different observation. These features likely arise from the illumination of the moderately ionized disk by thermally Comptonized emission from the boundary layer or warm corona. While the source remained in the soft spectral state during both observations, the 2022 observation exhibits a relatively larger thermal contribution, together with a steeper Comptonized component and lower coronal optical depth. Both reflection and disk-continuum modeling favor a truncated disk, with the latter indicating a smaller inner disk radius in 2022. The inferred radial extent of the boundary layer of $\sim8~r_{\rm g}$ may play a role in the observed disk truncation. Assuming that the disk is truncated at the magnetospheric radius, we obtain an upper limit on the magnetic field strength of $\lesssim3\times10^{10}$ G. Timing analysis reveals a relatively stable disk component and a highly variable Comptonized component, consistent with the typical characteristics of the soft state.

Astrophysical systems provide powerful probes of dark matter interactions, complementing direct-detection searches. We propose using accretion flows of the supermassive black hole as a bolometer for light DM. The kinetic energy of sub-MeV dark matter in the black hole gravitational potential can remain below the thermal energy of the plasma electrons. The colder dark matter population can therefore absorb energy through elastic scattering, cooling the electrons and modifying their emission spectrum. We integrate dark matter cooling into the advection-dominated accretion flow around M87$^{*}$ and solve the full set of thermodynamic equations to obtain the resulting temperature profile and radio-to-millimeter emission. By comparing the predicted spectrum with observations accompanying the Event Horizon Telescope campaign, we use, for the first time, the thermal energy balance of the hot accretion flow to constrain DM-electron scattering, obtaining stringent limits on keV-to-MeV DM for both heavy and light mediators.
