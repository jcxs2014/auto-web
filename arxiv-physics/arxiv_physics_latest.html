We study the linear energetic stability of non-Abelian vortices in an $SO(3)$ gauge-Higgs theory with residual gauge group $A_4$. In our previous work, we identified two families of such vortices associated with order-two and order-three elements of $A_4$, and found Bogomol'nyi--Prasad--Sommerfield-like behavior at special ratios of the Higgs and gauge-boson mass scales. Here, we investigate their stability against $SO(3)$ gauge and Higgs perturbations by analyzing the second order variation of the vortex tension using analytical and numerical methods. In particular, when all relevant mass scales are equal, we prove linear stability for all vortices in the order-three family. By contrast, at the same mass point, we prove that a vortex in the order-two family is unstable. Thus, although both vortex families exhibit Bogomol'nyi--Prasad--Sommerfield-like behavior in their reduced equations at this mass point, their stability properties in the full $SO(3)$ theory are qualitatively different.

We study the relation between the exact-WKB Delabaere--Dillinger--Pham Stokes jumps and the multi-instanton expansion of a one-dimensional potential with $N$ equal-depth minima. For a single barrier this relation is the Bogomolny--Zinn-Justin cancellation between the instanton--anti-instanton ambiguity and the Borel ambiguity of perturbation theory, and we extend it to every consecutive barrier interval and to the integration contours themselves. In the dilute large-$T$ regime at fixed nonperturbative grade, we derive the quasi-zero mode integral over the instanton separations from the Euclidean path integral and reduce it to Gamma and Hankel periods. With the local Weber level function of each well and one normalization per well fixed by the local Weber connection matrix, these periods reproduce the two lateral connected exact-WKB coefficients. The two lateral contours of each instanton--anti-instanton separation differ by exactly one Hankel cycle, and this linear Picard--Lefschetz relation reproduces the Stokes jump of the connected coefficient. A numerical solution of the Schrödinger equation for a symmetric triple well confirms the resulting two-barrier level splitting. At higher fixed grade, the map from ordered event sequences to normalized scalar coefficient products is not one-to-one. At leading dilute order the scalar weight of a closed event sequence depends only on the numbers of barrier crossings, $\mathcal I\bar{\mathcal I}$ pairs, and $\mathcal I\mathcal I$/$\bar{\mathcal I}\bar{\mathcal I}$ pairs. We construct two inequivalent grade-six triple-well event sequences with the same product of local Weber amplitudes, which embed in every potential with $N\ge3$ wells. Our results hold within the regular dilute, one-loop, local Weber approximation.

Kemp and Ramgoolam proposed a basis of monomials for a commutative semisimple algebra with generators \(C_1,\ldots, C_L\), constructed from a degeneracy graph which records how the eigenspaces of \( C_1\) are successively split by \( C_2,\ldots, C_L\). They showed that the basis property is equivalent to the non-vanishing of the determinant of the matrix of values of these monomials on the projector basis, and conjectured a factorised formula for this determinant, as a product of powers of eigenvalue differences. We prove this formula for any number of generators. The proof uses graph analogues of Newton polynomials. Their evaluation matrix is not triangular in general, but expanding the rows of the children of each node of the penultimate layer in a Newton basis of their eigenvalues makes it block triangular, with diagonal blocks given by smaller degeneracy graphs in which the final layer is removed. The formula then follows by induction on the number of layers. We show that the exponents in the determinant are overlap volumes of higher-dimensional Young diagrams attached to the nodes of the graph: intervals, ordinary Young diagrams, plane partitions and their higher analogues. Finally, the block triangular structure gives an explicit recursive inversion of the evaluation matrix, expressing the projectors in the monomial basis.

A Lagrangian submanifold in an almost Calabi-Yau manifold is called positive if the real part of the holomorphic volume form restricted to it is positive. It is called $ε$-positive if the ratio of the restrictions of the imaginary and real parts of the holomorphic volume form is bounded by $\frac{1}ε.$ An exact isotopy class of positive Lagrangian submanifolds admits a natural Riemannian metric. We prove that the Riemannian metric restricted to the subset of $ε$-positive Lagrangians induces a path metric when the relative Kähler class of the ambient Calabi-Yau manifold lies on a line of rational slope. The proof relies on the construction of a sequence of quantization maps from a circle bundle over the space of positive Lagrangians to the dual of the space of holomorphic sections of powers of a prequantum line bundle. The rationality condition for the relative Kähler class allows us to reduce to the case of Bohr-Sommerfeld Lagrangians. Building on ideas of Tian and Nirenberg-Wells, Hörmander estimates are used to show that functions on a Bohr-Sommerfeld Lagrangian can be approximated by holomorphic sections of a sufficiently high power of the prequantum line bundle. Consequently, the quantization maps eventually separate fibers of the circle bundle.

Magnetic null points define the magnetic field topology in stellar coronae, planetary magnetospheres, and astrospheres, and are commonly associated with magnetic reconnection. We investigate the local structure of stationary three-dimensional (3D) resistive, incompressible magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) flows in the vicinity of an isolated magnetic null and an isolated velocity stagnation point, assuming that the two nulls are colocated. The governing MHD equations are solved self-consistently using a first-order local expansion of the magnetic and velocity fields about the null. For any given magnetic-null configuration, the equations admit a two-parameter family of stationary solutions, comprising multiple solution branches with distinct velocity topologies. Along these families, the velocity-null polarity can change at bifurcation points where the flow becomes effectively 2.5D. A single magnetic null can therefore support positive-radial, negative-radial, and spiral stagnation flows, with qualitatively different inflow, outflow, and rotational properties. The associated pressure and resistivity distributions are likewise determined self-consistently and vary across the solution family, demonstrating that they are coupled to the flow topology rather than prescribed independently. The generic solutions do not require the velocity and magnetic separatrices to coincide, allowing plasma to cross the magnetic fan separatrix and providing a local reconnective flow. These results demonstrate that, in the presence of nonuniform resistivity, the magnetic topology alone does not uniquely determine the associated stationary flow, pressure, or resistivity structure around a 3D magnetic null, revealing the nonlinear feedback between all macroscopic variables and resistivity. The constant-resistivity field-aligned and spiral field-crossing solutions identified previously are recovered as special cases.

We present the design methodology, implementation, and data products of the Energetic Particle Detector (EPD) instrument on-board the Electron Losses and Fields Investigation (ELFIN) dual SmallSat mission. ELFIN was designed to study the time-evolution of energetic particle distributions in Earth's magnetosphere, having operated in polar Low Earth Orbit (LEO) from 2018 to 2022. Each satellite's instrument payload contained a pair of detector heads to sense differential-directional fluxes of 50 keV to $\sim$5 MeV electrons (EPDE), and 50 keV to $\sim$2 MeV atomic ions (EPDI), with an energy resolution better than 50% ($ΔE/E<0.5$). The satellites were spinning with a nominal period of 2.8 s in a plane containing Earth's background magnetic field, with particle counting intervals subdivided into 16 consecutive spin sectors. Each EPD was paired with a three-axis Fluxgate Magnetometer (FGM) sampled at 80 Hz for local pitch-angle determination. Each detector head operated on the principle of pulse-height analysis (PHA) in solid state media, collimated by a rectangular 22.5$^\circ$ field of view and entrance aperture. The EPDE contained a stack of 6 silicon detectors (0.7 cm total thickness) with 0.15 cm$^2$ str geometric factor, with ion contamination mitigated by an aperture foil and digital coincidence logic. The EPDI contained a stack of 2 silicon detectors (0.1 cm total thickness) with 0.03 cm$^2$ str geometric factor, with electron contamination mitigated by a magnetic broom stage and digital anti-coincidence logic.
