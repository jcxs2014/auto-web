Self-supervised learning (SSL) and explicit physical inductive biases have driven major advances in jet tagging, yet they are typically pursued along separate tracks. In this work, we unify both paradigms by integrating the Lorentz-equivariant Geometric Algebra Transformer slim (L-GATr-slim) architecture with the LeJEPA framework for representation learning. Pre-training on 100 million jets from the JetClass dataset, we optimize towards a latent space, which is invariant across physics-motivated jet augmentations. We demonstrate that enforcing Lorentz symmetry in the latent space is critical for representation quality and that this can be achieved either architecturally or learned via kinematic augmentations. With a standard non-invariant Transformer backbone, LeJEPA outperforms established autoregressive, masked particle modelling, and self-distillation baselines in linear- and MLP-probe accuracy, with L-GATr-slim providing further complementary gains. Across downstream benchmarks, our pre-trained models consistently outperform baselines trained from scratch or through different pre-training objectives.

The construction of canonical basis is an important procedure in the calculation of multi-loop Feynman integrals via the canonical differential equation methods. Currently, there are various different methods for this purpose. Among them, no single method is universally applicable. Thus, obtaining a valid basis often requires repeated attempts and verification. In this paper, we present CANON, an agent-assisted framework designed to make this search-and-verification process more convenient and more efficient. In this workflow, the agent selects methods and calls the corresponding tools to revise the basis proposal or the associated differential-equation system. Independent programs then verify whether the agent's result is valid and ask the agent to retry if verification fails. This framework contains a specific state-management tool to supports long-range basis searching works which requires multiple sub-agents. Its modular architecture enables future extensions to the framework itself. In this paper, we demonstrate this framework by applying it to some two-loop and three-loop integral families.

Color Lie (super)algebras were introduced in 1978 by Rittenberg and Wyler as generalizations of ordinary Lie (super)algebras. These color structures, graded by abelian groups, are defined by brackets which satisfy generalized Jacobi identities. Only recently the relevance of color Lie (super)algebras to physical applications has been pointed out and proved. The color framework not only describes, but also provides physical signatures for two broad types of parastatistics (particles beyond ordinary bosons/fermions): i) paraparticles which are exchanged via the permutation group and can exist in any space dimension D and ii) anyons (exchanged via the braid group), which exist up to D=2 space dimensions. This paper presents an update of the recent advancements which motivate a brand new "colored look" at questions involving parastatistics and braid statistics.

The Knizhnik--Polyakov--Zamolodchikov (KPZ) relation allows scaling exponents of two-dimensional (2D) critical systems to be derived from their counterparts in Liouville quantum gravity. Krylov and spread complexity map quantum dynamics onto a quantum walk on a half-line whose hopping amplitudes are the Lanczos coefficients, so that Krylov dynamics is the theory of orthogonal polynomials and Jacobi operators. We use this correspondence to study Krylov chains generated by the two-parameter $(p,q)$-deformed oscillator, which interpolates between the Heisenberg--Weyl chain, the $q$-oscillator chain of double-scaled SYK, and chains with exponentially growing Lanczos coefficients. For the deformed displacement chain the spread complexity is the mean excitation number, its rate is the deformed momentum quadrature, and its acceleration is $\ddot C_K=2ω^2\langle[a,a^\dagger]\rangle$, so the deformed commutator acts as a force along the Krylov chain and the mean deformation controls early-time growth. The deformation parameters separate four regimes. For $\max(p,q)<1$ the complexity stays bounded, a localization produced by the algebra alone. For $p=1$, $q<1$ it grows ballistically, with an exact asymptotic velocity given by a theta-type series. At $p=q=1$ it grows as $t^2$. For $\max(p,q)>1$ the Jacobi operator is not essentially self-adjoint, the moment problem is indeterminate, and the Lanczos coefficients no longer determine the dynamics. Even and odd truncations converge to two different evolutions, the two reflection-symmetric N-extremal solutions of the moment problem, the odd one carrying a zero-energy atom of closed-form weight; the self-adjoint extensions yield a family of complexity curves that coincide at early times and separate once the Krylov wavefront reaches infinity. Krylov complexity in this regime is thus defined only once a boundary condition at infinite Krylov index is specified. The orthogonal-polynomial procedure and its checks on exactly solvable chains are given in the appendices.

We investigate quantum gate teleportation as a primitive for satellite-enabled distributed quantum computing. In the considered architecture, a satellite distributes entangled photon pairs to two optical ground stations, which transfer the shared quantum resource to remote quantum processing units. This entanglement is then used to implement a nonlocal controlled-NOT gate between two circuit qubits located in different processors. We formulate the minimal four-qubit protocol, consisting of two network qubits that provide the entangled resource and two circuit qubits on which the distributed gate is performed. Atmospheric propagation effects in the gravitational field are modeled through an effective two-qubit depolarizing channel whose parameters depend on atmospheric and optical quantities, including the Fried parameter and receiver aperture. The performance of the protocol is quantified by the fidelity between the ideal CNOT output state and the state produced by noisy quantum gate teleportation. Our results show that the fidelity is governed by the quality of the distributed entanglement and depends jointly on atmospheric coherence, aperture size, and effective depolarization strength. These findings connect physical-layer modeling of satellite optical channels with gate-level performance in distributed quantum computing.

We develop a gauge-independent theory of magnetic reconnection beyond the standard special-relativistic framework of inertial systems in flat spacetime. Our definitions of reconnection and its rate are independent of reference frame and coordinate system, allowing physical effects of gravity and net linear acceleration to be distinguished from coordinate effects. We extend the mesoscale theory of Liu et al. [PRL 118, 085101 (2017)], in which the reconnection rate depends on physics both within the diffusion region and in an upstream ideal, force-free region.
