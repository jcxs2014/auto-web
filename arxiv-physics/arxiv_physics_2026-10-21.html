We extend the holographic test of entanglement cohomology from $d=2$ to higher-dimensional conformal field theories. For a $d$-dimensional CFT with a spherical entangling region $A$, the vacuum modular Hamiltonian is known explicitly, and the boundary relative entropy of a primary-operator perturbation can be computed via conformal Ward identities. On the bulk side, the perturbation is dual to a scalar field in AdS$_{d+1}$, and the bulk canonical energy is computed using the JLMS relation. We carry out the computation explicitly for $d=3$ and $d=4$ and find that the boundary and bulk results agree at first order in the perturbation, with identical coefficients $κ_d Δ^2 / C_T$, where $κ_3 = π^2/12$ and $κ_4 = 3π^3/16$. The result extends the quantitative verification of the holographic dual of entanglement cohomology beyond the $d=2$ case treated in the companion paper and confirms that the modular Hamiltonian perturbation is the correct holographic image of the first entanglement cohomology class. We discuss the role of the conformal anomaly, the shape of the entanglement wedge, and an outlook on TsT-deformed backgrounds. \keywords{Entanglement entropy \and Cyclic cohomology \and von Neumann algebras \and Holography \and Modular Hamiltonian}

Ultraviolet-softened extensions of general relativity can weaken gravity at short distances, whereas a positive Källén--Lehmann spectral density forbids gravity from falling below Newtonian strength. Which property of the mediator must such a weakening sacrifice? For Lorentz-invariant Gaussian mediators coupled linearly to the conserved stress tensor, we formulate three independent interferometric conditions that jointly imply this bound: visibility is never amplified, decoherence is fixed by the energy radiated by the difference source, and no interferometer responds to a later one. At tree level, the three ghost prescriptions of quadratic gravity each violate exactly one: negative-norm ghosts amplify coherence, negative-energy ghosts decohere without positive energy transfer from the difference source, and a time-symmetric fakeon response produces advanced fringe shifts. Any universal short-distance weakening of gravity therefore certifies that its mediator violates one of these conditions, and the observables proposed to certify quantum gravity become consistency tests of its ultraviolet completion.

We study quark matter at high isospin asymmetry in the limit of vanishing baryon density, a state of matter governed by the condensation of charged pions. In the weak-coupling limit, this state of matter can be reliably described by conventional perturbative methods where a trivial ground state is assumed. However, since the condensate of charged pions generates a gap in the excitation spectrum of the quarks with a non-perturbative dependence on the strong coupling, corrections to the results from conventional perturbative methods arise. This situation is very reminiscent of the one encountered in isospin-symmetric quark matter at high baryon densities which is expected to be a color superconductor. In the present work, we calculate the thermodynamic properties of superfluid quark matter at high isospin asymmetry, zero temperature, and zero baryon density at next-to-leading order (NLO) in the coupling in the presence of a finite gap. We work in the limit of two massless quark flavors and further assume that the gap is small compared to the isospin chemical potential. Similar to the situation in color-superconducting quark matter at high baryon densities, we find that the gap-induced corrections lead to an increase of the pressure and the speed of sound above their values obtained for non-superfluid quark matter. Notably, starting from asymptotically high isospin asymmetries, these corrections lead to a systematic change of the slope of the speed of sound when the isospin asymmetry is decreased. We compare our results with recent lattice QCD and Dyson-Schwinger equation studies and find good agreement in the regimes where these methods are applicable.

We develop a systematic method to compute the subsystem fidelity and Rényi fidelity in two-dimensional conformal field theories (CFTs) from the operator product expansion of twist operators, and apply it to the subsystem quantum Bhattacharyya distance (BD) and Rényi-Bhattacharyya distance (RBD). We validate the method against exact results in the free massless fermion and boson theories, and then use it to compute the BD and RBD in holographic CFTs between two heavy primary states, between two thermal states, and between a heavy primary state and a thermal state. When the two states differ in their classical, order-$c$ data, the distances are of order $c$, and the states are distinguishable already at the level of classical gravity; when the leading data coincide, the distances are of order $1/c$ at every order of the short interval expansion, so that the distinguishability becomes a perturbative quantum gravity effect. For a heavy primary state and its thermal counterpart, the leading contribution to the BD appears at order $\ell^8$ and is of order $c^0$, whereas the RBD is generically of order $c$, in line with the earlier finding that a heavy primary state and a thermal state share the same order-$c$ entanglement entropy but have different order-$c$ Rényi entropies in the short interval expansion.

Magnetic null points define the magnetic field topology in stellar coronae, planetary magnetospheres, and astrospheres, and are commonly associated with magnetic reconnection. We investigate the local structure of stationary three-dimensional (3D) resistive, incompressible magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) flows in the vicinity of an isolated magnetic null and an isolated velocity stagnation point, assuming that the two nulls are colocated. The governing MHD equations are solved self-consistently using a first-order local expansion of the magnetic and velocity fields about the null. For any given magnetic-null configuration, the equations admit a two-parameter family of stationary solutions, comprising multiple solution branches with distinct velocity topologies. Along these families, the velocity-null polarity can change at bifurcation points where the flow becomes effectively 2.5D. A single magnetic null can therefore support positive-radial, negative-radial, and spiral stagnation flows, with qualitatively different inflow, outflow, and rotational properties. The associated pressure and resistivity distributions are likewise determined self-consistently and vary across the solution family, demonstrating that they are coupled to the flow topology rather than prescribed independently. The generic solutions do not require the velocity and magnetic separatrices to coincide, allowing plasma to cross the magnetic fan separatrix and providing a local reconnective flow. These results demonstrate that, in the presence of nonuniform resistivity, the magnetic topology alone does not uniquely determine the associated stationary flow, pressure, or resistivity structure around a 3D magnetic null, revealing the nonlinear feedback between all macroscopic variables and resistivity. The constant-resistivity field-aligned and spiral field-crossing solutions identified previously are recovered as special cases.

We present the design methodology, implementation, and data products of the Energetic Particle Detector (EPD) instrument on-board the Electron Losses and Fields Investigation (ELFIN) dual SmallSat mission. ELFIN was designed to study the time-evolution of energetic particle distributions in Earth's magnetosphere, having operated in polar Low Earth Orbit (LEO) from 2018 to 2022. Each satellite's instrument payload contained a pair of detector heads to sense differential-directional fluxes of 50 keV to $\sim$5 MeV electrons (EPDE), and 50 keV to $\sim$2 MeV atomic ions (EPDI), with an energy resolution better than 50% ($ΔE/E<0.5$). The satellites were spinning with a nominal period of 2.8 s in a plane containing Earth's background magnetic field, with particle counting intervals subdivided into 16 consecutive spin sectors. Each EPD was paired with a three-axis Fluxgate Magnetometer (FGM) sampled at 80 Hz for local pitch-angle determination. Each detector head operated on the principle of pulse-height analysis (PHA) in solid state media, collimated by a rectangular 22.5$^\circ$ field of view and entrance aperture. The EPDE contained a stack of 6 silicon detectors (0.7 cm total thickness) with 0.15 cm$^2$ str geometric factor, with ion contamination mitigated by an aperture foil and digital coincidence logic. The EPDI contained a stack of 2 silicon detectors (0.1 cm total thickness) with 0.03 cm$^2$ str geometric factor, with electron contamination mitigated by a magnetic broom stage and digital anti-coincidence logic. Based on black-hole perturbation-theory calculations and an effective-one-body picture of binary black hole mergers, it has been suggested that a direct wave may be present and measurable in the ringdown. Furthermore, it has been suggested that this signal might originate from substantially inside the light ring (or photon sphere) of the remnant black hole, perhaps even from close to the event horizon. In this paper, we present three independent strands of evidence against such claims. In the process, we show that there is indeed an early-time part of the ringdown signal that cannot be directly modeled by quasinormal modes. However, there is no evidence that this is a direct wave as it has previously been defined, and we show that the properties of this component are related to the remnant's light ring rather than its horizon.

We present a comprehensive set of accurate Compton-scattering tables for use in multigroup, multiangle radiation-transport calculations. The tables contain the exact thermal Compton kernel integrated over incident-energy groups, scattered-energy groups, and angular bins. To generate them, we develop a deterministic integration method in which the pointwise kernel is evaluated with a robust numerical treatment that remains accurate across challenging regions of parameter space. The multidimensional integrals are computed using nested quadratures whose panels adapt to the Compton recoil ridge, its thermal broadening, and the nearly elastic forward-scattering boundary layer. We validate the tabulated results against published kernel profiles, angular and energy moments, mean free paths, multigroup redistribution data, and an independent Monte Carlo estimator. The resulting tables provide the radiation-transport community with accurate, reproducible, and readily usable Compton redistribution matrices and angular moments.

We perform a statistical analysis of the thermal evolution of isolated neutron stars by confronting theoretical cooling curves with luminosity-age measurements for 24 sources. For each object, we explore five purely nucleonic equations of state (APR4, FSU2R, DD2, IST, and NL3$ωρ$) combined with representative models of neutron superfluidity and proton superconductivity, while varying the gravitational mass and envelope composition. The analysis spans approximately $10^5$ cooling curves. Within this framework, we find that only the NL3$ωρ$ equation of state and, to a lesser extent, IST, both including neutron triplet pairing, provide an overall satisfactory description of the data. The remaining equations of state are in significant tension with the observations. These results demonstrate that NS cooling provides complementary constraints on dense matter, alongside nuclear structure, global NS observables, and gravitational-wave observations, while probing the interplay between the EoS and nucleon pairing.

我们通过将理论冷却曲线与 24 个源的光度年龄测量值进行对比，对孤立中子星的热演化进行了统计分析。对于每个物体，我们探索了五个纯核子状态方程（APR4、FSU2R、DD2、IST 和 NL3$ωρ$），并结合中子超流性和质子超导性的代表性模型，同时改变了引力质量和包络成分。该分析涵盖大约 10^5$ 的冷却曲线。在此框架内，我们发现只有 NL3$ωρ$ 状态方程以及较小程度上的 IST（两者都包括中子三重态配对）提供了总体令人满意的数据描述。其余的状态方程与观测结果存在显着的张力。这些结果表明，NS 冷却与核结构、全局 NS 可观测值和引力波观测一起提供了对致密物质的补充约束，同时探测了 EoS 和核子配对之间的相互作用。
