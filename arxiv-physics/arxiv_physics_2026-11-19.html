Let $P=A_N\cdots A_1$ be a product of independent standard real or complex Gaussian matrices of arbitrary fixed sizes. For complex entries we determine the exact leading terms, of the form $κ\,\varepsilon^C(\log1/\varepsilon)^q$, of the operator-norm and Frobenius small-ball probabilities of $P$, together with the corresponding asymptotics of the Laplace transform of $\|P^2\|_{F}$. The exponent $C$ is the known diversity order, while the logarithmic power follows a filling rule: when fermions are placed on ladders of states determined by the sizes and the last occupied level has degeneracy $μ$ and contains $h$ particles, $q=h(μ-h)$. The determinant of this last shell expands in Plücker coordinates graded by the Schubert cells of the Grassmannian $\mathrm{Gr}(h,μ)$, with leading coefficients proportional to normalized degrees of Schubert varieties. For real entries we prove that the Frobenius small-ball probability has an exact positive leading term of order $\varepsilon^{C/2}(\log1/\varepsilon)^q$ and that the operator-norm probability has the same order, which gives a proof of the known real log canonical threshold and its multiplicity that needs no explicit resolution of singularities. We evaluate the real leading coefficient in closed form for every shell with at most one logarithm, a class that contains all products of two factors, and we conjecture its general form, which agrees with the classical constant of vector chains. Applications include the outage probability of cascaded multi-antenna channels at high signal-to-noise ratio and the Bayesian evidence of deep linear networks.

The kernel gives leading DPS logarithms for other pairs with this DPS configuration.

We present a comprehensive study linking the single-particle flavor entanglement of a \(ν_{e} \rightarrow ν_μ\) oscillation to CPT violation using the concurrence. In a two-flavor vacuum with CP-conserving mixing, CPT invariance strictly demands \(\mathcal{C}_ν(L) = \mathcal{C}_{\barν}(L)\), meaning any measured difference \(Δ\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{CPT}} \equiv \mathcal{C}_ν - \mathcal{C}_{\barν}\) serves as a clean, background-free witness of CPT violation. We derive exact, first-order analytical relations connecting this entanglement asymmetry to the standard probability asymmetry, the baseline reflection sums, and the long-baseline decoherence floor. To rigorously test the experimental viability of this framework, we perform extensive numerical simulations. We demonstrate that the framework projects stringent sensitivity limits on Standard-Model Extension (SME) parameters at DUNE-like densities, isolate a coordinate-independent CPT invariant, and show that the signal remains robust across realistic, variable Earth matter density profiles. Crucially, we identify a distinct 24-hour sidereal modulation of the entanglement asymmetry, providing a definitive temporal "smoking gun" to disentangle CPT violation from matter effects. Finally, we show that at PeV astrophysical energies, the reflection-sum mismatch scales up to \(10^{13}\) meters, opening a novel frontier for CPT tests beyond terrestrial experiments.

Thus, our work provides one of the first attempts toward the study of the post-Newtonian waveforms involving wormholes (ECOs).

We investigate quantum gate teleportation as a primitive for satellite-enabled distributed quantum computing. In the considered architecture, a satellite distributes entangled photon pairs to two optical ground stations, which transfer the shared quantum resource to remote quantum processing units. This entanglement is then used to implement a nonlocal controlled-NOT gate between two circuit qubits located in different processors. We formulate the minimal four-qubit protocol, consisting of two network qubits that provide the entangled resource and two circuit qubits on which the distributed gate is performed. Atmospheric propagation effects in the gravitational field are modeled through an effective two-qubit depolarizing channel whose parameters depend on atmospheric and optical quantities, including the Fried parameter and receiver aperture. The performance of the protocol is quantified by the fidelity between the ideal CNOT output state and the state produced by noisy quantum gate teleportation. Our results show that the fidelity is governed by the quality of the distributed entanglement and depends jointly on atmospheric coherence, aperture size, and effective depolarization strength. These findings connect physical-layer modeling of satellite optical channels with gate-level performance in distributed quantum computing.

We develop a gauge-independent theory of magnetic reconnection beyond the standard special-relativistic framework of inertial systems in flat spacetime. Our definitions of reconnection and its rate are independent of reference frame and coordinate system, allowing physical effects of gravity and net linear acceleration to be distinguished from coordinate effects. We extend the mesoscale theory of Liu et al. [PRL 118, 085101 (2017)], in which the reconnection rate depends on physics both within the diffusion region and in an upstream ideal, force-free region. The theory identifies three non-inertial and curved-spacetime (NICS) effects, including gravitationally induced magnetic tension: gravitational distortion of the reconnection plane changes the curvature of magnetic field lines. Applying the theory to a simplified accretion plasma near )a compact object, we find that (1) whether gravity increases or decreases the reconnection rate depends on the system's orientation relative to the gravitational source, with the greatest increase occurring when the outflow jet points toward or away from the source; and (2) the magnitude of the gravitational effect scales with the dimensionless parameter $\barκ \equiv r_sΔy^2/r^3$, where $r$ is the Schwarzschild radial coordinate, $r_s$ is the source's Schwarzschild radius, and $Δy$ is the mesoscale length.

Energetic-particle irradiation can produce persistent charge separation within lunar regolith, but the internal fields predicted by subsurface-charging models depend on how particle interactions are translated into retained charge. This critical review examines the evidence and assumptions connecting microscopic charge formation to macroscopic charging. We distinguish energy deposition, net charge transfer across a specified boundary, and the spatial distribution of signed excess charge. We organize these distinctions using an average retained-charge response defined for specified material, irradiation, electrical-state, and evaluation conditions. Its use as a volumetric source requires consistent charge accounting, compatible incident-flux definitions, and an adequate separation between response formation and subsequent transport. Applying these criteria to existing models, emission studies, and silicate irradiation experiments yields three assessments. First, emission measurements constrain electron loss across the measured boundary but do not locate internally redistributed charge. Second, evidence of internal reabsorption does not by itself establish that the charge separation sustaining subsurface fields is reduced; an illustrative calculation shows that the same net charge supply can produce different peak fields depending on transfer depths. Third, laboratory electrical damage in silicate crystals does not determine breakdown rates in cryogenic, porous regolith, which additionally require constraints on local fields, failure properties, and discharge recurrence. Joint constraints on boundary charge transfer, spatial charge separation, and post-irradiation relaxation are central to evaluating lunar subsurface-charging predictions.

A proposed resolution of the black-hole information paradox must explain which information an outside observer can recover from escaping radiation\cite{Hawking,Page,Hayden,Almheiri}. Environmental memory can return correlations to the source without delivering them to that observer\cite{ChenQi,ChenMemory}. Here we establish a sharp recovery frontier at fixed mean radiation yield for single-excitation channels and realize its qubit limit with an autonomous quantum-memory emitter. Unequal transmissions balance retained coherence against information in failed collection events. A reference--radiation correlation test distinguishes this gain even when the mean collected energy is unchanged. Identical transmission channels instead give the same recovery at matched collection, regardless of their emission timescales. For a broader class of interacting sources, an ordering of collected and inaccessible coupling spectra forbids unassisted quantum transmission irrespective of internal memory. A separate coding example supports a positive rate where uniform transmission at matched actual yield has zero capacity. Together, these results identify what openness must change to assist recovery: the accessible transmission structure. They supply a physically constrained comparison of evaporation mechanisms, separating information delivered to radiation from coherence retained elsewhere or an increase in the collected output.

TianQin, a space-borne gravitational wave observatory, is designed to detect gravitational-wave signals via laser interferometry. As the inhomogeneous space plasma surrounds the inter-satellite laser links, the propagation of the laser in space plasma can introduce pointing error noise. In this work, we quantitatively assess the effect of plasma on the pointing accuracy of TQ's inter-satellite laser links. We employ a magnetohydrodynamic model to simulate the plasma distribution around the TQ orbit for different orbital orientations. Using the fourth-order Runge-Kutta method to conduct the ray-tracing calculations over a TQ orbital cycle under a solar wind dynamic pressure close to the 23-year average, we find that the plasma-induced laser deflection angle and pointing eccentricity are on the order of $10^{-20} \ \mathrm{rad}$ and picometers, respectively. These values are significantly lower than TQ's requirements. Our results demonstrate that the impact of space plasma on TQ's pointing error is negligible in moderate space weather conditions, indicating a positive outlook for the TQ mission.

We numerically study three key depolarization mechanisms of synchrotron polarization, including wavelength independent depolarization, differential Faraday rotation depolarization, and beam depolarization, across subAlfvénic and superAlfvénic turbulent regimes. Using 3D MHD simulations and synthetic observations, we analyze their effects on polarization degree and power spectra. We find that (1) SubAlfvénic turbulence shows high but strongly suppressed polarization under Faraday rotation, while superAlfvénic turbulence remains low and stable; (2) Differential Faraday depolarization dominates in ordered subAlfvénic media. For disordered superAlfvénic regimes, beam depolarization dominates at high frequencies, while at low frequencies it saturates and differential Faraday depolarization takes over as the dominant mechanism; and (3) Depolarization significantly distorts spectra in superAlfvénic turbulence but weakly affects subAlfvénic cases. Our results provide a quantitative basis for correcting depolarization biases in magnetic turbulence measurements with radio polarization data.

The nature of the Quasi-Periodic Eruptions (QPE) is still not firmly established while the phenomenon probes the innermost part of the accretion flow undergoing dramatic changes. In this chapter we review the possibility that the observed rapid variability is caused by the accretion disk instability. We summarise the defining observational properties of QPE sources: time scales of recurrence, the amplitudes of soft X-ray flares, high coherence over many cycles, and comparatively modest time-averaged luminosities. We then translate these into constraints on the characteristic radius, accretion rate, and energy reservoir of the inner disk. We review the main classes of disk instabilities relevant to supermassive black holes: radiation pressure induced thermal/viscous limit cycles, magnetically mediated inner disk transitions, and coupling to disk winds that can produce the oscillatory behavior. For each scenario we discuss the required parameter space (black hole mass, Eddington ratio, viscosity prescription, and truncation radius), the predicted spectral and timing signatures, and the degree of fine tuning needed to reproduce the observed duty cycles and flare morphologies. We highlight key tensions, including the extremely soft spectra, short recurrence times compared with standard viscous times at tens of gravitational radii, and the weak or absent contemporaneous optical/UV variability in several systems. Finally, we outline observational tests that may help distinguish between instability driven and geometrical or orbital models: phase resolved spectroscopy to track the temperature evolution, searches for hysteresis in hardness-intensity diagrams, reverberation and absorption variability tied to winds, and long baseline monitoring to measure drift in period and energy output.

The coupling between AGN feedback and the multiphase circumgalactic medium (CGM) is central to understanding galaxy evolution. In a previous paper, we showed that the black hole mass ($M_{\rm BH}$) correlates negatively with cold gas mass in IllustrisTNG100. We now extend this to the Eddington ratio ($λ_{\rm Edd}$), which traces the current accretion state, using the same sample of 5089 central galaxies at $z=0$ under the same group-specific partial correlation framework, controlling for stellar and dark matter halo mass. We find that $λ_{\rm Edd}$ correlates positively with warm gas in star-forming galaxies ($ρ\approx 0.6$-$0.7$), persisting after jointly controlling for $M_{\rm BH}$, the mass in the other non-star-forming gas phases, and specific star formation rate. The $λ_{\rm Edd}$-cold gas correlation instead reverses sign, from negative in star-forming galaxies ($ρ\approx -0.32$) to positive in quenched galaxies ($ρ\approx 0.40$). Controlling for $M_{\rm BH}$ leaves this correlation essentially unchanged in all three galaxy types. Further controlling for the total gas mass in the other phases strengthens the star-forming correlation and weakens the quenched correlation to $ρ\approx 0.21$, which remains significant. These results establish the Eddington ratio as a phase- and type-dependent tracer of the gas-black hole connection: the cold gas reservoir records the integrated black hole growth, the warm gas in star-forming galaxies carries the signature of the current accretion state, and the sign reversal of the cold-gas correlation marks the transition between the star-forming and quenched regimes.

Red supergiants (RSGs) represent a brief yet pivotal phase in the evolution of massive stars, encompassing the final stages of nuclear burning for many core-collapse supernova progenitors. Having expanded to 100 times larger than their main-sequence sizes, they occupy the cool, luminous extreme of the Hertzsprung--Russell diagram. Characterised by extended convective envelopes, complex and dynamic atmospheres, and, sometimes, substantial mass loss, RSGs play a critical role in shaping both their own evolutionary outcomes and the circumstellar environments into which they ultimately explode. Despite decades of study, fundamental aspects of RSG physics remain poorly constrained, including the mechanisms driving mass loss and the connection between observed RSG populations and the properties of Type II supernovae. Recent observational advances have transformed the field, revealing episodic mass-loss events, asymmetric circumstellar structures, and signs of rapid evolutionary change in individual objects. Alongside these advances, studies of Type II supernovae and their progenitors have raised important new questions about the final stages of massive star evolution, including the possibility of rapid changes in the years immediately preceding core collapse. Motivated by these developments, this review aims to describe our current understanding of how RSGs live and die, from their observed properties through to their role as supernova progenitors, and where that understanding remains incomplete.

The X-ray emission of young supernova remnants gives access to the composition and spatial distribution of elements ejected in supernovae. The emission from the ejecta provides crucial insights into the explosion mechanism, especially for Fe-group elements formed in the innermost layers of the star. The objective of this paper is to provide constraints on the supernova progenitor and explosion mechanism of Tycho's type Ia supernova remnant by studying the properties of Cr and Mn. The unprecedented spectral resolution of XRISM/Resolve allows us to detect significant emission lines of Cr and Mn in different regions of Tycho's supernova remnant, even at small scale, providing a much more precise spatial distribution of Cr and Mn with respect to previous measurements. Significant Cr and Mn emission lines are observed in the Fe bright northeastern-to-southeastern emitting rim, some in the center region, but none in the southern region. Overall the best agreement is found for models with a too high metallicity to be realistic for Tycho's location. This points to additional sources of neutronisation such as carbon simmering for Chandrasekhar mass progenitor. For sub-Chandrasekhar mass, double detonation models with a WD mass lower than 1Msol are suggested.

When the gravitational wave frequency of an inspiraling binary matches a normal-mode frequency of a neutron star (NS), the mode can be resonantly excited. The excitation transfers orbital energy to the star and shifts the phase of the emitted gravitational-wave (GW) signal, thereby providing a potential probe of the NS interior. However, estimates of this phase shift in the literature vary by orders of magnitude due to different approximations and formulations used in calculating the tidal coupling strength. We revisit this problem using a fully relativistic scattering formalism, which serves as our benchmark calculation and provides a gauge-invariant mode response together with an effective tidal-mode coupling strength. We calculate the phase shifts induced by the excitation of stratification g-modes, crustal interface i-modes, and hadron-quark interface i-modes, and estimate their detectability with Fisher-matrix forecasts for current and next-generation detectors. In our models, direct g-mode excitation remains below the detection thresholds, even for the strongest stratification considered. Crust-core i-modes at large shear speeds provide the most favorable prospects and crust melting can produce a substantially larger phase shift. Hadron-quark i-modes remain below the next-generation detection thresholds.
