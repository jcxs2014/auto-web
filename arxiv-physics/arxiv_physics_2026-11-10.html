We study vacuum electron--positron pair production in phase-modulated elliptically polarized electric fields using the quantum kinetic equations. We analyze how the modulation amplitude, frequency, ellipticity, and modulation form affect the spin-resolved momentum spectra and the spin asymmetry. Cosine-type modulation can deform ring-shaped momentum distributions into spiral patterns. This deformation depends nonmonotonically on the modulation amplitude and frequency and is most pronounced for circular polarization. In the transverse momentum plane, the spin-resolved yields favor the spin channel along the direction of field handedness. Reversing the field handedness would reverse the favored channel while leaving the total yield unchanged. Sine-type modulation preserves ring-shaped and mirror-symmetric spectra approximately, however, it leads to a stronger dependence of the spin asymmetry on the modulation amplitude than cosine-type modulation does. These results show that phase modulation and ellipticity can control the momentum spectra patterns in vacuum pair production. Moreover, phase modulation can also affect the degree of spin asymmetry.

We study two-photon production of the pseudoscalar pairs $π^{0}η$, $π^{0}η^{\prime}$, $ηη$, $ηη^{\prime}$, and $η^{\prime}η^{\prime}$ at center-of-mass energies $2<Q<4\,\mathrm{GeV}$ in $k_{T}$ factorization, including the chirally enhanced twist-3 amplitudes and the two-gluon component of the $η^{(\prime)}$. The twist-3 amplitudes raise the hard cross sections by about an order of magnitude in all five channels. They bring $ηη$ close to the Belle data, while $π^{0}η$ stays 3 to 5 times below and falls too slowly with energy. Varying the two-gluon distribution amplitude within its uncertainty changes the cross sections by about 2% in $π^{0}η$ and $ηη$ but by up to 24% in the $η^{\prime}$ channels, making them its probe. The hard and handbag mechanisms each give nearly constant ratios $σ(π^{0}η)/σ(π^{0}π^{0})$ and $σ(ηη)/σ(π^{0}π^{0})$, whereas below $3.2\,\mathrm{GeV}$ the measured $π^{0}η$ ratio lies above both predictions and the $ηη$ ratio below them. Adding the handbag amplitude coherently, with its modulus fixed by the pion and kaon channels and its phase fitted, reproduces the $π^{0}η$ ratio up to $4\,\mathrm{GeV}$ and the $ηη$ ratio below $2.8\,\mathrm{GeV}$, and steepens $σ(π^{0}η)$ to a $Q^{-n}$ dependence with $n=9.7\pm1.6$, against the measured $10.5\pm1.2\pm0.5$. The energy dependence of the cross section ratios can thus be understood as the interference of two mechanisms of comparable size.

Weyl geometric gravity theory is a gravitational theory with Lagrangian density constructed from the square of the Weyl curvature scalar and the strength of the Weyl vector. The theory can be reformulated as a scalar - vector - tensor theory in the Riemann geometry by introducing an auxiliary scalar field, in the presence of a non - minimal coupling between the Ricci scalar and the scalar field. Assuming that the Weyl vector has only a radial component, an exact spherically symmetric black hole type solution of the field equations in the vacuum can be obtained. The solution depends on three arbitrary integration constants. As compared to the case of the Schwarzschild black hole, the Weyl geometric gravity black hole solution contains two new terms, linear and quadratic, in the radial coordinate. It is the main goal of the present work to investigate astrophysical properties of the Weyl geometric black holes by considering the behavior of the timelike geodesics, which allows to explore how the geometric nonmetricity influences the strong-field dynamics of massive test particles as well as the topological structure of the periodic orbits. The resulting gravitational wave emission in extreme mass-ratio inspiral (EMRI) systems is also considered. Our results show that the presence of nonmetricity in Weyl geometric gravity leaves distinct and measurable signatures on both the orbital topology and the emitted gravitational waveforms of compact EMRI systems. These unique temporal and amplitude modulations provide a promising observational avenue for testing the presence of nonmetricity on cosmic scales.

High-energy astrophysical neutrinos probe non-standard neutrino interactions over cosmological distances. We study flavor-dependent interactions between neutrinos and dark matter (DM) for neutrinos produced inside the dense DM spike surrounding the supermassive black hole of the blazar TXS~0506+056. A flavor-diagonal potential $V_α=A_α\,ρ_χ(r)$ induced by coherent forward scattering modifies the mixing in the medium, and the flavor-dependent scattering cross section attenuates the neutrino flux along the line of sight. We show that the propagation is adiabatic throughout the spike and the halo, so that the matter eigenstates produced at the source are conserved and only re-projected onto flavors at Earth. Two regimes emerge. For $A\ll 10^{-38}\,{\rm cm^3}$ the mixing is unaffected and the IceCube three-flavor composition limits the cross section to $σ_{νχ}/m_χ\lesssim 10^{-28}\,{\rm cm^2/GeV}$. For larger $A$ the interacting flavor is isolated into a single matter eigenstate at the source; the two remaining eigenstates escape without attenuation, producing a survival plateau that relaxes the limits by up to four orders of magnitude. The sign of the potential can differ between neutrinos and antineutrinos, so that the isolated state connects to different vacuum mass eigenstates for $ν$ and $\barν$, which leaves a characteristic imprint on the flavor composition even without attenuation. The requirement that the potential induced by the dark matter density around Earth do not affect the observed PMNS matrix, so that the flavor projection at Earth is performed with the local matter mixing, gives A$\lesssim 10^{-30}\text{cm} ^3$. The combined constraints are presented in the $(A,σ_{νχ}/m_χ)$ plane and compared with a vector-mediator benchmark.

Energetic-particle irradiation can produce persistent charge separation within lunar regolith, but the internal fields predicted by subsurface-charging models depend on how particle interactions are translated into retained charge. This critical review examines the evidence and assumptions connecting microscopic charge formation to macroscopic charging. We distinguish energy deposition, net charge transfer across a specified boundary, and the spatial distribution of signed excess charge. We organize these distinctions using an average retained-charge response defined for specified material, irradiation, electrical-state, and evaluation conditions. Its use as a volumetric source requires consistent charge accounting, compatible incident-flux definitions, and an adequate separation between response formation and subsequent transport. Applying these criteria to existing models, emission studies, and silicate irradiation experiments yields three assessments. First, emission measurements constrain electron loss across the measured boundary but do not locate internally redistributed charge. Second, evidence of internal reabsorption does not by itself establish that the charge separation sustaining subsurface fields is reduced; an illustrative calculation shows that the same net charge supply can produce different peak fields depending on transfer depths. Third, laboratory electrical damage in silicate crystals does not determine breakdown rates in cryogenic, porous regolith, which additionally require constraints on local fields, failure properties, and discharge recurrence. Joint constraints on boundary charge transfer, spatial charge separation, and post-irradiation relaxation are central to evaluating lunar subsurface-charging predictions.

A proposed resolution of the black-hole information paradox must explain which information an outside observer can recover from escaping radiation\cite{Hawking,Page,Hayden,Almheiri}. Environmental memory can return correlations to the source without delivering them to that observer\cite{ChenQi,ChenMemory}. Here we establish a sharp recovery frontier at fixed mean radiation yield for single-excitation channels and realize its qubit limit with an autonomous quantum-memory emitter. Unequal transmissions balance retained coherence against information in failed collection events. A reference--radiation correlation test distinguishes this gain even when the mean collected energy is unchanged. Identical transmission channels instead give the same recovery at matched collection, regardless of their emission timescales. For a broader class of interacting sources, an ordering of collected and inaccessible coupling spectra forbids unassisted quantum transmission irrespective of internal memory. A separate coding example supports a positive rate where uniform transmission at matched actual yield has zero capacity. Together, these results identify what openness must change to assist recovery: the accessible transmission structure. They supply a physically constrained comparison of evaporation mechanisms, separating information delivered to radiation from coherence retained elsewhere or an increase in the collected output.
