We complete a proof of the quantum null energy condition (QNEC) in the mathematical framework of Half-Sided Translations and Modular Inclusions (HST/HSMI) removing an assumption that applied to previous proofs. Here we establish a theorem, which when translated to a physical setting implies that the relative entropy comparing the state of fields outside a stationary black hole with a Hartle-Hawking (HH) state is always a convex function of the horizon cut. Previous proofs only applied when a purification with finite null energy was available. We also obtain a QNEC generalization in which the HH Hilbert space is replaced with one based on a weight which is plausibly of relevance to evaporating black holes in flat space.

We investigate the entanglement entropy of topological quantum states associated with $T_{d,\,dn}$ torus links in (2+1)-dimensional SU(2) Chern--Simons theory at level $k$. These links consist of $d$ components with pairwise linking number $n$, and the corresponding link-complement states provide a natural setting to study multipartite entanglement in topological quantum field theories. We analyze the semiclassical limit $k\to\infty$ of the entanglement entropy by deriving the asymptotic behavior of the moments of the reduced density matrix. To achieve this, we decompose the integrable-representation space into left-edge, right-edge, and bulk regions and employ the stationary-phase method to evaluate their respective contributions. We find that the leading asymptotic behavior is governed entirely by the two edge regions, while the bulk contribution is asymptotically suppressed. The resulting expression for the limiting entanglement entropy is given in terms of Hurwitz zeta functions and exhibits a dependence on the residue class of $k$ modulo $n$. We further investigate the dependence of the limiting entanglement entropy on the winding parameter $n$ and the number of link components $d$. In the large-$d$ limit, the entropy reduces to a binary entropy determined by two normalized weights associated with the left and right edges. Finally, we show that the two iterated limits, $k\to\infty$ and $d\to\infty$, commute, reproducing the result obtained previously by taking the large-party limit first.

Which complex classical paths contribute to a real-time Feynman path integral? Picard-Lefschetz theory answers this question with the intersection numbers of the thimbles, which are notoriously hard to compute in infinite dimensions. We show that resurgence provides a practical alternative. We compute the asymptotic series of the real and complex classical paths to high order, with Feynman diagrams and with a transport recursion for the cumulants, and read off the adjacency of the classical paths from the divergent tails of the series with Borel-Padé methods. The South-East rule then turns adjacency into relevance. For a particle meeting a Rosen-Morse barrier, we identify the relevant complex classical paths in front of and behind the barrier, including the complex path that carries quantum tunnelling, follow ordinary and higher-order Stokes phenomena as the final point moves, and verify the predictions with the exact propagator. The resummed series reproduce the propagator with exponential accuracy. Finally, we outline how the expansion extends to relativistic path integrals governed by a Hamiltonian constraint.

Direct-product spacetimes offer a tractable route to exact solutions in gravity theories beyond general relativity. We study $n$-dimensional direct-product spacetimes of $(m_1+m_2)$, $(2+m)$, and $(2+\ldots+2)$ type in linear gravity, general pure quadratic-curvature gravity, and one of the recently proposed higher-dimensional extensions of conformal gravity, allowing the two-dimensional subspaces to have nonconstant curvature. We classify the resulting solution spaces and determine how the allowed geometries depend on the gravitational theory and, in quadratic gravity, on the curvature couplings. Metrics containing two-dimensional part(s) of nonconstant curvature are governed either by algebraic conditions on a curvature profile or, in one special class of quadratic gravity, by a differential equation. Relations governing metrics with only constant-curvature $m$-dimensional subspaces are purely algebraic. We further examine conformal relations to linear-gravity solutions, generalized Petrov types, and compatibility with electromagnetic sources. Particularly noteworthy is that standard static black-hole geometries are absent from the direct-product sector of the considered form of conformal gravity for $n>4$. Four- and six-dimensional cases are analyzed in detail, and explicit analytic solutions illustrating the resulting structure are presented whenever possible.

Energetic-particle irradiation can produce persistent charge separation within lunar regolith, but the internal fields predicted by subsurface-charging models depend on how particle interactions are translated into retained charge. This critical review examines the evidence and assumptions connecting microscopic charge formation to macroscopic charging. We distinguish energy deposition, net charge transfer across a specified boundary, and the spatial distribution of signed excess charge. We organize these distinctions using an average retained-charge response defined for specified material, irradiation, electrical-state, and evaluation conditions. Its use as a volumetric source requires consistent charge accounting, compatible incident-flux definitions, and an adequate separation between response formation and subsequent transport. Applying these criteria to existing models, emission studies, and silicate irradiation experiments yields three assessments. First, emission measurements constrain electron loss across the measured boundary but do not locate internally redistributed charge. Second, evidence of internal reabsorption does not by itself establish that the charge separation sustaining subsurface fields is reduced; an illustrative calculation shows that the same net charge supply can produce different peak fields depending on transfer depths. Third, laboratory electrical damage in silicate crystals does not determine breakdown rates in cryogenic, porous regolith, which additionally require constraints on local fields, failure properties, and discharge recurrence. Joint constraints on boundary charge transfer, spatial charge separation, and post-irradiation relaxation are central to evaluating lunar subsurface-charging predictions.

A proposed resolution of the black-hole information paradox must explain which information an outside observer can recover from escaping radiation\cite{Hawking,Page,Hayden,Almheiri}. Environmental memory can return correlations to the source without delivering them to that observer\cite{ChenQi,ChenMemory}. Here we establish a sharp recovery frontier at fixed mean radiation yield for single-excitation channels and realize its qubit limit with an autonomous quantum-memory emitter. Unequal transmissions balance retained coherence against information in failed collection events. A reference--radiation correlation test distinguishes this gain even when the mean collected energy is unchanged. Identical transmission channels instead give the same recovery at matched collection, regardless of their emission timescales. For a broader class of interacting sources, an ordering of collected and inaccessible coupling spectra forbids unassisted quantum transmission irrespective of internal memory. A separate coding example supports a positive rate where uniform transmission at matched actual yield has zero capacity. Together, these results identify what openness must change to assist recovery: the accessible transmission structure. They supply a physically constrained comparison of evaporation mechanisms, separating information delivered to radiation from coherence retained elsewhere or an increase in the collected output.

TianQin, a space-borne gravitational wave observatory, is designed to detect gravitational-wave signals via laser interferometry. As the inhomogeneous space plasma surrounds the inter-satellite laser links, the propagation of the laser in space plasma can introduce pointing error noise. In this work, we quantitatively assess the effect of plasma on the pointing accuracy of TQ's inter-satellite laser links. We employ a magnetohydrodynamic model to simulate the plasma distribution around the TQ orbit for different orbital orientations. Using the fourth-order Runge-Kutta method to conduct the ray-tracing calculations over a TQ orbital cycle under a solar wind dynamic pressure close to the 23-year average, we find that the plasma-induced laser deflection angle and pointing eccentricity are on the order of $10^{-20} \ \mathrm{rad}$ and picometers, respectively. These values are significantly lower than TQ's requirements. Our results demonstrate that the impact of space plasma on TQ's pointing error is negligible in moderate space weather conditions, indicating a positive outlook for the TQ mission.

We use the core-radius, $r_γ,$ of a galaxy's central core along with a volume-limited sample of brightest cluster galaxies (BCG) to determine the $z=0$ mass distribution function of the most massive super-massive black holes (SMBH). We recover a $M_{BH}-r_γ$ relationship between the SMBH mass, $M_{BH},$ and $r_γ$ of form $M_{BH} \approx1.3\times10^{10}~(r_γ/{\mathrm{kpc}})~M_\odot,$ with $σ= 0.25\pm0.03$ dex scatter in $M_{BH}.$ We derive a core distribution function (CDF), based on HST and DLS images of the BCGs. The CDF falls steeply as $N(r_γ)\sim r_γ^{-3}$ for $r_γ>0.50$ kpc. Only $\sim5\%$ of BCGs have $r_γ>1$ kpc. Combining the CDF and $M_{BH} - r_γ$ relationship yields the BH mass distribution function (MDF). For $M_{BH} \geq 7\times10^{9}~\mathrm{M_\odot}$ (the mean $M_{BH}$ at $r_γ=0.50$ kpc) we derive: $\log\left(\frac{dN(M_{BH})}{d{M_{BH}/10^{10} M_\odot}}~[\rm Mpc^{-3}]\right)=(-3.05\pm0.42)~\log\left(\frac{M_{BH}}{10^{10}M_\odot}\right)~-~5.11\ (+0.41, -0.36)$. Cores with $r_γ\geq 0.50$ kpc are found almost exclusively in BCGs, implying that SMBHs with $M_{BH} \geq7\times10^{9}~\mathrm{M_\odot}$ may also be exclusive to BCGs. Core-radius correlates with $α,$ a parameter that characterizes the growth of BCG envelopes by mergers, suggesting that the most massive SMBHs grew in tandem with their BCG hosts. The BH MDF embodies a significant population of SMBH with $M_{BH} >7\times10^{9} M_\odot.$ When combined with the Liepold & Ma (2024) BH MDF to account for SMBH with $M_{BH} < 7\times10^{9} M_\odot,$ it can comfortably explain the nHz gravitational wave background.

Magnetic null points define the topology of magnetic fields in stellar coronae, planetary magnetospheres, and astrospheres and are commonly associated with magnetic reconnection. We investigate the local structure of stationary 3D resistive, incompressible magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) flows in the vicinity of an isolated magnetic null and an isolated velocity stagnation point, assuming that the two nulls are colocated. The governing MHD equations are solved self-consistently using a first-order local expansion of the magnetic and velocity fields about the null. For any given magnetic-null configuration, the equations admit a two-parameter family of stationary solutions, comprising multiple solution branches with distinct velocity topologies. Along these families, the velocity-null polarity can change at bifurcation points where the flow becomes effectively 2.5D. A single magnetic null can therefore support positive-radial, negative-radial, and spiral stagnation flows, with qualitatively different inflow, outflow, and rotational properties. The associated pressure and resistivity distributions are likewise determined self-consistently and vary across the solution family, demonstrating that they are coupled to the flow topology rather than prescribed independently. The generic solutions do not require the velocity and magnetic separatrices to coincide, allowing plasma to cross the magnetic fan separatrix and providing a local reconnective flow. These results demonstrate that, in the presence of nonuniform resistivity, the magnetic topology alone does not uniquely determine the associated stationary flow, pressure, or resistivity structure around a 3D magnetic null, revealing the nonlinear feedback between all macroscopic variables and resistivity. The constant-resistivity field-aligned and spiral field-crossing solutions identified previously are recovered as special cases.

The first highly significant detection of the polarization of the X-rays from the Black Hole X-ray Binary Cygnus X-1 revealed surprisingly strong polarization parallel to its radio jet. The observation is now commonly interpreted as resulting from the Comptonization of some lower frequency seed photons in a laterally extended corona. The emitting plasma is likely moving along the jet axis with a sizable fraction beta of the speed of light, amplifying the polarization degree. In this letter we address the question of how laterally extended the corona actually needs to be to account for the observations, and we calculate the required outflow velocities of velocity-stratified coronas as a function of the stationary-frame aspect ratio h=H/2R of cylindrical coronas of height H and radius R. We find that models with h lesser equal 0.8 and beta=0.5-0.9 at the top of the corona can give both the observed photon index of Gamma=1.6 and the observed 4% polarization parallel to the symmetry axis. We do not find suitable solutions for larger h. Given the constraints on h, we discuss the likely size of the corona, emphasizing that compact coronas close to the black hole are preferred as they can tap more efficiently into the gravitational energy and the rotational energy of the black hole, and are less exposed to their own emission reprocessed in the optically thick portion of the accretion flow.

We investigate how the late-time decays of unstable particles into dark matter and neutrinos can leave measurable imprints on both cosmological observables and collider experiments. We study this mechanism within the scotogenic model, where the lightest singlet fermion $N_1$ serves as the dark matter candidate and is produced via the late decay of the lightest inert doublet scalar via the superWIMP mechanism. By incorporating a kination-dominated era in the early Universe, the altered expansion rate allows to match the observed $N_1$ relic density in parameter regions that would be underabundant in standard cosmology. The energy exchange between the injected neutrinos and photons resulting from the late-time decay can produce CMB spectral $μ$-distortions, detectable shifts in the effective number of relativistic species $N_{\rm eff}$, or both simultaneously. Concentrating on the lightest component of inert doublet scalars with masses between $200$ and $500~\text{GeV}$, we find that these cosmological signals are accompanied by complementary signatures accessible at the LHC. Although our analysis is derived within the scotogenic framework, our findings apply more broadly to scenarios in which the unstable particle undergoes late-time out-of-equilibrium decay to produce dark matter and non-thermal neutrinos, offering how future CMB measurements and collider searches can jointly probe dark matter production and the early Universe's thermal history.

Thanks to recent measurements by space and ground-level detectors, the energy spectra of cosmic ray protons and helium nuclei are now known with good precision over a broad energy range extending for more than six orders of magnitude, from a few GeV to approximately 10PeV. These measurements reveal that both spectra do not have a simple power--law form, and exhibit several features. The proton to helium ratio exhibits a very unexpected behavior: it decreases for rigidities $\lesssim 10^5$~GV, and increases for higher rigidities. Several authors have interpreted the observed spectral features as revealing the fact the CR spectra are formed by the contributions of a few components (in some cases as few as two) produced by sources that belong to distinct classes of astrophysical accelerators, with sources in the same class producing spectra with the same characteristic shape. Most of these authors also consider it necessary to include the contribution of one (or perhaps more than one) ``local'' sources, recently active in the vicinity of the solar system. However, this approach has several limitations, and in this work we discuss an alternative scenario in which the cosmic ray sources, even belonging to the same class, produce spectra with a broad range of shapes. The power--law form of the total source spectrum emerges as the average of these contributions. In this framework observable spectral features can be produced by statistical fluctuations, and different spectral slopes for protons and helium nuclei can be observed even if one assumes that acceleration is rigidity-dependent.
