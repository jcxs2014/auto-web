The Standard Model does not contain sufficient amount of CP violation to account for the observed baryon asymmetry of the universe. Beyond Standard Model scenarios that aim to explain this asymmetry introduce new sources of CP violation, but these generically feed into electric dipole moments (EDMs), which are severely constrained by experiment. We indentify a new class of CP-violating sources that cannot generate EDMs directly: EDM operators are C conserving and P violating, whereas CP violation introduced here is, by construction, C violating and P conserving. We give a concrete model realisation accommodating such sources and discuss their relevance for baryogenesis.

Jet tagging is one of the most important applications of machine learning at the Large Hadron Collider. Recent models build in more physics knowledge or rely on large-scale pretraining, but the experiments place strict limits on their inference cost. Knowledge distillation can transfer their performance to small students that are cheap to run. We ask which properties of a teacher help the student, how small a student can get and how close it can come to the state of the art. On a jet tagging benchmark, we distil six teachers that differ in architecture, symmetry and pretraining, including two foundation models, into five small students. Every teacher improves every student, and the gain depends mainly on the strength of the teacher. We then shrink our two strongest distilled students, Transformer and L-GATr-slim, in width and in depth. With 20 thousand parameters, a distilled L-GATr-slim outperforms every published jet tagger trained from scratch at a fraction of the inference cost of every teacher. Even with two thousand parameters, it matches ParT, a model with a thousand times as many parameters. We also study what the students learn from their teachers. Our benchmark of inference cost shows that students of the same size can differ by almost an order of magnitude in speed. We release the code and the weights of all trained models.

The Fröhlich-Pizzo (FP) model is a quantum-mechanical model originally developed to describe the interaction between an atom and the electromagnetic field in the nonrelativistic limit $c\to\infty$. We show that an adaptation of the FP model based on the local algebras of a $U(1)$ chiral current captures essential features of the horizon-induced decoherence mechanism identified by Danielson, Satishchandran, and Wald (DSW) in the limit of large holding time $T$. Our analysis suggests that the effective dynamics of the system undergoing decoherence in the DSW setting retains non-Markovian features. Our approach also offers a glimpse into the possible role of quantum events in the dynamics underlying horizon-induced decoherence.

When topological solitons are coupled to fermions, they often induce a nontrivial fermion number. In the chiral quark soliton model (CQSM), this phenomenon underlies the identification of soliton topology with baryon number. However, whether this topology--fermion-number correspondence is determined solely by soliton topology or also depends on the fermion--soliton coupling scheme remains unclear. In this paper, we investigate this question by comparing several coupling schemes to the same $\mathrm{SU}(N)$ chiral soliton background. To treat the corresponding Dirac Hamiltonians within a common framework, we evaluate the variation of the regularized fermion-number along a model-parameter space. For the standard chiral Yukawa coupling of the CQSM, this variation reproduces the topological charge $Q$ associated with $π_3(\mathrm{SU}(N))$. The same result is obtained for an axial-vector coupling, whereas the fermion number variation vanishes for a vector coupling, despite the nontrivial topology of the chiral soliton background. These results provide an explicit example in which soliton topology alone does not determine the fermionic response; the topology--fermion-number correspondence also depends on how the background field is coupled to fermions.

We develop a generalized coordinate Bethe-ansatz framework for the massive Thirring model with an explicitly time-dependent fermion mass. Unlike the generalized Bethe ansatz for chiral quantum field theories, where particle transport acts in internal spin spaces and leads to quantum Knizhnik-Zamolodchikov equations, the present construction acts directly on particle coordinates and physical rapidities. Its central new feature is a rapidity lattice. Because the time-dependent mass couples right- and left-moving components already in the one-particle sector, a fixed-rapidity block is not closed under spatial winding. Periodicity therefore requires all sectors generated by the finite shift $β\toβ-p$. Particle exchange relates amplitudes on this lattice through scalar analytic difference equations of Ruijsenaars type, whose solutions determine the complete time-dependent many-body wavefunction. Consistency of the coordinate construction also selects an exponential evolution of the bare mass, providing a new realization of the time dependent integrability-renormalization group correspondence. In the static limit, the rapidity-lattice construction reduces to the conventional massive-Thirring Bethe equations. Through the massive-Thirring/sine Gordon equivalence, the result also connects this exact quantum construction to the generalized space-time dependent Lax formalism.

Magnetic null points define the magnetic field topology in stellar coronae, planetary magnetospheres, and astrospheres, and are commonly associated with magnetic reconnection. We investigate the local structure of stationary three-dimensional (3D) resistive, incompressible magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) flows in the vicinity of an isolated magnetic null and an isolated velocity stagnation point, assuming that the two nulls are colocated. The governing MHD equations are solved self-consistently using a first-order local expansion of the magnetic and velocity fields about the null. For any given magnetic-null configuration, the equations admit a two-parameter family of stationary solutions, comprising multiple solution branches with distinct velocity topologies. Along these families, the velocity-null polarity can change at bifurcation points where the flow becomes effectively 2.5D. A single magnetic null can therefore support positive-radial, negative-radial, and spiral stagnation flows, with qualitatively different inflow, outflow, and rotational properties. The associated pressure and resistivity distributions are likewise determined self-consistently and vary across the solution family, demonstrating that they are coupled to the flow topology rather than prescribed independently. The generic solutions do not require the velocity and magnetic separatrices to coincide, allowing plasma to cross the magnetic fan separatrix and providing a local reconnective flow. These results demonstrate that, in the presence of nonuniform resistivity, the magnetic topology alone does not uniquely determine the associated stationary flow, pressure, or resistivity structure around a 3D magnetic null, revealing the nonlinear feedback between all macroscopic variables and resistivity. The constant-resistivity field-aligned and spiral field-crossing solutions identified previously are recovered as special cases.

We present the design methodology, implementation, and data products of the Energetic Particle Detector (EPD) instrument on-board the Electron Losses and Fields Investigation (ELFIN) dual SmallSat mission. ELFIN was designed to study the time-evolution of energetic particle distributions in Earth's magnetosphere, having operated in polar Low Earth Orbit (LEO) from 2018 to 2022. Each satellite's instrument payload contained a pair of detector heads to sense differential-directional fluxes of 50 keV to $\sim$5 MeV electrons (EPDE), and 50 keV to $\sim$2 MeV atomic ions (EPDI), with an energy resolution better than 50% ($ΔE/E<0.5$). The satellites were spinning with a nominal period of 2.8 s in a plane containing Earth's background magnetic field, with particle counting intervals subdivided into 16 consecutive spin sectors. Each EPD was paired with a three-axis Fluxgate Magnetometer (FGM) sampled at 80 Hz for local pitch-angle determination. Each detector head operated on the principle of pulse-height analysis (PHA) in solid state media, collimated by a rectangular 22.5$^\circ$ field of view and entrance aperture. The EPDE contained a stack of 6 silicon detectors (0.7 cm total thickness) with 0.15 cm$^2$ str geometric factor, with ion contamination mitigated by an aperture foil and digital coincidence logic. The EPDI contained a stack of 2 silicon detectors (0.1 cm total thickness) with 0.03 cm$^2$ str geometric factor, with electron contamination mitigated by a magnetic broom stage and digital anti-coincidence logic. The total assembled mass, volume, and power required for the EPD detector heads and circuit boards were 939 g, 10$\times$9.8$\times$8.4 cm$^3$, and 2.3 W, respectively. We describe each EPD design element alongside instrument data products and processing.

Collisionless shocks confine the particles they accelerate with the upstream wave field they themselves drive. Streaming particles resonantly amplify Alfvén waves, and the waves then confine the same particles by pitch-angle scattering. The rigidity a shock precursor can hold is therefore capped by how fast those waves grow, and that coupling has not previously been constrained in-situ at any interplanetary shock. Here we report Parker Solar Probe measurements of a fast, near-parallel coronal mass ejection-driven shock at 0.24~AU on 2023 March 13 that constrain this wave-particle coupling through the energetic-particle population it accelerates. We measure the spectral folding energy, the boundary between the confined and the upstream-escaping protons, and find that it falls from $\sim$6 to $\sim$1.5~MeV as $E_\mathrm{fold}\proptoΔt^{\,α}$ with $α\simeq0.34$, where $Δt$ is the time remaining before shock arrival. One precursor convective time before arrival this boundary is at $E_\mathrm{c}\simeq 2.46$~MeV, a characteristic confinement energy of a precursor of scale $L_\mathrm{EP}$. Read through the quasi-linear marginal-stability condition, the same evolution implies a resonant wave-growth timescale $τ_\mathrm{growth}(E)\propto E^{1/α}\simeq E^{2.92}$ that exceeds the precursor convective time above $E_\mathrm{c}$. The post-shock pitch-angle anisotropy reverses sign across the same confinement--escape transition at 2--5~MeV. The rigidity to which a shock precursor can confine particles is therefore set, in-situ, by how quickly the streaming population builds its own confining waves.

Black holes are usually described either as classical geometries on which quantum fields propagate or as objects whose complete quantum description lies beyond semiclassical gravity. Hawking radiation already shows, however, that a black hole is not an exactly isolated system: the horizon carries a thermal scale and, after coarse graining, may be associated with fluctuations, dissipation and environmental correlations. Whether this horizon-scale openness can be encoded directly in the gravitational background, before any stochastic treatment of the metric, remains an open question. Here we answer it by introducing, for the first time, a conserved Hawking-scale anisotropic source representing the coarse-grained near-horizon atmosphere and solving the Einstein equations for a static black hole at fixed ADM mass. The dressed geometry has a renormalized mass function, a self-consistently shifted horizon and a modified surface gravity. Because the same lapse governs the horizon and timelike geodesics, the thermal response is accompanied by a correlated orbital restructuring: the effective potential is renormalized, the ISCO moves inward and the near-horizon residence time increases. The construction adds no Langevin force to a fixed Schwarzschild background; rather, it defines a deterministic dressed mean geometry, providing a controlled background on which stochastic metric fluctuations may subsequently be treated in the Einstein-Langevin framework. This establishes the deterministic mean-field sector as the necessary first step for future stochastic gravity programs addressing horizon-scale openness.

Collisionless shocks accelerate particles at a rate set by the diffusion coefficient of those particles in waves they themselves generate. Theory fixes that coefficient only in steady state, and no measurement has tested whether an evolving shock ever reaches that state. We report Parker Solar Probe observations of a fast, near-parallel interplanetary shock at 0.24 au, encountered four hours after eruption, with the upstream waves and the protons they scatter recorded on the same magnetic field line. From the wave spectra alone we derive the parallel mean free path of 0.05-30 MeV protons, resolved in energy and in distance from the shock. The mirror force of the measured compressive fluctuations supplies the scattering through the pitch angles that gyroresonance cannot reach. The mean free path grows with distance nearly as the steady self-generated solution requires, yet the steady balance holds at one energy only, near 1.3 MeV. The intensity profiles are a factor of two shallower than the measured coefficient predicts, because the waves are still growing. Confinement fails between 2.3 and 6 MeV, beginning a factor of two below what the age of the shock allows. The growth of the waves its own particles drive, rather than its age or its size, limits the energy an evolving shock confines.

We present an updated High-Altitude Water Cherenkov (HAWC) analysis of the crowded Galactic-plane area near PSR J1928+1746, which was previously linked to the extended source 3HWC J1928+178. With 2904 days of HAWC data, improved event reconstruction, and a multicomponent likelihood model that includes six TeV source components and Galactic diffuse emission, we describe the region using six components and find a distinct, moderately extended source close to PSR J1928+1746. This source, HAWC J1928+1750, is detected from about 2 TeV to above 100 TeV and is well-described by a log-parabola spectrum with normalization $K = 5.0 \times 10^{-15}$ TeV$^{-1}$ cm$^{-2}$ s$^{-1}$ at 12 TeV, index $α= 2.09$, curvature $β= 0.35$, and Gaussian extension $σ= 0.19^\circ$. Interpreting the emission using diffusion-halo templates under the assumption $B = 8 μ\mathrm{G}$ yields the diffusion coefficient D(100 TeV) = $2.1 \times 10^{27}$ cm$^2$ s$^{-1}$, which is about 500 times smaller than typical interstellar diffusion values at similar energies. This suggests that particle transport around PSR J1928+1746 is strongly suppressed under the TeV-halo interpretation. The TeV spectrum is consistent with leptonic inverse-Compton emission originating from pulsar-injected electrons and positrons, which requires less than approximately 1% of the pulsar's characteristic spin-down energy. However, a hadronic contribution from the surrounding environment cannot be excluded.

The r-modes in rotating neutron stars are a promising source of continuous gravitational waves, and may grow or be excited to amplitudes $α\lesssim1$. We perform general relativistic simulations of nonlinear $l=|m|=2$ r-modes in a rapidly rotating neutron star with spin frequency $f_{spin}\approx792$ Hz, relaxing the Cowling and small-$α$ approximations. The star is idealised as an unmagnetised, one-component, perfect fluid with a polytropic equation of state. The r-mode frequency is measured to be $f_{r-mode}=1014$ Hz for initial amplitude $α_0=0.2$. The dimensionless frequency $κ\approx0.7197$ agrees with an estimate from linear perturbation theory to within 1.7%. $f_{r-mode}$ increases quadratically with amplitude, and $κ$ decreases by 3.4% across simulations where $α_0$ is varied through the range $0.2\leqα_0\leq1.2$. The gravitational wave strain calculated from the Newman-Penrose scalar implies $h_{r-mode}\approx9.7\times10^{-22}$ at $10$ kpc for $α_0=0.2$, which agrees with Einstein's quadrupole formula to within 35%. We also report on nonlinear phenomena observed in our simulations, e.g. differential rotation, amplitude decay, and signatures of nonlinear mode coupling in the spacetime oscillations.

Encounters of stellar-mass black holes (sBH) in active galactic nuclei discs naturally form mean-motion resonances of sBH pairs. These mean-motion resonances can be broken close to the central supermassive black hole when gravitational-wave emission dominates over gas torques. We derive a simple condition that depends only on the sBH mass ratio and the resonance index for which a pair of sBH in a mean-motion resonance will remain intact and can be detectable as a multiple Extreme-Mass-Ratio-Inspirals (mEMRIs) in the LISA band. We present for the first time the gravitational-wave strain signatures of a pair of co-migrating sBH in mean-motion resonance, including additional gravitational-wave harmonics associated with orbital conjunctions of the individual EMRIs. The frequency evolution and the associated chirp mass is degenerate with isolated EMRIs of different masses. However, the mEMRI mass ratio is quantised by the resonance order and is larger than the true mass ratio. The degeneracy can be broken if the true sBH mass ratio can be inferred from individual strain amplitudes. We argue that mEMRIs should be common if disc driven EMRIs are to be detected by LISA, with $\sim 10$ mEMRIs per year in the local universe ($z \lesssim 0.2$), highlighting the need to model mEMRI systems in future LISA data analysis.

We report a rapid optical changing-look event in the Seyfert galaxy NGC 3227: the optical continuum faded by more than 70% and broad H-beta emission nearly disappeared within weeks, before recovering within two months. Damped random walk simulations calibrated to the source's normal variability show that such a decline is unlikely for stochastic accretion fluctuations (p = 0.002). Simultaneous Swift monitoring reveals X-ray spectral hardening, pointing to enhanced line-of-sight absorption. During the dim state, reverberation mapping yields a persistent 6-8 day lag between the continuum and broad H-beta, showing that the broad-line region continued to reverberate and placing the obscurer interior to the broad-line region. Photoionization calculations require a fully ionized, Compton-thick column with N_H ~ 1.5 x 10^24 cm^-2 to reproduce the optical continuum suppression. Together, these observations demonstrate that compact, high-column-density obscurers interior to the broad-line region can produce extreme optical changing-look behavior on week timescales.

We study the supernova (SN) shock breakout (SBO) from an optically thick stellar wind. The SN ejecta-wind interaction forms a forward shock (FS) plus reverse shock (RS) structure. The RS's internal energy and shock velocity are both a fraction $μ\lesssim1/6$ of the FS's. The lower RS velocity makes it easier to build up local thermal equilibrium (LTE) and generate blackbody (BB) emission. In the same time a $μ^{-1}\gtrsim6$ times larger non-BB emission may be produced by the likely non-LTE FS. We apply the theory to analyze the recent detected type Ic-BL SN2026gzf associated X-ray flash EP260321a, and find it is naturally accounted for by a SN wind breakout: the $\sim0.13$ keV, thermal SBO emission can be produced by the RS during the interaction of a typical type Ic-BL SN ejecta ($E_k\sim10^{52}$erg and $M_e\sim1M_\odot$) and a stellar wind with a mass $M_w\sim10^{-4}M_\odot$ confined within a radius $r_w\sim10^{13}$cm; the X-ray rise time is mainly determined by the RS emission time, whereas the long duration and decrease time are due to the scatterings of SBO photons by a distant shell-like medium which has a large photosphere, $r_{ph}\sim10^{14}$ cm; the early, within 30 ks, optical emission can be naturally explained by the cooling emission of a shocked pure C/O envelope, since the observed SBO onset is delayed by $\gtrsim$ a few ks due to the large medium photosphere. BB+non-BB components from RS and FS, respectively, may be common in SN wind breakouts. SBO observations can be crucial in the study of progenitor stars by providing information for the ejecta from the SN explosion and the nearby wind from the pre-SN activities.

How general relativity stacks up: Testing gravity by coherently combining gravitational-wave observations

A core mission of gravitational-wave astronomy is to test general relativity (GR) in the high-curvature, high-velocity regime. The field has already seen significant progress in this space, though a remaining challenge is how to combine data from a population of binary mergers. This can both boost the significance of potential GR violations, and develop a phenomenological picture of how deviations manifest in different mergers. We propose a framework to coherently combine gravitational-wave measurements to build a population-level test of physics beyond GR. We assume that deviations from GR are (i) strongest near the merger, (ii) vary smoothly with the total mass of the binary, and (iii) are most prominent in the $\ell=|m|=2$ mode. While these assumptions do not describe every possible deviation of GR, they provide us with a plausible starting point upon which a more general framework can be built. We apply our framework to 28 high signal-to-noise ratio binary black hole mergers from LIGO-Virgo-KAGRA's Fifth Gravitational-Wave Transient Catalog, finding no evidence for deviations from GR. Our framework allows one to show how deviations could vary as a function of total mass of the system. We argue that such a reconstruction can be used to convincingly distinguish between systematic waveform errors and genuine GR violations.
