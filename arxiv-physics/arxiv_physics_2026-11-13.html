A normalized two-point function with several poles does not, on its own, fix the source-free thermal partition function. For the Gaussian measure of the original gluon field, a positive-residue rational propagator contributes determinants from its poles and from zeros interlaced between them. The zero factors distinguish this completion from a gas of independent pole fields and restore two ultraviolet polarizations per adjoint eigenvalue at every finite truncation. We apply the construction to a background-covariant completion of the elliptic Dyson-Schwinger gluon kernel. The first-order SU(3) transition is already present with a single pole, and the saturated six-pole result shifts $T_c/M_g$ by only $1.48\%$, where $T_c$ is the coexistence temperature and $M_g$ is the lowest pole mass. At six poles, the zero determinants raise $T_c/M_g$ by $2.31\%$ and lower $L/T_c^4$ by $12.4\%$, where $L$ is the latent heat and each prescription uses its own $T_c$. At fixed $M_g$ the dimensional latent heat decreases by $4.0\%$. The fixed-holonomy free-energy difference gives an ultraviolet test independent of the additive pressure normalization. At fixed pole number $P$, its asymptotic value agrees with the two-polarization Weiss result when the zeros are retained and is $(3P-1)/2$ times as large in the poles-only prescription.

We develop a spinorial symmetry framework for metric Dirac structures in $H$-twisted exact Courant algebroids. A generalized metric equips a Dirac structure with an intrinsic Riemannian Lie-algebroid geometry and identifies it isometrically with the positive subbundle of the generalized tangent bundle. For sections preserving the generalized metric, we prove an exact intertwining between the positive-spinor Kosmann--Dorfman action and the intrinsic Kosmann derivative. The comparison is independent of the auxiliary compatible generalized connection. Within the intrinsic geometry we derive Killing--Yano (KY) and conformal Killing--Yano (CKY) integrability identities, construct Killing-spinor-preserving operators from odd special KY and even special closed CKY forms, and obtain these forms from Hermitian bilinears of Killing spinors with imaginary Killing number. We illustrate the framework on a global Dirac structure over $S^2\times S^1$ with nontrivial $H$-flux class and rank-two anchor. Its spin connection and symmetry operators are explicit, and its Killing-spinor space is a transverse family $C^\infty(S^1,\mathbb C^2)$. Finally, intrinsic Killing sections act on Killing spinors by a Lie algebra representation. For a graph Dirac structure over $S^3$, the odd Jacobi identity of the Hermitian-current bracket fails by a phase, and adjoining a single even phase generator yields a Lie superalgebra. These results connect generalized isometries, hidden spinorial symmetries, and flux-sensitive Lie-algebroid geometry.

We investigate inducing data for three dimensional asymptotically flat BMS gravity on hyperbolic Virasoro orbits with winding, which contain no constant supermomentum, and the restriction of formal smooth equivariant sections to global Lorentz orbits. Using the developing map of the associated Hill operator we rederive their stabilizer and show that, in the spin double cover, it is the direct product of a line with a cyclic group of even order. The character of the cyclic factor is therefore a discrete inducing label independent of the continuous one, unlike for the massive BMS particles, where it is fixed by the spin. At points with the corresponding finite rotational symmetry, restriction of formal smooth equivariant sections to a global Lorentz orbit yields the finite cyclic equivariance condition, while an independently defined unitary global branch realizes the associated finite difference equation. At points with timelike projected momentum we derive the resulting ladder of Poincare helicities, show that the projected rest energy is not an orbit invariant, construct locally flat winding BMS geometries with hyperbolic Lorentz holonomy, prove that the smooth real periodic near horizon current model excludes these winding orbits, and obtain selection rules and closed form expressions for finite monodromy resolved orbital functionals. A unitary realization on the full infinite dimensional orbit is not constructed, and the interpretation of the cyclic sectors in quantum gravity remains conjectural.

We introduce a geometric construction whose terminal configuration determines the kernel and cokernel of a morphism between positive Lagrangian branes in the derived Fukaya category of a Calabi-Yau manifold. Thus, if the construction always terminates, the full subcategory of positive Lagrangian branes is abelian, as conjectured by Joyce. We show that this abelian category admits Harder-Narasimhan filtrations with respect to the central charge defined by the holomorphic volume form. Under appropriate split-generation assumptions, we construct a Bridgeland stability condition whose stable objects are isomorphic to special Lagrangian branes, as predicted by the Thomas-Yau conjecture. The geometric construction is based on a one-parameter family of special Lagrangian submanifolds with boundary. One part of the boundary is contained in the Lagrangian underlying the domain of the morphism and the other in the Lagrangian underlying the codomain. Surgeries occur as the family encounters singularities or obstruction walls. We prove that the construction always terminates for the two-torus and provide higher dimensional examples.

In this paper, we have generalized Poisson's(1993) calculation for the case of gravitational radiation from a particle in a circular orbit around a black hole to the case of the wormhole. Considering the motion of a test particle around a Schwarzschild like wormhole and using Teukolsky formalism, we have obtained an analytical result of flux at infinity under the assumption of small velocities. Focusing on the 22-mode, we have found a novel 0.5PN order term beyond the quadrupole order, which vanishes in the black hole limit. On the other hand, all higher order terms are getting modified in such a way that they give correct correspondence in the black hole limit. We have further corroborated our results with the numerical investigations, which show that the two waveforms match well when the particle lies far from ISCO. Thus, our work provides one of the first attempts toward the study of the post-Newtonian waveforms involving wormholes (ECOs).

We investigate quantum gate teleportation as a primitive for satellite-enabled distributed quantum computing. In the considered architecture, a satellite distributes entangled photon pairs to two optical ground stations, which transfer the shared quantum resource to remote quantum processing units. This entanglement is then used to implement a nonlocal controlled-NOT gate between two circuit qubits located in different processors. We formulate the minimal four-qubit protocol, consisting of two network qubits that provide the entangled resource and two circuit qubits on which the distributed gate is performed. Atmospheric propagation effects in the gravitational field are modeled through an effective two-qubit depolarizing channel whose parameters depend on atmospheric and optical quantities, including the Fried parameter and receiver aperture. The performance of the protocol is quantified by the fidelity between the ideal CNOT output state and the state produced by noisy quantum gate teleportation. Our results show that the fidelity is governed by the quality of the distributed entanglement and depends jointly on atmospheric coherence, aperture size, and effective depolarization strength. These findings connect physical-layer modeling of satellite optical channels with gate-level performance in distributed quantum computing.

We develop a gauge-independent theory of magnetic reconnection beyond the standard special-relativistic framework of inertial systems in flat spacetime. Our definitions of reconnection and its rate are independent of reference frame and coordinate system, allowing physical effects of gravity and net linear acceleration to be distinguished from coordinate effects. We extend the mesoscale theory of Liu et al. [PRL 118, 085101 (2017)], in which the reconnection rate depends on physics both within the diffusion region and in an upstream ideal, force-free region. The theory identifies three non-inertial and curved-spacetime (NICS) effects, including gravitationally induced magnetic tension: gravitational distortion of the reconnection plane changes the curvature of magnetic field lines. Applying the theory to a simplified accretion plasma near )a compact object, we find that (1) whether gravity increases or decreases the reconnection rate depends on the system's orientation relative to the gravitational source, with the greatest increase occurring when the outflow jet points toward or away from the source; and (2) the magnitude of the gravitational effect scales with the dimensionless parameter $\barκ \equiv r_sΔy^2/r^3$, where $r$ is the Schwarzschild radial coordinate, $r_s$ is the source's Schwarzschild radius, and $Δy$ is the mesoscale length.

Energetic-particle irradiation can produce persistent charge separation within lunar regolith, but the internal fields predicted by subsurface-charging models depend on how particle interactions are translated into retained charge. This critical review examines the evidence and assumptions connecting microscopic charge formation to macroscopic charging. We distinguish energy deposition, net charge transfer across a specified boundary, and the spatial distribution of signed excess charge. We organize these distinctions using an average retained-charge response defined for specified material, irradiation, electrical-state, and evaluation conditions. Its use as a volumetric source requires consistent charge accounting, compatible incident-flux definitions, and an adequate separation between response formation and subsequent transport. Applying these criteria to existing models, emission studies, and silicate irradiation experiments yields three assessments. First, emission measurements constrain electron loss across the measured boundary but do not locate internally redistributed charge. Second, evidence of internal reabsorption does not by itself establish that the charge separation sustaining subsurface fields is reduced; an illustrative calculation shows that the same net charge supply can produce different peak fields depending on transfer depths. Third, laboratory electrical damage in silicate crystals does not determine breakdown rates in cryogenic, porous regolith, which additionally require constraints on local fields, failure properties, and discharge recurrence. Joint constraints on boundary charge transfer, spatial charge separation, and post-irradiation relaxation are central to evaluating lunar subsurface-charging predictions.

A proposed resolution of the black-hole information paradox must explain which information an outside observer can recover from escaping radiation\cite{Hawking,Page,Hayden,Almheiri}. Environmental memory can return correlations to the source without delivering them to that observer\cite{ChenQi,ChenMemory}. Here we establish a sharp recovery frontier at fixed mean radiation yield for single-excitation channels and realize its qubit limit with an autonomous quantum-memory emitter. Unequal transmissions balance retained coherence against information in failed collection events. A reference--radiation correlation test distinguishes this gain even when the mean collected energy is unchanged. Identical transmission channels instead give the same recovery at matched collection, regardless of their emission timescales. For a broader class of interacting sources, an ordering of collected and inaccessible coupling spectra forbids unassisted quantum transmission irrespective of internal memory. A separate coding example supports a positive rate where uniform transmission at matched actual yield has zero capacity. Together, these results identify what openness must change to assist recovery: the accessible transmission structure. They supply a physically constrained comparison of evaporation mechanisms, separating information delivered to radiation from coherence retained elsewhere or an increase in the collected output.

TianQin, a space-borne gravitational wave observatory, is designed to detect gravitational-wave signals via laser interferometry. As the inhomogeneous space plasma surrounds the inter-satellite laser links, the propagation of the laser in space plasma can introduce pointing error noise. In this work, we quantitatively assess the effect of plasma on the pointing accuracy of TQ's inter-satellite laser links. We employ a magnetohydrodynamic model to simulate the plasma distribution around the TQ orbit for different orbital orientations. Using the fourth-order Runge-Kutta method to conduct the ray-tracing calculations over a TQ orbital cycle under a solar wind dynamic pressure close to the 23-year average, we find that the plasma-induced laser deflection angle and pointing eccentricity are on the order of $10^{-20} \ \mathrm{rad}$ and picometers, respectively. These values are significantly lower than TQ's requirements. Our results demonstrate that the impact of space plasma on TQ's pointing error is negligible in moderate space weather conditions, indicating a positive outlook for the TQ mission.

The Zwicky Transient Facility (ZTF) has observed the northern sky observable from Palomar Observatory since March 2018, accumulating a median of a thousand photometric measurements across $gri$ for each of more than a billion sources. In a neutron star low-mass X-ray binary, heavy elements (with mass number ~100) are believed to be synthesized through the rapid-proton (rp) capture process, as indirectly indicated by the long tail of X-ray burst light curves. Although direct evidence for the rp-process was lacking so far, we reported in our previous study that an unidentified hump is present at ~30 keV of the Suzaku spectra of Aquila X-1 in its hard state. The feature was tentatively identified with a recombination quasi-continuum from highly ionized ions of cadmium or indium, which are possibly produced by the rp process during X-ray bursts and lifted up into the neutron-star atmosphere. To reinforce this scenario, we quantitatively evaluated the physical conditions of the assumed emitting region in the atmosphere, and confirmed that the scenario is possible when the putative rp-process ashes in the atmosphere has ~10^7 times higher abundances than their solar values. The results are compared with theoretical model calculations for the nucleosynthesis in X-ray bursts. We suggest that the observed spectral hump is a radiative recombination feature from the stable isotope 106Cd, produced in X-ray bursts via the rp-process. Further issues about the detectability are briefly discussed.

We investigate the excitation of a second-order exceptional point (EP) in the quasinormal mode (QNM) spectrum by a plunging particle. Starting from a gravitational action, we derive the master equation with a Gaussian bump modified potential. For the odd-parity mode $(\ell,m)=(2,1)$, we compute the waveform numerically and derive an analytic expression for the EP contribution to the ringdown waveform directly from the particle source. Comparison with the numerical waveform shows that the particle excites the EP response, which, together with its mirror contribution, dominates the intermediate ringdown stage. We use waveform fitting and Bayesian analysis to assess the limitations of conventional QNM templates for EP ringdown. In the fitting, the EP template recovers the complex frequency $ω$ more accurately than the ordinary template. In Bayesian analysis, at a signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) of $ρ=100$, the ordinary template yields a narrow frequency posterior whose $90\%$ credible region excludes the true EP frequency, whereas the broader EP-template region contains it. The Bayes factor nevertheless favors the ordinary template at lower SNRs and the EP template only at higher SNRs. These results show that in the EP case a narrow posterior and favorable Bayesian evidence do not guarantee accurate frequency inference when a conventional template omits the characteristic EP ringdown structure.

We discuss constraints that the X-ray emission from supernovae can place on their progenitor stars. Since almost all X-ray supernovae arise from massive stars, we concentrate on implications for massive stars. These stars lose mass throughout their lifetime in the form of stellar winds. X-ray emission can be used to gauge the wind properties, especially the mass-loss rates, via various techniques. The abundance of various elements formed by nucleosynthesis within the stars, and released in the stellar explosion, can be estimated from X-ray spectra. In many cases, a combination of X-ray light curves and spectra can allow us to extricate the structure and morphology of the surrounding medium formed by pre-supernova mass loss into which the SN shock is expanding.

High-resolution X-ray spectroscopy with XRISM has resolved line profiles of highly ionised ions, including P Cygni profiles, in many systems containing compact objects. To interpret these line profiles, forward modelling with three-dimensional radiative transfer (3D RT) calculations including the relevant ionic processes is required. Following our previous work on the Lyman-series lines of H-like ions, we present the implementation of the K-series lines of He-like ions with Z = 2-30 in the open-source Monte Carlo RT code SKIRT, providing a numerical tool for interpreting highresolution X-ray spectra of photoionised plasmas. Building upon the framework developed for H-like ions, we incorporate the resonance scattering (RS) and radiative recombination (RR) channels. We also implement the inner-shell ionisation (II) channel of Li-like ions for the production of He-like lines. We resolve fine-structure levels and account for level populations, frequency redistribution, phase function, and linear polarisation through transition-specific Mueller matrices. The implementation reproduces the expected RT effects in He-like lines. The Fe xxv w and y lines, dominated by the RS channel, develop optically thick line-profile distortions and P Cygni profiles in an outflowing plasma, whereas the x and z lines, dominated by the RR and II channels, do not exhibit such behaviour. The line intensities from the RS and RR channels agree well with Cloudy. The II channel further enhances the z line, which is not yet included in Cloudy. The linear polarisation calculations reproduce the analytical and experimental angular dependence of resonance scattering on a line-by-line basis. This extension to He-like lines, together with our previous implementation of H-like lines, enables 3D RT modelling of the major X-ray lines, providing a tool for constraining gas kinematics around compact objects observed with XRISM.

Short gamma-ray bursts (sGRBs) are believed to originate from compact binary mergers, particularly binary neutron star (BNS) mergers, providing a probe of jet energetics and merger remnants. We compile broadband afterglow observations of 40 sGRBs and perform forward-shock modeling with off-axis Gaussian jets, excluding data potentially affected by additional energy injection. We derive distributions of beaming-corrected jet kinetic energy $E_k$ and radiative efficiency $η_γ$, and combine these with binary population synthesis and numerical simulations to infer remnant black hole (BH) magnetic fields assuming the Blandford-Znajek (BZ) jet-launching mechanism. For the 15 strictly constrained bursts, we find a median $E_k\simeq3.8\times10^{49}$ erg and $η_γ\simeq0.35$, with respective 68% intervals of $[5.1\times10^{48},1.9\times10^{50}]$ erg and [0.09, 0.79]. The inferred magnetic field supplied by the accretion disk and threading the BH horizon is approximately $7\times10^{15}$ G, with a 68% credible interval of $[2.3\times10^{15},2.4\times10^{16}]$ G, depending slightly on the neutron star equation of state. The full sample yields a higher median field of approximately $2\times10^{16}$ G, with the high-energy and high-field tails predominantly contributed by less well-constrained bursts. These results provide statistical constraints on sGRB jet energetics and suggest characteristic BH magnetic fields of order $10^{16}$ G. Comparison with pre-merger neutron star magnetic fields indicates a characteristic amplification factor of $η_B\sim10^3$, providing an observational constraint on magnetic field amplification during BNS mergers and subsequent BH formation.
