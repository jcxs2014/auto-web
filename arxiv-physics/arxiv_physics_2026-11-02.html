Let $L_n$ be the finite-dimensional irreducible $\mathfrak{sl}_2$-module of highest weight $n$. We study the space of highest-weight vectors in $L_n\otimes L_m$ for the diagonal $\mathfrak{sl}_2$-action. The extremal projector constructs these singular vectors and gives operators connecting the highest-weight lines in the Clebsch-Gordan decomposition. We interpret these operators in the Mickelsson-Zhelobenko algebra of the diagonal pair $(\mathfrak{sl}_2\oplus\mathfrak{sl}_2, \mathfrak{sl}_2^{\mathrm{diag}})$. A rescaling of their quadratic relation yields the cubic Smith relation $[E,F]=2(H^3-cH)$. This cubic algebra is also known as a Higgs algebra in the physics literature. We express both the central parameter $c$ and the Smith Casimir in terms of the quadratic $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ Casimir elements of the two tensor factors. The singular subspace becomes an irreducible Smith module of dimension $\min\{n,m\}+1$, with explicit formulas for the actions of the raising and lowering operators $E$ and $F$. Finally, comparison with finite-dimensional simple Smith modules gives a necessary and sufficient condition for a module to arise from this tensor-product construction.

Subregions in gauge theories give rise to edge modes and corner charges supported on their boundaries. While this is well-established classically and in quantum lattice gauge theories, the fate of corner modes remains poorly understood in continuum quantum field theories owing to their distributional nature. In this paper, we revisit this challenge and propose a new corner operator for a regional Cauchy slice algebra generated by local gauge-invariant observables of scalar QED in Minkowski spacetime. The corner mode is given by the longitudinal electric field sourced by the matter outside the subregion and thus constitutes a centre for the regional algebra. Our main goal is to show that there exists a perturbative GNS state on this algebra that quantises this mode. Pulling back the Fock vacuum to the slice, we construct this state by renormalising the divergences arising from the lack of temporal smearing. We clarify that the inclusion of the new centre mode necessitates a further renormalisation on the corner of the region, thereby physically distinguishing it and breaking the translation invariance of the state. Our work shows how the requirements of locality, renormalisability, and positivity together put strong constraints on the existence of a continuum quantum field theory which supports corner modes as genuine quantum operators. We finish with comments about the von Neumann completion of the regional algebra, as well as possible physical interpretations of the renormalised vacuum state.

We propose a phenomenological framework toward a Three-Body Holonomy Standard Model in which the number three is selected by the minimal primitive cyclic structure of a democratic many-body interaction network. The resulting threefold holonomy information is assumed to survive in $(3+1)$ dimensions as an internal three-state space. A Lorentz-invariant six-fermion interaction provides a candidate microscopic origin of the internal cyclic correlations, with their matching to Wilson holonomy left as a dynamical requirement. The parity-related holonomy sectors of the underlying $(1+1)$-dimensional Dirac system motivate left- and right-handed internal structures and a trinification-like $SU(3)_C\times SU(3)_L\times SU(3)_R$ gauge completion. Vertical holonomy selects a $2+1$ splitting, while spontaneous left-right vacuum misalignment provides a possible route to chiral symmetry breaking. Horizontal holonomy organizes the three-family mass structure, with heavy-fermion mixing generating effective flavor and CP-violating patterns. Although a complete ultraviolet derivation remains open, the framework provides a unified scenario connecting primitive three-body dynamics to gauge symmetry, symmetry breaking, and fermion flavor.

Quantum geometry is conventionally understood in parameter or momentum space and need not be related to a geometry in configuration space. Here we show for Dirac fermions in a background electromagnetic field that an induced Lorentzian geometry is generated from the fermionic quantum metric. We study a Lorentz covariant phase space worldline path integral construction, whose application of the adiabatic theorem onto spin degrees of freedom leads to the appearance of the Berry phase at $O(\hbar)$, and the quantum metric at $O(\hbar^2)$ in the worldline action. Both the Feynman and retarded/advanced propagators are examined in 3+1 and 2+1 dimensions, whose different on-shell implementations lead to related quantum metrics; QED/QED${}_3$ and condensed matter Dirac theory are considered. An effective theory is found by integrating out the momenta degrees of freedom, which gives rise to an emergent Lorentzian metric in configuration space, whose perturbation to the flat metric is dependent on the quantum metric and the background field strength. Apart from the spin degrees of freedom encoded into the Berry phase, the effective theory describes a scalar particle moving in the induced quantum geometry. Phenomenological applications to the covariant geometry are further studied using spectral holonomy in a gapped 3-dimensional Dirac material.

Ekholm and Shende's skeins-on-branes formalism provides invariant counts of holomorphic curves with boundary on a Maslov zero Lagrangian within a Calabi-Yau 3-fold. This paper proves the orientation relations required for worldsheet skein relations to cancel codimension-one boundary strata. However, standard Cauchy-Riemann Fredholm orientations reveal an unexpected sign puzzle: the orientation relation at a curve with a hyperbolic boundary crossing depends on whether the crossing lies on distinct boundary components or the same component. This domain-dependent sign contradicts the uniform crossing relations of the HOMFLYPT skein algebra. We show that this discrepancy is resolved by passing to an anti-holomorphic orientation convention. We establish all required orientation comparisons in the general setting of an oriented Lagrangian submanifold of a 6-dimensional symplectic manifold.

We study the light nucleus production in pp collisions at $\sqrt{s}=7$ and $13$~TeV reported by the ALICE Collaboration within the color string fragmentation framework. We adopt the Schwinger mechanism with string tension fluctuations as the particle production mechanism describing the $p_T$ spectrum. The approximately thermal behavior observed in the light-nucleus spectra motivates the use of a perturbative series expansion of the $q$-Gaussian string tension fluctuations around $q=1$. The resulting $p_T$ spectrum is expressed as a perturbative series expansion around the thermal distribution with nonthermal corrections given by Gamma distributions, providing closed forms for the effective temperature, moments, and cumulants. In the superstatistical framework, the associated temperature fluctuations show that the medium in which light nuclei are produced is out of equilibrium. Additionally, we derive closed formulas for the coalescence parameter at arbitrary order, which agree well with experimental data. Our results also show that the mass--temperature relation ($T_N/m_N \approx AT_A/m_A$) between the nucleons and nuclei is not preserved in the coalescence process.

TianQin, a space-borne gravitational wave observatory, is designed to detect gravitational-wave signals via laser interferometry. As the inhomogeneous space plasma surrounds the inter-satellite laser links, the propagation of the laser in space plasma can introduce pointing error noise. In this work, we quantitatively assess the effect of plasma on the pointing accuracy of TQ's inter-satellite laser links. We employ a magnetohydrodynamic model to simulate the plasma distribution around the TQ orbit for different orbital orientations. Using the fourth-order Runge-Kutta method to conduct the ray-tracing calculations over a TQ orbital cycle under a solar wind dynamic pressure close to the 23-year average, we find that the plasma-induced laser deflection angle and pointing eccentricity are on the order of $10^{-20} \ \mathrm{rad}$ and picometers, respectively. These values are significantly lower than TQ's requirements. Our results demonstrate that the impact of space plasma on TQ's pointing error is negligible in moderate space weather conditions, indicating a positive outlook for the TQ mission.

We use the core-radius, $r_γ,$ of a galaxy's central core along with a volume-limited sample of brightest cluster galaxies (BCG) to determine the $z=0$ mass distribution function of the most massive super-massive black holes (SMBH). We recover a $M_{BH}-r_γ$ relationship between the SMBH mass, $M_{BH},$ and $r_γ$ of form $M_{BH} \approx1.3\times10^{10}~(r_γ/{\mathrm{kpc}})~M_\odot,$ with $σ= 0.25\pm0.03$ dex scatter in $M_{BH}.$ We derive a core distribution function (CDF), based on HST and DLS images of the BCGs. The CDF falls steeply as $N(r_γ)\sim r_γ^{-3}$ for $r_γ>0.50$ kpc. Only $\sim5\%$ of BCGs have $r_γ>1$ kpc. Combining the CDF and $M_{BH} - r_γ$ relationship yields the BH mass distribution function (MDF). For $M_{BH} \geq 7\times10^{9}~\mathrm{M_\odot}$ (the mean $M_{BH}$ at $r_γ=0.50$ kpc) we derive: $\log\left(\frac{dN(M_{BH})}{d{M_{BH}/10^{10} M_\odot}}~[\rm Mpc^{-3}]\right)=(-3.05\pm0.42)~\log\left(\frac{M_{BH}}{10^{10}M_\odot}\right)~-~5.11\ (+0.41, -0.36)$. Cores with $r_γ\geq 0.50$ kpc are found almost exclusively in BCGs, implying that SMBHs with $M_{BH} \geq7\times10^{9}~\mathrm{M_\odot}$ may also be exclusive to BCGs. Core-radius correlates with $α,$ a parameter that characterizes the growth of BCG envelopes by mergers, suggesting that the most massive SMBHs grew in tandem with their BCG hosts. The BH MDF embodies a significant population of SMBH with $M_{BH} >7\times10^{9} M_\odot.$ When combined with the Liepold & Ma (2024) BH MDF to account for SMBH with $M_{BH} < 7\times10^{9} M_\odot,$ it can comfortably explain the nHz gravitational wave background.

Local star-forming regions have recently been the subject of scrutiny as cosmic-ray accelerators and sources of ultra-high-energy gamma-ray emissions. The Cygnus-X region is one of the richest star forming regions in the galaxy and over the last few decades has been extensively studied. Here we report the detection of a star-forming region located in the southern part of Cygnus-X, Sharpless 106 (S106), using the HAWC Observatory. The S106 region is spatially located near the cataloged gamma-ray source 1LHAASO J2027+3657. In this work, we perform a multi-wavelength analysis of S106, by combining HAWC data with re-analysis of Fermi-LAT and archival Chandra data. We construct a fully leptonic model for the multi-wavelength emission from S106 from a single population of high-energy leptons, with the gamma-rays greater than 1 TeV being produced by inverse Compton scattering of the locally-produced IR photon field from S106 itself. Our models show that hadronic mechanisms are not always required to explain the observed flux from gamma-ray star-forming regions and that understanding the specific details of local photon fields is crucial to modeling the multi-wavelength emission from star-forming regions.

We present the results from a high-cadence, multi-channel time-domain survey targeting the Coma galaxy cluster, utilizing the newly commissioned 1.6-m Multi-channel Photometric Survey Telescope (Mephisto). Mephisto's pioneering mosaic focal-plane design enables strictly simultaneous imaging in three distinct optical bands ($u/v, g/r, i/z$). This unique capability allows for the extraction of true, instantaneous spectral energy distributions (SEDs) of fast-evolving transients. Our survey continuously monitors a 20-square-degree footprint centered on the Coma cluster. By strategically targeting this massive local structure, we exploit cosmic anisotropy to significantly amplify the volumetric discovery rate of stellar explosions. During the initial six-month pilot operation, we successfully detected and tracked nine new supernovae (SNe). Remarkably, our high-cadence, multi-band strategy allowed us to capture ultra-early rising light curves and pre-discovery signals (e.g., SN 2026dyr) hours to days prior to formal discovery alerts. These instantaneous early-phase colors allow us to reconstruct intrinsic SEDs without temporal interpolation, providing reliable constraints on the physical parameters of early supernova (SN) evolution. Furthermore, our observed supernova yield shows good agreement with theoretical forecasts based on local stellar mass and star-formation rate overdensities. These pilot results validate the efficiency of the Mephisto Coma survey, demonstrating its objective capability to systematically explore the local transient population and provide rapid optical localizations for future multi-messenger events.

Kilohertz quasi-periodic oscillations (QPOs) have been observed in a few rare gamma-ray bursts (GRBs), but their physical origin remains uncertain. We reanalyze the two short GRBs with reported kilohertz QPOs, GRB 910711 and GRB 200415A, and find evidence for possible harmonic-like organization around a characteristic frequency scale near 360 hertz. The similar harmonic-like pattern in GRB 910711 and in the confirmed magnetar giant flare (MGF) event GRB 200415A, including features extending above 4 kilohertz, points to timing phenomenology on frequency scales relevant to magnetar oscillation modes. The temporal and spectral similarities between the two events and the difficulty of explaining these features in merger-driven scenarios favor an MGF-like interpretation of GRB 910711. This timing phenomenology may therefore provide an additional observational handle, complementary to spectral, temporal, and localization information, for distinguishing rare MGF-like transients from merger-driven GRBs.
