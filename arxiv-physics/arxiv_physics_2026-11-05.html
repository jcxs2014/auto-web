We reformulate two-dimensional conformal field theory (2d CFT) in the language of noncommutative geometry. To this end, we introduce automorphic forms for the von Neumann algebraic quantum group $\mathrm{SL}^+_b(2,\mathbb{R})$. A quantum measure space is a von Neumann algebra equipped with a weight. We study such spaces carrying a weight-preserving action of $\mathrm{SL}^+_b(2,\mathbb{R})$. We argue that every unitary bosonic 2d CFT with central charge $c=1+6(b+b^{-1})^2>1$ is equivalent to a quantum measure space with an action of $\mathrm{SL}^+_b(2,\mathbb{R})\times\mathrm{SL}^+_b(2,\mathbb{R})$. In this dictionary, Virasoro primary operators are quantum automorphic forms and their scaling dimensions correspond to the spectrum of a quantum Laplace operator. The Virasoro crossing equations express associativity of multiplication on the quantum measure space. For compact theories, the algebra is a type III$_1$ factor. Liouville CFT is realized as $\mathrm{SL}^+_b(2,\mathbb{R})$ acting on itself by left and right translations. Virasoro boundary conditions correspond to quantum measure spaces with an action of a single copy of $\mathrm{SL}^+_b(2,\mathbb{R})$. When the boundary spectrum is discrete, these are noncommutative counterparts of compact hyperbolic surfaces. The formulation accommodates models both with and without extended chiral algebras. It bridges the Virasoro conformal bootstrap with categorical and von Neumann algebraic approaches to 2d CFT.

We construct the free gluon and Faddeev--Popov ghost propagators in rigidly rotating cylindrical coordinates using an eigenfunction approach within the Feynman gauge. Starting from the quadratic gauge-fixed QCD action, we derive the corresponding kinetic operators and obtain their spectral representations. For the gluon field, a circular polarization basis diagonalizes the vector wave operator, leading to polarization-dependent shifts in the orders of the radial Bessel functions. In the absence of radial boundaries, we explicitly recover the standard Minkowski propagator in inertial Cartesian coordinates, showing that the off-diagonal tensor components in the cylindrical basis have a geometrical origin. We then extend the construction to a finite-radius cylinder satisfying the causality condition $|Ω|R<1$, imposing relative boundary conditions on the gluon field and Dirichlet conditions on the ghosts. The resulting discrete transverse spectra involve both Dirichlet and Neumann eigenvalues for the gluon modes, from which we obtain explicit spectral representations of the propagators. Our results provide a consistent framework for perturbative QCD calculations in rotating geometries, retaining the angular-momentum, polarization, and boundary effects relevant to future studies of correlation functions and radiative corrections.

We study a real massless scalar field in $d+1$ dimensions confined to a wedge of opening angle $α$, in the presence of a semitransparent circular arc that may oscillate radially. Within the worldline formulation, and for an arbitrary strength $λ$ of the $δ$-potential that models the arc, we expand the effective action in powers of the radial displacement $R$ of the arc. In this formulation, all the information about the media is contained in the propagator of a non-relativistic point particle, so that known results for that propagator can be used directly. At zeroth order we obtain the Casimir energy of the wedge, with Dirichlet, Neumann or mixed walls, and the contribution of the arc in closed form for arbitrary $λ$. We show that the worldline integrand is, point by point, the energy density obtained from the energy-momentum tensor of the scalar field with curvature coupling $ξ=1/4$; with this identification, and using dimensional regularization for the integrated quantities, we recover the known results for the wedge and for the wedge closed by a Dirichlet arc. At second order in $R$, we reduce the form factor that determines the imaginary part of the in-out effective action, and hence the probability of pair creation by the moving arc, to a one-loop expression involving the radial Green's functions of each angular sector. In the narrow-wedge limit, each angular sector behaves as a field with a mass set by the angular quantization, coupled to a moving $δ$-mirror; for a Dirichlet arc, the pair creation spectrum of the exterior region approaches that of a planar massive mirror, while the interior region behaves as a tapered cavity. Finally, by decomposing the electromagnetic field into TE and TM scalars, we obtain the Casimir energy of wedges with perfectly conducting, perfectly permeable and mixed walls.

We study non-Einstein Bach-flat Taub-NUT/Bolt instantons with weakened AdS boundary conditions. In particular, the next-to-leading-order coefficient in the Fefferman--Graham expansion is nonvanishing, and it sources the partially massless response in the dual conformal field theory. As in the Einstein case, the holographic stress tensor of these instantons represents a perfect fluid with vorticity. Remarkably, we identify a non-Einstein Taub-Bolt branch supporting a nonvanishing partially massless response at zero source, while preserving the full symmetry of the squashed conformal boundary. The additional holographic degrees of freedom enrich the thermodynamic phase structure, allowing the conventional NUT/Bolt transition to disappear or give way to reentrant transitions. We further show that non-Einstein Bolts can possess negative thermodynamic volume and establish the absence of negative modes of a conformally coupled probe scalar on regular Bach-flat NUT backgrounds.

A proposed resolution of the black-hole information paradox must explain which information an outside observer can recover from escaping radiation\cite{Hawking,Page,Hayden,Almheiri}. Environmental memory can return correlations to the source without delivering them to that observer\cite{ChenQi,ChenMemory}. Here we establish a sharp recovery frontier at fixed mean radiation yield for single-excitation channels and realize its qubit limit with an autonomous quantum-memory emitter. Unequal transmissions balance retained coherence against information in failed collection events. A reference--radiation correlation test distinguishes this gain even when the mean collected energy is unchanged. Identical transmission channels instead give the same recovery at matched collection, regardless of their emission timescales. For a broader class of interacting sources, an ordering of collected and inaccessible coupling spectra forbids unassisted quantum transmission irrespective of internal memory. A separate coding example supports a positive rate where uniform transmission at matched actual yield has zero capacity. Together, these results identify what openness must change to assist recovery: the accessible transmission structure. They supply a physically constrained comparison of evaporation mechanisms, separating information delivered to radiation from coherence retained elsewhere or an increase in the collected output.

TianQin, a space-borne gravitational wave observatory, is designed to detect gravitational-wave signals via laser interferometry. As the inhomogeneous space plasma surrounds the inter-satellite laser links, the propagation of the laser in space plasma can introduce pointing error noise. In this work, we quantitatively assess the effect of plasma on the pointing accuracy of TQ's inter-satellite laser links. We employ a magnetohydrodynamic model to simulate the plasma distribution around the TQ orbit for different orbital orientations. Using the fourth-order Runge-Kutta method to conduct the ray-tracing calculations over a TQ orbital cycle under a solar wind dynamic pressure close to the 23-year average, we find that the plasma-induced laser deflection angle and pointing eccentricity are on the order of $10^{-20} \ \mathrm{rad}$ and picometers, respectively. These values are significantly lower than TQ's requirements. Our results demonstrate that the impact of space plasma on TQ's pointing error is negligible in moderate space weather conditions, indicating a positive outlook for the TQ mission.

We investigate the sensitivity of the Pierre Auger Observatory to sterile neutrinos with masses in the MeV-to-100 MeV range. To this end, we consider a minimal $3+1$ framework in which a single sterile state mixes mainly with the $ν_τ$ flavor through an active--sterile mixing angle $θ_N$ to produce a mass eigenstate $N_1$. We compute the corresponding exposures with a detailed Monte Carlo simulation of neutrino propagation, interactions, $τ$ energy losses and decay, and derive upper limits at $90\%$ confidence level on an isotropic diffuse flux of $N_1$ neutrinos.
