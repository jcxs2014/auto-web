Conditional generative surrogates in high-energy physics, such as models that predict reconstructed jets from partons, are often evaluated on $1\text{D}$ marginal divergences among other metrics. Taken alone these metrics can reward conditional collapse. On a CMS simulation benchmark, a sampler that ignores the conditioner beats a trained Mixture Density Network (MDN) on all four target marginals under both KL and Wasserstein-1, and cannot be stably separated from a Conditional Flow Matching (CFM) model on two of the four. We instead score the models with the Continuous Ranked Probability Skill Score (CRPSS) from forecast verification, taking the exact marginal as the reference. We show how global skill depends on the evaluation spectrum, and we also report it within bins of a conditioning variable, where that dependence largely goes away. We then introduce a data-driven $k$-Nearest-Neighbor ($k$-NN) extrapolation that estimates the best CRPS the conditioning information allows. Both models agree with this estimate on all four targets, which suggests that current architectures have reached the information available in the conditioner, at least as far as these per-target scores can tell.

A recent remarkable paper by OpenAI showed that the spin-one Heisenberg chain with periodic boundary conditions has a spectral gap in the thermodynamic limit. The proof elegantly deduces this gap from the Hamiltonian's spectrum on a finite system. This basic idea is an inequality involving the purity of the original Gibbs state and a dual Gibbs state that is defined via space-time rotation. Here we consider a few generalizations of this purity bootstrap argument. We first extend it to arbitrary spatial dimensions. Our main result uses the space-time duality between symmetry generators and symmetry twists to obtain upper bounds on the splitting of nearly degenerate low-energy states, together with a nonzero gap above them, from finite-system information. As applications, we show that finite-system calculations for the 2+1-D toric code in a field and the 2+1-D transverse-field Ising model establish parameter regimes with four and two low-lying states whose splittings are bounded from above by $e^{-cL}$ and $e^{-cL^2}$ respectively for arbitrarily large system size $L$, while the gap above these states remains nonzero in the thermodynamic limit. Next, we extend it to fermionic systems and use a finite calculation to establish a symmetric mass generation phase in a $2+1$-D fermionic model. Finally, we apply the bootstrap to tensor-network states and establish exponential decay of connected correlations of all local operators in the symmetric square-lattice spin-one state introduced by Jian and Zaletel.

We establish conditions for exactness of the inverse renormalization group via constrained Markov processes, a class of Monte Carlo simulations which sample the degrees of freedom within a block on a fine lattice under the constraint that they remain consistent with the corresponding degree of freedom on the coarse lattice according to a forward renormalization group transformation. We show analytically, for an arbitrary transformation, that constrained Markov processes correspond to an exact inversion of the renormalization group at the fixed point action, and to a near-exact inversion in the vicinity of the fixed point. We then optimize renormalization group transformations directly on inversely renormalized configurations to effectively map the action onto itself and ensure computational stability in the generation of configurations for high-dimensional systems. Starting from a set of uncorrelated configurations for the three-dimensional $ϕ^{4}$ quantum field theory at lattice volume $V=16^{3}$, we invert the renormalization group with constrained Monte Carlo to construct a set of uncorrelated, near-equilibrium configurations for $V=128^{3}$ and extract two critical exponents of the system. The inversion exhibits no critical slowing down, with dynamic critical exponent $z=0.03(2)$.

Energetic-particle irradiation can produce persistent charge separation within lunar regolith, but the internal fields predicted by subsurface-charging models depend on how particle interactions are translated into retained charge. This critical review examines the evidence and assumptions connecting microscopic charge formation to macroscopic charging. We distinguish energy deposition, net charge transfer across a specified boundary, and the spatial distribution of signed excess charge. We organize these distinctions using an average retained-charge response defined for specified material, irradiation, electrical-state, and evaluation conditions. Its use as a volumetric source requires consistent charge accounting, compatible incident-flux definitions, and an adequate separation between response formation and subsequent transport. Applying these criteria to existing models, emission studies, and silicate irradiation experiments yields three assessments. First, emission measurements constrain electron loss across the measured boundary but do not locate internally redistributed charge. Second, evidence of internal reabsorption does not by itself establish that the charge separation sustaining subsurface fields is reduced; an illustrative calculation shows that the same net charge supply can produce different peak fields depending on transfer depths. Third, laboratory electrical damage in silicate crystals does not determine breakdown rates in cryogenic, porous regolith, which additionally require constraints on local fields, failure properties, and discharge recurrence. Joint constraints on boundary charge transfer, spatial charge separation, and post-irradiation relaxation are central to evaluating lunar subsurface-charging predictions.

A proposed resolution of the black-hole information paradox must explain which information an outside observer can recover from escaping radiation\cite{Hawking,Page,Hayden,Almheiri}. Environmental memory can return correlations to the source without delivering them to that observer\cite{ChenQi,ChenMemory}. Here we establish a sharp recovery frontier at fixed mean radiation yield for single-excitation channels and realize its qubit limit with an autonomous quantum-memory emitter. Unequal transmissions balance retained coherence against information in failed collection events. A reference--radiation correlation test distinguishes this gain even when the mean collected energy is unchanged. Identical transmission channels instead give the same recovery at matched collection, regardless of their emission timescales. For a broader class of interacting sources, an ordering of collected and inaccessible coupling spectra forbids unassisted quantum transmission irrespective of internal memory. A separate coding example supports a positive rate where uniform transmission at matched actual yield has zero capacity. Together, these results identify what openness must change to assist recovery: the accessible transmission structure. They supply a physically constrained comparison of evaporation mechanisms, separating information delivered to radiation from coherence retained elsewhere or an increase in the collected output.

TianQin, a space-borne gravitational wave observatory, is designed to detect gravitational-wave signals via laser interferometry. As the inhomogeneous space plasma surrounds the inter-satellite laser links, the propagation of the laser in space plasma can introduce pointing error noise. In this work, we quantitatively assess the effect of plasma on the pointing accuracy of TQ's inter-satellite laser links. We employ a magnetohydrodynamic model to simulate the plasma distribution around the TQ orbit for different orbital orientations. Using the fourth-order Runge-Kutta method to conduct the ray-tracing calculations over a TQ orbital cycle under a solar wind dynamic pressure close to the 23-year average, we find that the plasma-induced laser deflection angle and pointing eccentricity are on the order of $10^{-20} \ \mathrm{rad}$ and picometers, respectively. These values are significantly lower than TQ's requirements. Our results demonstrate that the impact of space plasma on TQ's pointing error is negligible in moderate space weather conditions, indicating a positive outlook for the TQ mission.

We use the core-radius, $r_γ,$ of a galaxy's central core along with a volume-limited sample of brightest cluster galaxies (BCG) to determine the $z=0$ mass distribution function of the most massive super-massive black holes (SMBH). We recover a $M_{BH}-r_γ$ relationship between the SMBH mass, $M_{BH},$ and $r_γ$ of form $M_{BH} \approx1.3\times10^{10}~(r_γ/{\mathrm{kpc}})~M_\odot,$ with $σ= 0.25\pm0.03$ dex scatter in $M_{BH}.$ We derive a core distribution function (CDF), based on HST and DLS images of the BCGs. The CDF falls steeply as $N(r_γ)\sim r_γ^{-3}$ for $r_γ>0.50$ kpc. Only $\sim5\%$ of BCGs have $r_γ>1$ kpc. Combining the CDF and $M_{BH} - r_γ$ relationship yields the BH mass distribution function (MDF). For $M_{BH} \geq 7\times10^{9}~\mathrm{M_\odot}$ (the mean $M_{BH}$ at $r_γ=0.50$ kpc) we derive: $\log\left(\frac{dN(M_{BH})}{d{M_{BH}/10^{10} M_\odot}}~[\rm Mpc^{-3}]\right)=(-3.05\pm0.42)~\log\left(\frac{M_{BH}}{10^{10}M_\odot}\right)~-~5.11\ (+0.41, -0.36)$. Cores with $r_γ\geq 0.50$ kpc are found almost exclusively in BCGs, implying that SMBHs with $M_{BH} \geq7\times10^{9}~\mathrm{M_\odot}$ may also be exclusive to BCGs. Core-radius correlates with $α,$ a parameter that characterizes the growth of BCG envelopes by mergers, suggesting that the most massive SMBHs grew in tandem with their BCG hosts. The BH MDF embodies a significant population of SMBH with $M_{BH} >7\times10^{9} M_\odot.$ When combined with the Liepold & Ma (2024) BH MDF to account for SMBH with $M_{BH} < 7\times10^{9} M_\odot,$ it can comfortably explain the nHz gravitational wave background.

Magnetic null points define the magnetic field topology in stellar coronae, planetary magnetospheres, and astrospheres, and are commonly associated with magnetic reconnection. We investigate the local structure of stationary three-dimensional (3D) resistive, incompressible magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) flows in the vicinity of an isolated magnetic null and an isolated velocity stagnation point, assuming that the two nulls are colocated. The governing MHD equations are solved self-consistently using a first-order local expansion of the magnetic and velocity fields about the null. For any given magnetic-null configuration, the equations admit a two-parameter family of stationary solutions, comprising multiple solution branches with distinct velocity topologies. Along these families, the velocity-null polarity can change at bifurcation points where the flow becomes effectively 2.5D. A single magnetic null can therefore support positive-radial, negative-radial, and spiral stagnation flows, with qualitatively different inflow, outflow, and rotational properties. The associated pressure and resistivity distributions are likewise determined self-consistently and vary across the solution family, demonstrating that they are coupled to the flow topology rather than prescribed independently. The generic solutions do not require the velocity and magnetic separatrices to coincide, allowing plasma to cross the magnetic fan separatrix and providing a local reconnective flow. These results demonstrate that, in the presence of nonuniform resistivity, the magnetic topology alone does not uniquely determine the associated stationary flow, pressure, or resistivity structure around a 3D magnetic null, revealing the nonlinear feedback between all macroscopic variables and resistivity. The constant-resistivity field-aligned and spiral field-crossing solutions identified previously are recovered as special cases.

Binary millisecond pulsars (MSPs) serve as exceptional astrophysical laboratories for a wide range of astrophysical studies, all of which rely on precise property measurements allowed by pulsar timing. In this paper, we present high-precision timing solutions for seven binary MSPs monitored by the European Pulsar Timing Array. By combining data collected from the five largest radio telescopes in Europe, as well as their coherently combined form, the Large European Array for Pulsars, we achieve phase-coherent timing solutions for all seven pulsars. We conduct a Bayesian timing and noise analysis on the combined data, yielding measurements of astrometric and orbital parameters with broadly improved precision, some reported for the first time. For PSRs J0125-2327, J1741+1351 and J2043+1711, we determine their distances and transverse velocities from astrometric measurements, and infer their masses via Shapiro delay measurements. Additionally, we examine the various contributions to the secular orbital-period variations for PSRs J0125-2327, J1125+7819 and J1741+1351, and estimate their kinematic distances. For PSRs J0125-2327 and J1741+1351, we detect the signature of annual-orbital parallax in the timing data. This allows us to derive a strongly favored, unique solution for the orbital ascending node and inclination angle for PSR J0125-2327, and a tightly constrained ascending node for PSR J1741+1351. Moreover, we use the orbital periods and their derivatives for several binary MSPs to place upper limits on gravitational-wave strain in both the sub-nanohertz and microhertz frequency regimes. Our results show that the orbits of binary MSPs could provide stringent constraints in microhertz range, thereby possibly complementing standard pulsar-timing-array analyses in the future.

Post-common-envelope evolution (CEE) circumbinary disks (CBDs) form when part of the ejected envelope remains bound and circularizes around the stripped core and its companion. Leveraging results of scale-free hydrodynamical simulations, we develop a framework to model the effect of such disks on subsequent binary evolution, and implement it in the population synthesis code COMPAS. Applying our framework to double neutron star (DNS) merger progenitors, we find that CBDs generally induce substantial orbital shrinkage, changing the merging DNS population and lowering the overall merger rate. We explore an extensive grid of binary-evolution and disk models, and find that CBD interactions reduce the DNS merger rate by up to $28\%$, primarily through 'premature' mergers between the neutron star (NS) and the giant's core. These mergers may power luminous fast blue optical transients (LFBOTs), occurring at a fiducial rate of $\simeq11 \:\rm Gpc^{-3} \rm yr^{-1}$. Our results indicate that natal kicks contribute little to LFBOT offsets, and that they should be associated with young stellar populations, though not necessarily active star-forming regions. Since LFBOT progenitors would otherwise produce short-delay DNS mergers, CBDs further challenge DNS mergers as the source of early r-process enrichment. For surviving DNS mergers, accretion from the disk leads to higher chirp masses, potentially explaining the puzzling mass of GW190425. Our framework provides a general tool for studying CBD-binary interactions in post-CEE systems.

We investigate the cosmological implications of supermassive primordial black holes (PBHs) as a component of dark matter (DM), focusing on their seed effect on large-scale structure formation. In contrast to the Poisson effect associated with the discrete distribution of PBHs, the seed effect arises because PBHs act as early gravitational seeds, accreting surrounding DM and developing dense DM spikes to form dressed PBHs. These structures enhance the matter power spectrum, particularly on small scales. We model their contribution within the halo-model framework under two prescriptions for DM accretion: an optimistic scenario (Case 1) and a conservative scenario (Case 2), which predict different modifications to the 1-halo term. We constrain the PBH abundance using weak-lensing measurements from the Dark Energy Survey Year 3 (DES Y3), performing a Markov Chain Monte Carlo analysis to derive upper limits on the PBH fraction $f_{\rm PBH}$, as a function of the PBH mass $M_{\rm PBH}$. We find that weak lensing places competitive constraints on supermassive PBHs, with the upper limits on $f_{\rm PBH}$ becoming progressively stronger toward higher PBH masses. Case 1, which predicts more efficient DM accretion and hence a larger enhancement of the matter power spectrum, yields stronger constraints than Case 2; in particular, it excludes $f_{\rm PBH}\gtrsim10^{-4}$ for $M_{\rm PBH}\gtrsim10^{10} M_\odot$. We further forecast the sensitivity of the Vera C. Rubin Observatory's Legacy Survey of Space and Time (LSST) using mock weak-lensing data and find that the constraints could improve by approximately two orders of magnitude. Our results establish weak gravitational lensing as a sensitive probe of the seed effect of supermassive PBHs and demonstrate its potential to complement other cosmological and astrophysical searches.

We study global atmosphere models of AF supergiants. In agreement with previous studies, we found super-Eddington layers in the photospheres of these stars, which lead to density inversions. Our tests showed that the super-Eddington layers are caused by hydrogen ionization. The analysis of radial perturbations has not revealed any instability of such layers, but we show that super-Eddington layers in an optically thick environment are prone to instabilities due to nonradial perturbations. We predict the wind parameters of AF supergiants from our global wind code with a comoving-frame radiative force. Despite the existence of density inversions, the predicted mass-loss rates are significantly lower than those estimated from observations. Theoretical values approach the observational estimates only for the most luminous supergiants, which are close to the Eddington limit due to light scattering on free electrons. Part of the discrepancy between the mass-loss rates could be attributed to the influence of the clumping on the observational properties. Low mass-loss rates imply that the winds of AF supergiants may be strongly influenced by the magnetic fields, suggesting an explanation for the observed spectral variability of these stars.

Blazars are active galactic nuclei with relativistic jets closely aligned with our line of sight, producing Doppler-boosted emission from radio to gamma rays. Millimeter emission near the synchrotron self-absorption turnover probes compact regions at the jet base, yet large statistical studies in this regime remain limited. We construct the FALCON (FERMI--ALMA Calibrator ObservatioN) catalogue, a millimeter-selected blazar sample by cross-matching the ALMA Calibrator Source Catalogue with the Third Data Release of the Fourth LAT AGN Catalog (4LAC-DR3). After removing duplicates, we obtain 949 unique gamma-ray-associated sources, corresponding to approximately 25 % of the 4LAC-DR3 blazar population. For the subset with available redshift measurements, we compute millimeter and gamma-ray luminosities and find a strong, statistically significant correlation that remains robust after accounting for redshift effects. Fermi-detected blazars exhibit systematically flatter millimeter spectral indices than gamma-ray non-detected ALMA calibrators, while the redshift-controlled luminosity correlation becomes progressively weaker from high-synchrotron-peaked BL Lacs toward flat-spectrum radio quasars. These results indicate an intrinsic connection between millimeter and gamma-ray emission, consistent with a common origin in relativistic jets governed by Doppler boosting and time-averaged jet power.
