A recently constructed superstring field theory has two decoupled sectors, the physical superstring and a shadow sector with a second copy of the superstring. These are both expanded around the same background conformal field theory and the theory is independent of marginal deformations of this common background. Test-time compute mechanisms, such as retries, confidence-based escalation and non-local routing, are conventionally deployed as black-box additions to trained models. They typically add forward passes or halting heads, but give no structural account of what the extra compute buys or what it costs against the model's stability guarantees. We therefore propose a theoretical and empirical framework for test-time compute on a Latent Dynamics Model used as the memory. When an encoder's latent state $(q,p)$ is advanced by a damped symplectic (velocity-Verlet) step of a learned Hamiltonian, every mechanism that expands access has an exact phase-volume Jacobian, a bounded energy change and an exact latch-transport law. We split reachability failures into escape failures and reach failures. A Lorentz squeeze resolves escape failures by injecting bounded, re-absorbable energy. A gated canonical translation (a one-step channel across $C_T$) resolves reach failures at $\detJ=1$ exactly. We verify this certificate stack on a designed analytic testbed. Reaching just beyond $C_T$ with the squeeze requires energy that grows quadratically in the excess distance. The gated channel's energy cost equals the potential difference between its endpoints, which is zero on the matched channel measured here, and so does not grow with distance in this testbed. An untrained state-replacing map annihilates phase volume and erases stored charge, while the gated channel transports it. On a learned physics-structured memory, we show calibrated compute rationing and evaluate the scaling limits of non-local routing against a physics-free baseline. The main contribution is the formalization and verification of the certificate stack, not benchmark performance.

We formulate the static, spherically symmetric vacuum equations of power-law metric $f(R)$ gravity as a covariant autonomous system using the $(1+1+2)$ formalism. Newton's law of universal gravitation describes the attraction between two masses through an inverse-distance potential. In Einstein's theory gravity gravitates: each mass reshapes the spacetime geometry through which the other acts. More than a century later, extracting the two-body interaction from this coupled geometry remains a fundamental analytical challenge. Even without motion or radiation, the static sector of the interaction harbours an infinite hierarchy of field couplings: the purest laboratory for gravitational non-linearity. Here we determine the static interaction from ninth to twelfth order in Newton's constant for arbitrary mass ratios, and derive a closed expression for the first backreaction beyond the Schwarzschild test-body limit to all orders in the gravitational coupling. We achieve both advances by formulating the problem around the stationary interaction energy. An exact one-body background resums one source's self-interaction, stationarity reduces the fields required from the other, and integrated response identities reveal cancellations across the coupled modes. Complementary expansions then resolve the complete mass dependence at finite order. Together, these results bridge the gap between an infinitesimal probe and comparable masses, exposing hidden analytical simplicity within Einstein's theory and providing essential building blocks for modelling orbital dynamics and gravitational-wave generation.

Biadjoint scalar theory partial amplitudes, $m_n(α,β)$, define an $(n-1)!/2\times (n-1)!/2$ matrix, $M_n$, with rows and columns labeled by orderings. The CHY construction of $M_n$ implies that its rank is $(n-3)!$. Invertible submatrices of maximal rank enter in the KLT construction relating graviton amplitudes to gluon amplitudes. Motivated by finding the KLT formula with the smallest number of terms, one of the authors posed the question of whether there is a critical $n_c$ such that for all $n>n_c$ there exists a diagonal submatrix of $M_n$ of rank $(n-3)!$. In this work we obtain bounds on the size of the largest non-singular diagonal submatrix of $M_n$, which we call its diagonal degree $Δ_n$, and we answer the question in the negative. We prove that the diagonal degree is exponentially suppressed relative to the CHY rank: $Δ_n/(n-3)! = \exp(-Θ(n))$ as $n\to \infty$. More explicitly, we establish the bounds $\exp(-1.5n)\leqΔ_n/(n-3)!\leq \exp \left[ -0.01n+O(1)\right]$.

TianQin, a space-borne gravitational wave observatory, is designed to detect gravitational-wave signals via laser interferometry. As the inhomogeneous space plasma surrounds the inter-satellite laser links, the propagation of the laser in space plasma can introduce pointing error noise. In this work, we quantitatively assess the effect of plasma on the pointing accuracy of TQ's inter-satellite laser links. We employ a magnetohydrodynamic model to simulate the plasma distribution around the TQ orbit for different orbital orientations. Using the fourth-order Runge-Kutta method to conduct the ray-tracing calculations over a TQ orbital cycle under a solar wind dynamic pressure close to the 23-year average, we find that the plasma-induced laser deflection angle and pointing eccentricity are on the order of $10^{-20} \ \mathrm{rad}$ and picometers, respectively. These values are significantly lower than TQ's requirements. Our results demonstrate that the impact of space plasma on TQ's pointing error is negligible in moderate space weather conditions, indicating a positive outlook for the TQ mission.

We use the core-radius, $r_γ,$ of a galaxy's central core along with a volume-limited sample of brightest cluster galaxies (BCG) to determine the $z=0$ mass distribution function of the most massive super-massive black holes (SMBH). We recover a $M_{BH}-r_γ$ relationship between the SMBH mass, $M_{BH},$ and $r_γ$ of form $M_{BH} \approx1.3\times10^{10}~(r_γ/{\mathrm{kpc}})~M_\odot,$ with $σ= 0.25\pm0.03$ dex scatter in $M_{BH}.$ We derive a core distribution function (CDF), based on HST and DLS images of the BCGs. The CDF falls steeply as $N(r_γ)\sim r_γ^{-3}$ for $r_γ>0.50$ kpc. Only $\sim5\%$ of BCGs have $r_γ>1$ kpc. Combining the CDF and $M_{BH} - r_γ$ relationship yields the BH mass distribution function (MDF). For $M_{BH} \geq 7\times10^{9}~\mathrm{M_\odot}$ (the mean $M_{BH}$ at $r_γ=0.50$ kpc) we derive: $\log\left(\frac{dN(M_{BH})}{d{M_{BH}/10^{10} M_\odot}}~[\rm Mpc^{-3}]\right)=(-3.05\pm0.42)~\log\left(\frac{M_{BH}}{10^{10}M_\odot}\right)~-~5.11\ (+0.41, -0.36)$. Magnetic null points define the magnetic field topology in stellar coronae, planetary magnetospheres, and astrospheres, and are commonly associated with magnetic reconnection. We investigate the local structure of stationary three-dimensional (3D) resistive, incompressible magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) flows in the vicinity of an isolated magnetic null and an isolated velocity stagnation point, assuming that the two nulls are colocated. The governing MHD equations are solved self-consistently using a first-order local expansion of the magnetic and velocity fields about the null. For any given magnetic-null configuration, the equations admit a two-parameter family of stationary solutions, comprising multiple solution branches with distinct velocity topologies. Along these families, the velocity-null polarity can change at bifurcation points where the flow becomes effectively 2.5D. A single magnetic null can therefore support positive-radial, negative-radial, and spiral stagnation flows, with qualitatively different inflow, outflow, and rotational properties. The associated pressure and resistivity distributions are likewise determined self-consistently and vary across the solution family, demonstrating that they are coupled to the flow topology rather than prescribed independently.
