We study the linear energetic stability of non-Abelian vortices in an $SO(3)$ gauge-Higgs theory with residual gauge group $A_4$. In our previous work, we identified two families of such vortices associated with order-two and order-three elements of $A_4$, and found Bogomol'nyi--Prasad--Sommerfield-like behavior at special ratios of the Higgs and gauge-boson mass scales. Here, we investigate their stability against $SO(3)$ gauge and Higgs perturbations by analyzing the second order variation of the vortex tension using analytical and numerical methods. In particular, when all relevant mass scales are equal, we prove linear stability for all vortices in the order-three family. By contrast, at the same mass point, we prove that a vortex in the order-two family is unstable. Thus, although both vortex families exhibit Bogomol'nyi--Prasad--Sommerfield-like behavior in their reduced equations at this mass point, their stability properties in the full $SO(3)$ theory are qualitatively different.

We study the relation between the exact-WKB Delabaere--Dillinger--Pham Stokes jumps and the multi-instanton expansion of a one-dimensional potential with $N$ equal-depth minima. For a single barrier this relation is the Bogomolny--Zinn-Justin cancellation between the instanton--anti-instanton ambiguity and the Borel ambiguity of perturbation theory, and we extend it to every consecutive barrier interval and to the integration contours themselves. In the dilute large-$T$ regime at fixed nonperturbative grade, we derive the quasi-zero mode integral over the instanton separations from the Euclidean path integral and reduce it to Gamma and Hankel periods. With the local Weber level function of each well and one normalization per well fixed by the local Weber connection matrix, these periods reproduce the two lateral connected exact-WKB coefficients. The two lateral contours of each instanton--anti-instanton separation differ by exactly one Hankel cycle, and this linear Picard--Lefschetz relation reproduces the Stokes jump of the connected coefficient. A numerical solution of the Schrödinger equation for a symmetric triple well confirms the resulting two-barrier level splitting. At higher fixed grade, the map from ordered event sequences to normalized scalar coefficient products is not one-to-one. At leading dilute order the scalar weight of a closed event sequence depends only on the numbers of barrier crossings, $\mathcal I\bar{\mathcal I}$ pairs, and $\mathcal I\mathcal I$/$\bar{\mathcal I}\bar{\mathcal I}$ pairs. We construct two inequivalent grade-six triple-well event sequences with the same product of local Weber amplitudes, which embed in every potential with $N\ge3$ wells. Our results hold within the regular dilute, one-loop, local Weber approximation.

Kemp and Ramgoolam proposed a basis of monomials for a commutative semisimple algebra with generators \(C_1,\ldots, C_L\), constructed from a degeneracy graph which records how the eigenspaces of \( C_1\) are successively split by \( C_2,\ldots, C_L\). They showed that the basis property is equivalent to the non-vanishing of the determinant of the matrix of values of these monomials on the projector basis, and conjectured a factorised formula for this determinant, as a product of powers of eigenvalue differences. We prove this formula for any number of generators. The proof uses graph analogues of Newton polynomials. Their evaluation matrix is not triangular in general, but expanding the rows of the children of each node of the penultimate layer in a Newton basis of their eigenvalues makes it block triangular, with diagonal blocks given by smaller degeneracy graphs in which the final layer is removed. The formula then follows by induction on the number of layers. We show that the exponents in the determinant are overlap volumes of higher-dimensional Young diagrams attached to the nodes of the graph: intervals, ordinary Young diagrams, plane partitions and their higher analogues. Finally, the block triangular structure gives an explicit recursive inversion of the evaluation matrix, expressing the projectors in the monomial basis.

A Lagrangian submanifold in an almost Calabi-Yau manifold is called positive if the real part of the holomorphic volume form restricted to it is positive. It is called $ε$-positive if the ratio of the restrictions of the imaginary and real parts of the holomorphic volume form is bounded by $\frac{1}ε.$ An exact isotopy class of positive Lagrangian submanifolds admits a natural Riemannian metric. We prove that the Riemannian metric restricted to the subset of $ε$-positive Lagrangians induces a path metric when the relative Kähler class of the ambient Calabi-Yau manifold lies on a line of rational slope. The proof relies on the construction of a sequence of quantization maps from a circle bundle over the space of positive Lagrangians to the dual of the space of holomorphic sections of powers of a prequantum line bundle. The rationality condition for the relative Kähler class allows us to reduce to the case of Bohr-Sommerfeld Lagrangians. Building on ideas of Tian and Nirenberg-Wells, Hörmander estimates are used to show that functions on a Bohr-Sommerfeld Lagrangian can be approximated by holomorphic sections of a sufficiently high power of the prequantum line bundle. Consequently, the quantization maps eventually separate fibers of the circle bundle.

Magnetic null points define the magnetic field topology in stellar coronae, planetary magnetospheres, and astrospheres, and are commonly associated with magnetic reconnection. We investigate the local structure of stationary three-dimensional (3D) resistive, incompressible magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) flows in the vicinity of an isolated magnetic null and an isolated velocity stagnation point, assuming that the two nulls are colocated. The governing MHD equations are solved self-consistently using a first-order local expansion of the magnetic and velocity fields about the null. For any given magnetic-null configuration, the equations admit a two-parameter family of stationary solutions, comprising multiple solution branches with distinct velocity topologies. Along these families, the velocity-null polarity can change at bifurcation points where the flow becomes effectively 2.5D. A single magnetic null can therefore support positive-radial, negative-radial, and spiral stagnation flows, with qualitatively different inflow, outflow, and rotational properties. The associated pressure and resistivity distributions are likewise determined self-consistently and vary across the solution family, demonstrating that they are coupled to the flow topology rather than prescribed independently. The generic solutions do not require the velocity and magnetic separatrices to coincide, allowing plasma to cross the magnetic fan separatrix and providing a local reconnective flow. These results demonstrate that, in the presence of nonuniform resistivity, the magnetic topology alone does not uniquely determine the associated stationary flow, pressure, or resistivity structure around a 3D magnetic null, revealing the nonlinear feedback between all macroscopic variables and resistivity. The constant-resistivity field-aligned and spiral field-crossing solutions identified previously are recovered as special cases.

We present the design methodology, implementation, and data products of the Energetic Particle Detector (EPD) instrument on-board the Electron Losses and Fields Investigation (ELFIN) dual SmallSat mission. ELFIN was designed to study the time-evolution of energetic particle distributions in Earth's magnetosphere, having operated in polar Low Earth Orbit (LEO) from 2018 to 2022. Each satellite's instrument payload contained a pair of detector heads to sense differential-directional fluxes of 50 keV to $\sim$5 MeV electrons (EPDE), and 50 keV to $\sim$2 MeV atomic ions (EPDI), with an energy resolution better than 50% ($ΔE/E<0.5$). The satellites were spinning with a nominal period of 2.8 s in a plane containing Earth's background magnetic field, with particle counting intervals subdivided into 16 consecutive spin sectors. Each EPD was paired with a three-axis Fluxgate Magnetometer (FGM) sampled at 80 Hz for local pitch-angle determination. Each detector head operated on the principle of pulse-height analysis (PHA) in solid state media, collimated by a rectangular 22.5$^\circ$ field of view and entrance aperture. The EPDE contained a stack of 6 silicon detectors (0.7 cm total thickness) with 0.15 cm$^2$ str geometric factor, with ion contamination mitigated by an aperture foil and digital coincidence logic. The EPDI contained a stack of 2 silicon detectors (0.1 cm total thickness) with 0.03 cm$^2$ str geometric factor, with electron contamination mitigated by a magnetic broom stage and digital anti-coincidence logic. The total assembled mass, volume, and power required for the EPD detector heads and circuit boards were 939 g, 10$\times$9.8$\times$8.4 cm$^3$, and 2.3 W, respectively. We describe each EPD design element alongside instrument data products and processing.

Collisionless shocks confine the particles they accelerate with the upstream wave field they themselves drive. Streaming particles resonantly amplify Alfvén waves, and the waves then confine the same particles by pitch-angle scattering. The rigidity a shock precursor can hold is therefore capped by how fast those waves grow, and that coupling has not previously been constrained in-situ at any interplanetary shock. Here we report Parker Solar Probe measurements of a fast, near-parallel coronal mass ejection-driven shock at 0.24~AU on 2023 March 13 that constrain this wave-particle coupling through the energetic-particle population it accelerates. We measure the spectral folding energy, the boundary between the confined and the upstream-escaping protons, and find that it falls from $\sim$6 to $\sim$1.5~MeV as $E_\mathrm{fold}\proptoΔt^{\,α}$ with $α\simeq0.34$, where $Δt$ is the time remaining before shock arrival. One precursor convective time before arrival this boundary is at $E_\mathrm{c}\simeq 2.46$~MeV, a characteristic confinement energy of a precursor of scale $L_\mathrm{EP}$. Read through the quasi-linear marginal-stability condition, the same evolution implies a resonant wave-growth timescale $τ_\mathrm{growth}(E)\propto E^{1/α}\simeq E^{2.92}$ that exceeds the precursor convective time above $E_\mathrm{c}$. The post-shock pitch-angle anisotropy reverses sign across the same confinement--escape transition at 2--5~MeV. The rigidity to which a shock precursor can confine particles is therefore set, in-situ, by how quickly the streaming population builds its own confining waves.

Black holes are usually described either as classical geometries on which quantum fields propagate or as objects whose complete quantum description lies beyond semiclassical gravity. Hawking radiation already shows, however, that a black hole is not an exactly isolated system: the horizon carries a thermal scale and, after coarse graining, may be associated with fluctuations, dissipation and environmental correlations. Whether this horizon-scale openness can be encoded directly in the gravitational background, before any stochastic treatment of the metric, remains an open question. Here we answer it by introducing, for the first time, a conserved Hawking-scale anisotropic source representing the coarse-grained near-horizon atmosphere and solving the Einstein equations for a static black hole at fixed ADM mass. The dressed geometry has a renormalized mass function, a self-consistently shifted horizon and a modified surface gravity. Because the same lapse governs the horizon and timelike geodesics, the thermal response is accompanied by a correlated orbital restructuring: the effective potential is renormalized, the ISCO moves inward and the near-horizon residence time increases. The construction adds no Langevin force to a fixed Schwarzschild background; rather, it defines a deterministic dressed mean geometry, providing a controlled background on which stochastic metric fluctuations may subsequently be treated in the Einstein-Langevin framework. This establishes the deterministic mean-field sector as the necessary first step for future stochastic gravity programs addressing horizon-scale openness.

Collisionless shocks accelerate particles at a rate set by the diffusion coefficient of those particles in waves they themselves generate. Theory fixes that coefficient only in steady state, and no measurement has tested whether an evolving shock ever reaches that state. We report Parker Solar Probe observations of a fast, near-parallel interplanetary shock at 0.24 au, encountered four hours after eruption, with the upstream waves and the protons they scatter recorded on the same magnetic field line. From the wave spectra alone we derive the parallel mean free path of 0.05-30 MeV protons, resolved in energy and in distance from the shock. The mirror force of the measured compressive fluctuations supplies the scattering through the pitch angles that gyroresonance cannot reach. The mean free path grows with distance nearly as the steady self-generated solution requires, yet the steady balance holds at one energy only, near 1.3 MeV. The intensity profiles are a factor of two shallower than the measured coefficient predicts, because the waves are still growing. Confinement fails between 2.3 and 6 MeV, beginning a factor of two below what the age of the shock allows. The growth of the waves its own particles drive, rather than its age or its size, limits the energy an evolving shock confines.

The observation of sub-solar mass neutron stars and black holes would signify a new class of astrophysical objects since these systems are not expected to form via standard stellar evolution. We present the first sub-solar mass search for binaries consisting of neutron star or black holes that uses the data from both the second part (O4b) and first part (O4a) of the fourth observing run of the Advanced LIGO detectors. The search covers primary masses 0.1 to 2 $M_\odot$ and secondary masses 0.1 to 1 $M_\odot$. Tidal effects up to $7\times10^5$ are accounted for in extremely low-mass neutron stars. No statistically significant candidates are identified. We place a $90\%$ confidence upper limit on the merger rate $\mathcal{R}_{90}$ for sub-solar mass black holes and neutron stars to be $< 5.2\times10^{2}\,\textrm{Gpc}^{-3}\,\textrm{yr}^{-1}$ for a chirp mass of 0.5 $M_\odot$, a factor $2.2 \times$ lower compared to previous O1-O4a searches. We constrain the effective local dark matter fraction to be $\tilde{f}_\textrm{PBH}<0.6\%$ for $M_{\textrm{PBH}}=0.8 \,M_\odot$, approximately $1.5$ factor improvement to previous O1-O4a constraints. Given our model assumptions, our local dark matter fraction constraints are $2-7$ times lower than the OGLE microlensing survey for $M_{\textrm{PBH}}\ge{0.6}~M_\odot$.

We investigate the long-term spin evolution of high-mass X-ray binary (HMXB) pulsars in the Small Magellanic Cloud (SMC) using archival measurements of spin periods and period derivatives from XMM-Newton, Chandra, and RXTE. In the P-|dP/dt| plane, the HMXB pulsars follow an approximately linear relation in log-log space with a slope of 1.02 +/- 0.11, similar to the trend shown by the youngest isolated rotation-powered pulsars (RPPs; e.g., Manchester et al., 2005; Lorimer and Kramer, 2005). For comparison, young RPPs from the ATNF Pulsar Catalogue give slopes that depend strongly on the characteristic-age cut: 1.10 +/- 0.16 for characteristic ages below 10,000 years and 0.72 +/- 0.08 for characteristic ages below 100,000 years. The full non-recycled population shows no significant correlation. The HMXB pulsars lie in the same characteristic-age range (approximately 1,000-10,000 years) as the youngest RPPs, but at spin periods two to three orders of magnitude longer. Reference characteristic-age contours in the HMXB P-|dP/dt| plane are roughly parallel to this relation, consistent with a continuous evolutionary sequence.

We conducted an investigation into the possible correlation between very-high-energy (VHE, $E>$ 0.1 TeV) gamma-ray sources, as identified by the Large High Altitude Air Shower Observatory (LHAASO), and high-energy neutrino events captured by the IceCube Neutrino Observatory. Through Monte Carlo simulations, we assessed the correlations between the neutrino events and LHAASO sources, achieving the best p-values of 0.237, 0.312, and 0.115, respectively, for all LHAASO sources, KM2A sources, and WCDA sources. These findings imply that the spatial coincidence between LHAASO sources and neutrino alerts is relatively weak. Furthermore, under the assumption that gamma-rays are exclusively generated through hadronic interactions, we estimated the most optimistic theoretical neutrino fluxes from LHAASO sources and compared them with the IceCube observed fluxes. The results indicate that the majority of these sources are improbable counterparts to the detected neutrinos. In conclusion, LHAASO sources could be potential galactic neutrino emitters but currently do not exhibit compelling evidence, thereby underscoring the necessity for elevated sensitivity levels in forthcoming detection instruments like IceCube-Gen2.

Linear Polarization of Synchrotron Radiation from Very-small-pitch-angle Electrons

For synchrotron radiation with pitch angle $α\ll 1/γ$ where $γ$ is the electron Lorentz factor, referred to as very-small-pitch-angle synchrotron radiation, the polarization properties differ significantly from those of conventional synchrotron radiation ($α\gg 1/γ$). In this work, we investigate the linear polarization characteristics of very-small-pitch-angle synchrotron radiation from relativistic thermal and nonthermal electrons. We calculate the linear polarization by applying power-law and Maxwellian electron energy distributions. Moreover, some synchrotron sources exhibit a hybrid distribution of nonthermal and thermal electrons, and we also calculate the polarization degree for a hybrid electron energy distribution. The calculations are performed in the radio, optical, X-ray, and $γ$-ray bands. Our results show that the linear polarization degree of very-small-pitch-angle synchrotron radiation is generally lower than that of conventional synchrotron radiation. Very-small-pitch-angle synchrotron radiation from both nonthermal and thermal electrons can produce significant linear polarization. However, the polarization exhibits diverse behaviors across different physical parameters. The general scenario provided in this work can be considered for the application to all kinds of synchrotron sources, as we suggest that the very-small-pitch-angle case is crucial for the study of synchrotron polarization.
