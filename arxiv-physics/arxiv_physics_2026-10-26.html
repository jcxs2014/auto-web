Can classically branched dynamics associated with spontaneous time-translation-symmetry breaking be quantized without explicitly retaining the branches of the Hamiltonian? We investigate this question using a reduced phase space. formulation in which the Hamiltonian is single-valued, while the critical structure of the classical Legendre map is encoded in a singular, momentum-dependent symplectic geometry. Quantization of the reduced Poisson algebra yields a deformed position-momentum commutator whose singularities determine distinct regular momentum sectors. Using the positive measure induced by the reduced symplectic structure, we identify a bounded central sector and two unbounded outer sectors. In the central sector, states with vanishing mean momentum obey a nonzero lower bound on position uncertainty, subject to the operator-domain assumptions of the representation. We further identify a possible field-theoretic connection with k-essence, where nonlinear scalar-field kinetics can produce finite-velocity degeneracies of the Legendre map. The relationship between the resulting reduced quantum description and branch-matching quantization remains unresolved and requires a comparison of the corresponding Hilbert spaces and operator domains.

The quantum mechanics of one $N\times N$ Hermitian matrix in the infinite $N$ limit is well known to define a two-dimensional string theory when the quartic coupling constant $g$ is tuned to a special negative value $g_*$. We study the thermodynamics of this Matrix Quantum Mechanics (MQM), treating the SU$(N)$ symmetry as a global symmetry. Crucially, the non-singlets, i.e. states in non-trivial representations of SU$(N)$, implement the effects of vortices on the discretized worldsheets. This random lattice model has the low-temperature $c=1$ and high-temperature $c=0$ phases, with the Berezinskii-Kosterlitz-Thouless (BKT) transition separating them. We map out the phase diagram in the coupling--temperature plane, which is separated by the critical stability curve $T_c(g)$ extending from $g_*< g <0$. We study this curve using the non-perturbative matrix bootstrap and low- and high-temperature perturbation theory, which explain the thermal instability. At low temperatures, thermally excited non-singlets backreact on the Fermi sea of matrix eigenvalues, causing it to spill over. At high temperatures, the theory is described by an effective matrix integral obtained by dimensional reduction on the thermal circle; the eigenvalue distribution of the zero mode becomes unstable at the critical coupling. We conjecture that the BKT transition occurs at a special point on the critical curve and estimate its position. Finally, we switch to the regular square lattice and study the MQM-inspired Villain model, where the link factor is the one-dimensional propagator on a circle. Using Monte Carlo simulations, we map out the BKT transition in this model.

We present refined predictions of the geoneutrino flux for the 500-tonne Jinping Neutrino Experiment (JNE). We construct a local crustal model with a resolution of \(0.2^\circ\times0.2^\circ\) based on the SWChinaCVM-2.0 seismic velocity model and integrate it with CRUST1.0 to establish our reference model. For comparison, we also calculate the geoneutrino flux using ECM1 and CRUST1.0 as standalone global crustal models. Assuming an energy scale of 500~p.e./MeV and an exposure of 500~t$\times$1500~days, we predict the inverse beta decay event spectrum, including both the geoneutrino signal and the reactor antineutrino background. The distinct spectral shapes of the $^{238}$U, $^{232}$Th, and reactor antineutrino components allow their respective contributions to be obtained through spectral fitting. These predictions provide inputs for the JNE data analysis, particularly for extracting the mantle geoneutrino signal and testing competing bulk silicate Earth models.

Recent work has introduced the notion of a time semicrystal, a driven nonequilibrium regime in which subharmonic temporal order survives inside deterministic chaos. Here we revisit this phenomenology from the viewpoint of classical nonlinear dynamics. Banded chaotic attractors consist of a finite number of chaotic sectors cyclically permuted by stroboscopic evolution, allowing discrete subharmonic spectral components to coexist with a broadband chaotic continuum. Exemplified through the driven Duffing oscillator and the logistic map, this structure naturally reproduces the characteristic signatures of time-semicrystalline dynamics. Within this framework, periodic subharmonic motion can enter chaos through the familiar period-doubling cascade while retaining a well-defined temporal skeleton governed by the universal Feigenbaum scaling. Temporal order within chaos can also reorganize or collapse through distinct mechanisms, producing continuous or abrupt transitions that may be intertwined with symmetry changes. These results connect time-semicrystal phenomenology with established structures of dissipative nonlinear dynamics, showing that its essential ingredients already arise in standard nonlinear systems and providing a simple framework for extending and testing the paradigm in more complex, many-body, and experimental settings.

In this paper, we review the impact of a strong magnetic field on the neutral two-flavor color superconducting (2SC) phase of dense quark matter. Using a Nambu-Jona-Lasinio (NJL) model approach in the mean-field approximation, we first solve the gap equations for the diquark condensate $Δ$ and the electric chemical potential $μ_e$ under a uniform external magnetic field, and discuss the coupled evolution of the chiral and diquark order parameters. We then evaluate the field-dependent Meissner masses of the rotationally charged gluons for a wide range of densities and moderate diquark coupling strengths. Combining the "zero-mode problem" mechanism with the neutrality condition, we show that the gluon masses become tachyonic even at moderate magnetic-field values, signaling a chromomagnetic instability in density regions where the system was stable at zero field. This instability can be resolved through the restructuring of the ground state with the formation of a gluon vortex condensate. A remarkable consequence of the vortex configuration is the paramagnetic amplification of the in-medium magnetic field: seed fields of the order of $10^{15}$ G can be enhanced by up to two orders of magnitude, reaching $\sim 10^{17}$ G, precisely the range predicted for magnetar cores. We compare this microscopic mechanism with the conventional dynamo explanation and discuss how it offers a plausible explanation for the extremely high magnetic fields expected to be realized in magnetar interiors.

We present a coherent axion-electrodynamics extension of the OSIRIS particle-in-cell code and use it to identify and benchmark a parametric axion-photon instability supported by two equal counter-propagating electromagnetic pumps. The module evolves a cell-centered axion field together with a separately stored first-order electromagnetic response on the native staggered OSIRIS grid. Re-examining the previously proposed strictly co-linear branch shows that it is removed when the axion-Maxwell equations are reduced consistently. A closely related standing-wave geometry, however, supports a distinct Floquet instability. We derive the coupled Floquet problem for an equal-amplitude counter-propagating pump pair and obtain a reduced analytic growth rate and cutoff for the dominant long-wavelength envelope branch. For an axion mass-to-pump-frequency ratio of 0.06 and a per-beam vector-potential amplitude of 0.3, simulations at coupling strengths of 0.60, 0.80, and 1.00 reproduce both the growth-rate spectrum and the coupling-dependent instability boundary. The reduced theory predicts envelope-mode cutoffs of 8.44, 10.80, and 13.23, while OSIRIS finds exponential growth through modes 8, 10, and 13, respectively, with the next resolved modes stable. The electromagnetic diagnostics additionally resolve exponentially growing sidebands at twice the pump wavenumber, while the zero-wavenumber electric component exhibits no sustained growth, as expected for the unstable Floquet eigenvector. The agreement provides an end-to-end benchmark of the coherent axion-electromagnetic coupling and demonstrates that parametric growth of the axion field is present in a two-beam pump geometry.

Magnetic null points define the magnetic field topology in stellar coronae, planetary magnetospheres, and astrospheres, and are commonly associated with magnetic reconnection. We investigate the local structure of stationary three-dimensional (3D) resistive, incompressible magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) flows in the vicinity of an isolated magnetic null and an isolated velocity stagnation point, assuming that the two nulls are colocated. The governing MHD equations are solved self-consistently using a first-order local expansion of the magnetic and velocity fields about the null. For any given magnetic-null configuration, the equations admit a two-parameter family of stationary solutions, comprising multiple solution branches with distinct velocity topologies. Along these families, the velocity-null polarity can change at bifurcation points where the flow becomes effectively 2.5D. A single magnetic null can therefore support positive-radial, negative-radial, and spiral stagnation flows, with qualitatively different inflow, outflow, and rotational properties. The associated pressure and resistivity distributions are likewise determined self-consistently and vary across the solution family, demonstrating that they are coupled to the flow topology rather than prescribed independently. The generic solutions do not require the velocity and magnetic separatrices to coincide, allowing plasma to cross the magnetic fan separatrix and providing a local reconnective flow. These results demonstrate that, in the presence of nonuniform resistivity, the magnetic topology alone does not uniquely determine the associated stationary flow, pressure, or resistivity structure around a 3D magnetic null, revealing the nonlinear feedback between all macroscopic variables and resistivity. The constant-resistivity field-aligned and spiral field-crossing solutions identified previously are recovered as special cases.

We present the design methodology, implementation, and data products of the Energetic Particle Detector (EPD) instrument on-board the Electron Losses and Fields Investigation (ELFIN) dual SmallSat mission. ELFIN was designed to study the time-evolution of energetic particle distributions in Earth's magnetosphere, having operated in polar Low Earth Orbit (LEO) from 2018 to 2022. Each satellite's instrument payload contained a pair of detector heads to sense differential-directional fluxes of 50 keV to $\sim$5 MeV electrons (EPDE), and 50 keV to $\sim$2 MeV atomic ions (EPDI), with an energy resolution better than 50% ($ΔE/E<0.5$). The satellites were spinning with a nominal period of 2.8 s in a plane containing Earth's background magnetic field, with particle counting intervals subdivided into 16 consecutive spin sectors. Each EPD was paired with a three-axis Fluxgate Magnetometer (FGM) sampled at 80 Hz for local pitch-angle determination. Each detector head operated on the principle of pulse-height analysis (PHA) in solid state media, collimated by a rectangular 22.5$^\circ$ field of view and entrance aperture. The EPDE contained a stack of 6 silicon detectors (0.7 cm total thickness) with 0.15 cm$^2$ str geometric factor, with ion contamination mitigated by an aperture foil and digital coincidence logic. The EPDI contained a stack of 2 silicon detectors (0.1 cm total thickness) with 0.03 cm$^2$ str geometric factor, with electron contamination mitigated by a magnetic broom stage and digital anti-coincidence logic. The total assembled mass, volume, and power required for the EPD detector heads and circuit boards were 939 g, 10$\times$9.8$\times$8.4 cm$^3$, and 2.3 W, respectively. We describe each EPD design element alongside instrument data products and processing.

Collisionless shocks confine the particles they accelerate with the upstream wave field they themselves drive. Streaming particles resonantly amplify Alfvén waves, and the waves then confine the same particles by pitch-angle scattering. The rigidity a shock precursor can hold is therefore capped by how fast those waves grow, and that coupling has not previously been constrained in-situ at any interplanetary shock. Here we report Parker Solar Probe measurements of a fast, near-parallel coronal mass ejection-driven shock at 0.24~AU on 2023 March 13 that constrain this wave-particle coupling through the energetic-particle population it accelerates. We measure the spectral folding energy, the boundary between the confined and the upstream-escaping protons, and find that it falls from $\sim$6 to $\sim$1.5~MeV as $E_\mathrm{fold}\proptoΔt^{\,α}$ with $α\simeq0.34$, where $Δt$ is the time remaining before shock arrival. One precursor convective time before arrival this boundary is at $E_\mathrm{c}\simeq 2.46$~MeV, a characteristic confinement energy of a precursor of scale $L_\mathrm{EP}$. Read through the quasi-linear marginal-stability condition, the same evolution implies a resonant wave-growth timescale $τ_\mathrm{growth}(E)\propto E^{1/α}\simeq E^{2.92}$ that exceeds the precursor convective time above $E_\mathrm{c}$. The post-shock pitch-angle anisotropy reverses sign across the same confinement--escape transition at 2--5~MeV. The rigidity to which a shock precursor can confine particles is therefore set, in-situ, by how quickly the streaming population builds its own confining waves.

Black holes are usually described either as classical geometries on which quantum fields propagate or as objects whose complete quantum description lies beyond semiclassical gravity. Hawking radiation already shows, however, that a black hole is not an exactly isolated system: the horizon carries a thermal scale and, after coarse graining, may be associated with fluctuations, dissipation and environmental correlations. Whether this horizon-scale openness can be encoded directly in the gravitational background, before any stochastic treatment of the metric, remains an open question. Here we answer it by introducing, for the first time, a conserved Hawking-scale anisotropic source representing the coarse-grained near-horizon atmosphere and solving the Einstein equations for a static black hole at fixed ADM mass. The dressed geometry has a renormalized mass function, a self-consistently shifted horizon and a modified surface gravity. Because the same lapse governs the horizon and timelike geodesics, the thermal response is accompanied by a correlated orbital restructuring: the effective potential is renormalized, the ISCO moves inward and the near-horizon residence time increases. The construction adds no Langevin force to a fixed Schwarzschild background; rather, it defines a deterministic dressed mean geometry, providing a controlled background on which stochastic metric fluctuations may subsequently be treated in the Einstein-Langevin framework. This establishes the deterministic mean-field sector as the necessary first step for future stochastic gravity programs addressing horizon-scale openness.

Collisionless shocks accelerate particles at a rate set by the diffusion coefficient of those particles in waves they themselves generate. Theory fixes that coefficient only in steady state, and no measurement has tested whether an evolving shock ever reaches that state. We report Parker Solar Probe observations of a fast, near-parallel interplanetary shock at 0.24 au, encountered four hours after eruption, with the upstream waves and the protons they scatter recorded on the same magnetic field line. From the wave spectra alone we derive the parallel mean free path of 0.05-30 MeV protons, resolved in energy and in distance from the shock. The mirror force of the measured compressive fluctuations supplies the scattering through the pitch angles that gyroresonance cannot reach. We analyse what physical quantities of SN ejecta can be obtained from a single epoch of nebular JWST spectroscopy. We provide an analytical framework, which together with a grid of helium star \texttt{SUMO} spectral synthesis models allows the determination of these quantities for any stripped-envelope SN. Using the framework, we derive critical electron densities for major emission lines, finding values in the range $10^5-10^6$ cm$^{-3}$. This means formation under close to LTE conditions for 200 -- 300 days. We obtain ionisation fractions of Ni and Co, as well as the temperature and filling factor of the Fe/He zone, the $^{56}$Ni and stable nickel masses, the Ni/Fe ratio, and the optical depths $τ_{\mathrm{[Ni\,II]\, 6.636 μm}}$ and $τ_{\mathrm{[Co\,II]\, 10.52 μm}}$. Besides these constraints from iron group elements, one can also obtain the filling factor of the carbon burning (O/Ne/Mg) zone from [Ne II] 12.81 $μ$m, and the masses of Ar II and Cl I. We apply our methodology to a publicly available JWST spectrum of Type Ic SN 2024aecx. We find that this SN produced a chlorine mass of $M_{\mathrm{Cl\, I}} \simeq M_{\mathrm{Cl}} = 5.1^{+2.0}_{-1.6}\times 10^{-4}$$M_{\odot}$, a factor a few larger than expected from stellar evolution modeling. Furthermore, we find that the SN experienced an unexpectedly small 'Ni-bubble' effect, with $ρ_{\mathrm{FeHe}} = 2.5^{+0.7}_{-0.4} \times 10^{-15}$ g cm$^{-3}$ (or an expansion factor of 1 -- 3 for the Ni-bubble, depending on the adopted model) at 167d for the silicon burning material. We also determine $M_{\mathrm{Ar\, II}} = 4.9^{+2.2}_{-1.5} \times 10^{-4}$$M_{\odot}$, which from ionisation fractions in our NLTE models yields a best estimate of $M_{\mathrm{Ar}} = 1.1 \pm 0.4 \times 10^{-3}$$M_{\odot}$.

Pulsar timing arrays will soon be able to resolve the loudest supermassive black hole binaries contributing to the stochastic gravitational wave background at nanohertz frequencies. While the few decades of observations are typically not enough to see these binaries evolve in frequency, the so-called pulsar terms offer a glimpse into the past and can help us map out the frequency evolution of these binaries over tens of thousands of years. Measuring this long-term frequency evolution can allow us to look for deviations from a vacuum binary evolution due to various environmental effects. We develop a Fisher-matrix approach to calculate how precisely the frequency can be tracked over time in this manner, and demonstrate its use on a realistic near-future pulsar timing array with a 30-year baseline. We find that the environmental effects predicted by 2-dimensional hydrodynamic simulations of circumbinary disks are unlikely to be detectable in the circular binaries we considered, however, in some cases they might result in biased chirp mass recovery if ignored. We also demonstrate how this approach allows one to test the orbital evolution predicted by general relativity in a purely gravitational-wave driven inspiral, and to put upper limits on additional generic environmental effects described by a power-law hardening model.

脉冲星计时阵列很快就能解析出最大声的超大质量黑洞双星，这些双星在纳赫频率下产生随机引力波背景。虽然几十年的观测通常不足以看到这些双星的频率演化，但所谓的脉冲星术语可以让我们一睹过去的风采，并可以帮助我们绘制出这些双星数万年来的频率演化。测量这种长期频率演化可以让我们寻找由于各种环境影响而导致的真空双星演化的偏差。我们开发了费舍尔矩阵方法来计算以这种方式随时间跟踪频率的精确度，并演示了其在具有 30 年基线的现实近期脉冲星计时阵列上的使用。我们发现，通过环双星盘的二维流体动力学模拟预测的环境影响不太可能在我们考虑的圆形双星中检测到，但是，在某些情况下，如果忽略它们可能会导致有偏差的线性调频质量恢复。我们还演示了这种方法如何允许人们在纯引力波驱动的螺旋中测试广义相对论预测的轨道演化，并为幂律硬化模型描述的其他通用环境效应设定上限。
