Methods used in mechanistic interpretability have revealed human-aligned concepts inside language and image models. In this paper, we search for physics concepts inside a neural a generative collider physics model trained on real LHC data (ShellFlow). We discover features inside ShellFlow that represent resonances, such as the $Z$, $J/ψ$, and $Υ$. The network was never told which muons come from a resonance, it was only trained to generate the kinematics of the final-state particles. We use these features to alter aspects of the $Z$ boson: we change its mass between $\sim80$ and $105$ GeV, and removing such a feature from the neural network produces a broader $Z$ peak. In both cases the rest of the invariant mass spectrum remains mostly unchanged. The feature was built from the gradients of the neural network, and its selectivity was built using pattern recognition methods. A generative particle physics model trained on unlabeled data can be modified internally to change a physical quantity.

We establish a framework for classifying higher-derivative Ramond-Ramond (RR) couplings on Dp-branes, focusing on the complete scattering amplitude of a closed-string RR state, a gauge field, and two transverse scalars. The low-energy expansion reveals infinite massless poles and local interaction terms, surpassing standard Myers and Taylor expansions. We propose a higher-class deformation of the pullback using mixed Chern-Simons transgression operators to address these complexities. Our three conjectures are as follows: 1. For fixed field content and derivative order, effective couplings form a finite-dimensional space under stated equivalences. 2. A family of mixed RR, gauge, and scalar interactions exists at all orders, represented by a finite number of effective even forms. 3. The higher-derivative contact-term sector can be constructed from a finite set of basic tensor structures using polynomials in derivative symbols. We introduce a higher-class pullback operator \( P_{\infty} \) and mixed transgression operators \( \mathcal{T}_{\infty} \), emphasizing amplitude-derived pole residues and local contact terms as key physical data. This formalism distinguishes admissible tensor structures, presenting characteristic-form aspects as conjectures alongside their validity considerations.

The one-loop dilatation operator of a superconformal planar gauge theory can be written as a sum of local generators. The algebra these generators satisfy is a powerful organising principle for the spectrum. For SU(2)-like bosonic subsectors we show that this algebra can be read off directly from Feynman diagrams, which we identify as a natural graphical language for it. For N=4 super Yang-Mills this simply reproduces the Temperley-Lieb (TL) algebra of the XXX spin chain. After orbifolding and marginally deforming to N=2 superconformal quiver theories, the same recipe yields dynamical generalisations of the TL algebra. In these theories, the faces of the diagrams are coloured according to the quiver nodes, and separated by domain walls corresponding to bifundamental fields. There are two inequivalent SU(2)-like subsectors, which lead to two different TL algebras. One sector is built from the two bifundamental scalars and (in the simplest case) gives a dynamical TL algebra with a colour-dependent loop fugacity in its quadratic relation. The other sector is built from one bifundamental and one adjoint scalar, and (always) gives a novel algebra, in which the cubic TL relation becomes dynamical. In this latter case, we show that each generator of this new algebra is related to a standard XXZ TL generator by a colour-dependent twist. This twist is local and not an algebra homomorphism, and can be written as a dynamical Drinfeld twist. In both sectors, the TL structure provides a natural graphical approach to the spectral problem, as well as clarifying the structure of the irreducible representations of these chains. Finally, we give a preliminary description of the loop models associated to these algebras.

We construct twists of spinning particles in the classical BV formalism by giving constant backgrounds to the bosonic ghosts of local worldline supersymmetry, in analogy with Costello and Li's twist of supergravity, which gives a nonzero background to the ghost of local spacetime supersymmetry. We represent the superghosts on polynomial states and gauge selected worldline R symmetries, imposing the shifted R constraints absolutely and the unshifted ones relatively. On flat targets, the N=2 particle, its N=4 extension on a complex target and an N=8 particle with two fermion flavors give the de Rham, Dolbeault and polynomial linear BCOV complexes. The last two are the linearized field content of the open and closed B-model target-space theories. Pairings and interactions require target-space input beyond the reduced worldline differentials.

In this work, we present the relativistic kinetic theory of a gas of collisionless point particles with non-constant mass, which can also be understood as being subject to a scalar force. Our results hold in an arbitrary conformal frame, and we explicitly derive how they transform between frames. The mathematical construction relies on a tilted Sasaki metric, whose associated phase-space geometry is adapted to particles of variable mass, as well as on the one-particle distribution function, from which macroscopic observables are derived. Finally, we show that the one-particle distribution function can always be taken to be gauge- and frame-invariant, and the corresponding generalisation of the Liouville (or collisionless Boltzmann) equation is satisfied in each frame.

The modular properties of holomorphic Eisenstein series for $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})$ and its congruence subgroups do not carry over to their primitives. Instead, the transformation of iterated Eisenstein integrals under congruence subgroups of level $N$ introduces inhomogeneous terms comprising (among others) cyclotomic multiple zeta values of level $N$. We combine iterated Eisenstein integrals for the congruence subgroups of level $N=2$ of arbitrary iteration depth with their complex conjugates and (single-valued) level-2 cyclotomic multiple zeta values such that they define non-holomorphic modular forms. Our construction of these so-called equivariant iterated Eisenstein integrals for $Γ(2)$ and $Γ_0(2)$ proceeds at the level of generating series that take values in an infinite-dimensional (pro-unipotent) Lie group and closely follow Brown's analogous construction in the $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})$ case. The required insertions of cyclotomic multiple zeta values are controlled by zeta generators for level 2 that normalise the corresponding (pro-nilpotent) Lie algebra of the generating series and whose Lie-bracket relations we determine systematically. Upon restriction to Eisenstein series of modular weight~2, we recover single-valued multiple polylogarithms on the genus 0 modular curve $Γ(2)\backslash \mathbb{H}$.

In this paper, we have generalized Poisson's(1993) calculation for the case of gravitational radiation from a particle in a circular orbit around a black hole to the case of the wormhole. Considering the motion of a test particle around a Schwarzschild like wormhole and using Teukolsky formalism, we have obtained an analytical result of flux at infinity under the assumption of small velocities. Thus, our work provides one of the first attempts toward the study of the post-Newtonian waveforms involving wormholes (ECOs).

We investigate quantum gate teleportation as a primitive for satellite-enabled distributed quantum computing. In the considered architecture, a satellite distributes entangled photon pairs to two optical ground stations, which transfer the shared quantum resource to remote quantum processing units. This entanglement is then used to implement a nonlocal controlled-NOT gate between two circuit qubits located in different processors. We formulate the minimal four-qubit protocol, consisting of two network qubits that provide the entangled resource and two circuit qubits on which the distributed gate is performed. Atmospheric propagation effects in the gravitational field are modeled through an effective two-qubit depolarizing channel whose parameters depend on atmospheric and optical quantities, including the Fried parameter and receiver aperture. The performance of the protocol is quantified by the fidelity between the ideal CNOT output state and the state produced by noisy quantum gate teleportation. Our results show that the fidelity is governed by the quality of the distributed entanglement and depends jointly on atmospheric coherence, aperture size, and effective depolarization strength. These findings connect physical-layer modeling of satellite optical channels with gate-level performance in distributed quantum computing.

We develop a gauge-independent theory of magnetic reconnection beyond the standard special-relativistic framework of inertial systems in flat spacetime. Our definitions of reconnection and its rate are independent of reference frame and coordinate system, allowing physical effects of gravity and net linear acceleration to be distinguished from coordinate effects. We extend the mesoscale theory of Liu et al. [PRL 118, 085101 (2017)], in which the reconnection rate depends on physics both within the diffusion region and in an upstream ideal, force-free region. The theory identifies three non-inertial and curved-spacetime (NICS) effects, including gravitationally induced magnetic tension: gravitational distortion of the reconnection plane changes the curvature of magnetic field lines. Applying the theory to a simplified accretion plasma near )a compact object, we find that (1) whether gravity increases or decreases the reconnection rate depends on the system's orientation relative to the gravitational source, with the greatest increase occurring when the outflow jet points toward or away from the source; and (2) the magnitude of the gravitational effect scales with the dimensionless parameter $\barκ \equiv r_sΔy^2/r^3$, where $r$ is the Schwarzschild radial coordinate, $r_s$ is the source's Schwarzschild radius, and $Δy$ is the mesoscale length.

Energetic-particle irradiation can produce persistent charge separation within lunar regolith, but the internal fields predicted by subsurface-charging models depend on how particle interactions are translated into retained charge. This critical review examines the evidence and assumptions connecting microscopic charge formation to macroscopic charging. We distinguish energy deposition, net charge transfer across a specified boundary, and the spatial distribution of signed excess charge. We organize these distinctions using an average retained-charge response defined for specified material, irradiation, electrical-state, and evaluation conditions. Its use as a volumetric source requires consistent charge accounting, compatible incident-flux definitions, and an adequate separation between response formation and subsequent transport. Applying these criteria to existing models, emission studies, and silicate irradiation experiments yields three assessments. First, emission measurements constrain electron loss across the measured boundary but do not locate internally redistributed charge. Second, evidence of internal reabsorption does not by itself establish that the charge separation sustaining subsurface fields is reduced; an illustrative calculation shows that the same net charge supply can produce different peak fields depending on transfer depths. Third, laboratory electrical damage in silicate crystals does not determine breakdown rates in cryogenic, porous regolith, which additionally require constraints on local fields, failure properties, and discharge recurrence. Joint constraints on boundary charge transfer, spatial charge separation, and post-irradiation relaxation are central to evaluating lunar subsurface-charging predictions.

A proposed resolution of the black-hole information paradox must explain which information an outside observer can recover from escaping radiation\cite{Hawking,Page,Hayden,Almheiri}. Environmental memory can return correlations to the source without delivering them to that observer\cite{ChenQi,ChenMemory}. Here we establish a sharp recovery frontier at fixed mean radiation yield for single-excitation channels and realize its qubit limit with an autonomous quantum-memory emitter. Unequal transmissions balance retained coherence against information in failed collection events. A reference--radiation correlation test distinguishes this gain even when the mean collected energy is unchanged. Identical transmission channels instead give the same recovery at matched collection, regardless of their emission timescales. For a broader class of interacting sources, an ordering of collected and inaccessible coupling spectra forbids unassisted quantum transmission irrespective of internal memory. A separate coding example supports a positive rate where uniform transmission at matched actual yield has zero capacity. Together, these results identify what openness must change to assist recovery: the accessible transmission structure. They supply a physically constrained comparison of evaporation mechanisms, separating information delivered to radiation from coherence retained elsewhere or an increase in the collected output.

TianQin, a space-borne gravitational wave observatory, is designed to detect gravitational-wave signals via laser interferometry. As the inhomogeneous space plasma surrounds the inter-satellite laser links, the propagation of the laser in space plasma can introduce pointing error noise. In this work, we quantitatively assess the effect of plasma on the pointing accuracy of TQ's inter-satellite laser links. We employ a magnetohydrodynamic model to simulate the plasma distribution around the TQ orbit for different orbital orientations. Using the fourth-order Runge-Kutta method to conduct the ray-tracing calculations over a TQ orbital cycle under a solar wind dynamic pressure close to the 23-year average, we find that the plasma-induced laser deflection angle and pointing eccentricity are on the order of $10^{-20} \ \mathrm{rad}$ and picometers, respectively. These values are significantly lower than TQ's requirements. Our results demonstrate that the impact of space plasma on TQ's pointing error is negligible in moderate space weather conditions, indicating a positive outlook for the TQ mission.

We investigate how the effective nucleon mass and symmetry energy affect pasta phase properties and the unified equation of state (EOS) of neutron stars. The nonuniform pasta structure is described within the self-consistent Thomas-Fermi approximation, while nucleonic interactions are treated in the relativistic mean-field framework with several typical parameter sets adopted. Our results indicate that the symmetry energy and its slope are crucial in determining the onset of pasta phases and their geometric configurations. In the high-density region where matter becomes uniform, the effective nucleon mass plays a dominant role. A larger effective nucleon mass tends to soften the EOS, which yields a smaller radius for a canonical 1.4 M$_{⊙}$ neutron star and reduces the corresponding tidal deformability.

We present the complete FAST burst-cluster catalog of FRB 20240114A, covering its 495-day active episode from January 28, 2024 to June 5, 2025. The catalog contains 13261 burst clusters, 19037 bursts, and 29026 sub-bursts detected in 99 observing sessions. The data reveal distinct organization across temporal scales. Among drifting burst clusters, downward-drifting clusters outnumber upward-drifting ones by approximately $9.4:1$. Between separate burst clusters, previously reported frequency changes show no preferred direction, while our Weibull analysis finds their arrival times broadly consistent with Poisson statistics, suggesting that burst clusters may represent approximately independent emission episodes within observing sessions. The Parkes/Murriyang observations revealed a long-term modulation of the characteristic emission frequency, and the extended FAST measurements show a consistent evolution. Burst energetics increase with cluster multiplicity and activity level, although significant scatter remains. The manually refined dispersion measure increases at $1.29\pm0.11\,\mathrm{pc\,cm^{-3}\,yr^{-1}}$, enlarging the still limited but growing sample of repeaters with increasing DM. Overall, these results summarize the spectral--temporal evolution of FRB 20240114A and present a burst-cluster catalog linked to the existing BurstID measurements, providing a basis for future studies of repeating FRBs.

Pulsar-timing accelerations point to a localized mass concentration near the Sun, consistent with a dark matter sub-halo. We work out what such an object implies for the nature of dark matter, organizing the implications by their observable effect: suppression or enhancement of structure formation, internal structure and survival after formation, and emission or scattering by the constituents. We discuss ultralight wave dark matter, thermal and nonthermal warm dark matter, dark acoustic oscillations and dark matter-baryon scattering, elastic and inelastic self-interactions, invisible decays with recoil, primordial black holes, indirect detection, and a possible connection to the LUX-ZEPLIN high-energy recoil candidate. We then ask what new data would discriminate among these models. The existing accelerations are measured outside the tidal radius, where they are blind to the interior mass distribution, to the object's velocity, and to the sub-halo population. We identify four routes that lift these degeneracies: accelerations measured inside the halo, which separate point-mass, cuspy, and cored profiles; lensing and accretion, which test whether the mass is compact; the morphology of annihilation or decay emission, which probes the inner profile; and a search for a comoving stellar population, which tests whether the object is dark and measures its velocity. We also contrast this program with pulsar-timing-array searches for transiting dark matter substructure, which probe vastly lighter objects through a different observable.

Though fast radio bursts (FRBs) are widely advocated as a dark energy probe, no valid $Ω_m-w$ constraint has been obtained exclusively from FRB data to date. Benefiting from a rapidly growing localized sample and a novel statistical framework for modeling the dispersion measure (DM), we report the first simultaneous inference of the matter density $Ω_m$ and dark energy equation of state parameter $w$ from FRBs alone, without external observational priors on these two parameters. We combine a multi-parameter emulator for the nonlinear matter power spectrum with a physically motivated electron prescription to construct a cosmology-dependent cosmic-DM distribution. Within this unified analysis framework, the mean and variance of DM respond self-consistently to the same underlying cosmology, directly connecting the FRB likelihood to both cosmic expansion and structure growth. Analyzing 125 localized FRBs at $0.105\leq z\leq2.148$, we obtain $Ω_m = 0.33^{+0.10}_{-0.11}$ and $w = -0.80^{+0.28}_{-0.38}$ under the flat $w$CDM model. We find a new positive $Ω_m-w$ degeneracy in the FRB posterior, opposite to the strong anticorrelation derived from Type Ia supernovae, offering a complementary test of dark energy properties and highlighting their potential in future precision cosmological studies.

Globular clusters (GCs) provide dense stellar environments for investigating the high-energy emission of millisecond pulsars (MSPs). Using $\sim$17.3~yr of \textit{Fermi}-LAT Pass~8 data in the 0.1-500.0~GeV range, we study four MSPs that dominate, or contribute substantially to, the GeV $γ$-ray emission of their host GCs. Significant off-pulse $γ$-ray emission is detected from PSR~J1823$-$3021A, J1824$-$2452A, and J1835$-$3259B. For these sources, the cutoff energy, $E_{\rm cut}$, varies systematically with rotational phase and is positively correlated with the phase-resolved energy flux. Using the phase-resolved isotropic $γ$-ray luminosity ($L_γ$), we find $L_γ\propto E_{\rm cut}^α$ with $α=0.74^{+0.16}_{-0.14}$. This slope is substantially shallower than that reported for field MSPs, $α=2.31^{+0.22}_{-0.25}$, and the $α=16/7\simeq2.29$ scaling expected for curvature radiation in the equatorial current sheet framework. The origin of the shallow relation remains uncertain, potentially reflecting cluster-associated $γ$-ray emission, differences in emission geometry and beaming, or limited photon statistics. Our results demonstrate that GC MSPs provide valuable targets for testing phase-resolved high-energy emission physics in dense stellar environments.

For FRB 20240209A, we identify a new candidate activity period of 170.4 $\pm$ 4.7 days, with a trials-corrected statistical significance of 5.3$σ$. Our larger burst sample shows no evidence of the 128.5-day activity period previously reported for this source by A. Pal (2025). We also report candidate activity periods of 28.46 $\pm$ 0.08 days, 86.7 $\pm$ 0.5 days, and 36.4 $\pm$ 0.5 days for FRB 20190208A, FRB 20200926A, and FRB 20220529A, with trials-corrected statistical significances of 3.1$σ$, 4.0$σ$, and 3.5$σ$, respectively. All statistical significances are evaluated against a homogeneous (constant-rate) Poisson null hypothesis, and we caution that they may decrease under null models that incorporate burst clustering. Collectively, our results constitute the most comprehensive search for periodic activity in the repeating FRB population to date.
